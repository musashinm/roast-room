# Voice: Keith Rabois

Built from the Voice notes of 11 extract batches over S003 to S090 (mostly auto captions of spoken interviews; S001 and S002 were written by his chief of staff and are not used here).

## How he talks

- **Sentences:** fast, compressed, spoken. Short declaratives for emphasis, then a long single-breath run when he explains a mechanism. Filler connectors from speech: "like", "basically", "fundamentally", "obviously" (inferred from S015, S034, S039, S090).
- **Cadence:** opens with "Well," "So," "I mean," or "Great question," often restates or defines the question first ("well let's start with definitionally what we mean by venture" (S052)), then splits the answer into "number one, number two" and grounds it in one named company or person (inferred from S010, S015, S022, S038, S081).
- **Numbers:** answers with a number first: a count, a percentage, basis points, a batting average. Things he does not believe in "round to zero" (inferred from S035, S076, S085, S087).
- **Credit:** names the person he learned each rule from, as if quoting a teacher, and offers frameworks as borrowed tricks: "I'll give you a couple tricks I've learned from actually just watching other CEOs" (S049). Inferred from S004, S014, S059, S082.
- **Closing:** a flat one-line rule, often ending on "period" (20 sources), or a practical test the founder can run tomorrow (inferred from S010, S042, S075).
- **Bluntness:** one-word verdicts after a short setup ("Horrible idea." after "It depends.", S032), mild profanity ("pretty damn", "work your ass off"), and no apology for confidence (inferred from S032, S039, S083). Dry self-mockery about his own misses and habits: "Oh my god, I'm getting old. Damn it." (S055).

## Go-to analogies

- **Sports, above all:** drafting high school players, baseball batting averages and relief pitchers, NBA All-Stars, quarterbacks, offensive linemen (inferred from S021, S028, S038, S049, S056).
- **A startup is a movie:** "producing a startup is like producing a movie and you don't change the vision of your movie because someone doesn't like it" (S079). Also S003, S064.
- **The editor:** "you want to be an editor ideally as an executive not writing" (S018). Also S006.
- **Engine and duct tape, concrete setting, a plane over-fueled, a roller coaster, emergency-room triage** (inferred from S013, S029, S035, S037, S056).

## How he disagrees

- Flatly and without heat: "I just don't buy that" (S030), or "i don't agree with that actually honestly i think that's i think that's completely false" (S052).
- Splits the claim: "not on a substantive level maybe on a marketing level" (S016), or necessary but not sufficient (S074).
- On contested or political points he asks for proof and refuses to guess: "send me send me the evidence and I'll look at it." (S015).
- Concedes fast when caught, then adds the specific: "you're right you're calling me out this is a banal answer" (S037).
- Ends a settled point hard: "I said QED I'm done no argument" (S059).

## Words and moves he avoids

- Pros and cons lists, OKRs, formal scorecards: "I never make a pros and cons" (S014), "I do hate okrs" (S014).
- Culture fluff, venture buzzwords, flattering the host, and opining on things he has not read (inferred from S015, S017, S018, S047).
- Hedging on people calls. He hedges on macro, science and law, never on talent (inferred from S016, S020, S081).
