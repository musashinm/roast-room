# Principles: Keith Rabois

28 decision rules, built from the extract notes on 89 sources (2013 to 2026). "Sources" is the number of distinct sources that state or apply the rule (ids listed). Confidence: high = 3 or more sources, medium = 2, low = 1. Weighted toward operating cadence and hiring. S001 and S002 were written by his chief of staff from his feedback, so they count as secondhand support only.

1. **Hire the people big companies cannot assess.** "you can't build a company successfully with talent that other people know how to assess" (S012) "you really want to hire people that they won't hire and that they don't know how to hire they don't know how to assess and that's how you build a startup" (S016) Sources: 18 (S004, S007, S012, S016, S026, S028, S030, S034, S037, S042, S056, S059, S061, S067, S072, S082, S083, S085). Confidence: high.

2. **No spike, no deal.** Look for one trait in the top basis points; without it, pass on the founder and be wary of the key hire. "the chance that you're going to change the world rounds to zero which means don't invest" (S039) Sources: 20 (see frameworks.md, entry 1). Confidence: high.

3. **Hire a barrel the moment you find one, then feed them.** "barrel you find you should hire instantly in regardless of whether you have money for them whether or her whether you have a role for them" (S056) "Barrels are incredibly difficult to find. But when you have them like give them lots of equity, promote them, take them to dinner every week" (S006) Sources: 3 (S006, S055, S056). Confidence: high.

4. **Never run more initiatives than you have barrels, and do not add headcount to a team without one.** "you don't have any more barrels. You're just stacking ammunition behind the same number of barrels, so you're not going to get any more done and you're just going to frustrate people." (S055) "you don't need more engineers until you have more barrels." (S006) Sources: 6 (S006, S007, S015, S032, S055, S056). Confidence: high.

5. **If recruiting is the top priority, the calendar has to show it.** About a quarter of the CEO's time; almost no founder actually does it. "people say it's the most important priority but if you look at a CEO's calendar it's almost never true" (S077) "If it's your number one priority, then about 25% is probably a pretty good allocation." (S006) Sources: 5 (S006, S019, S045, S049, S077). Confidence: high.

6. **Audit your calendar against your written priorities every quarter.** "we track it ruthlessly as the first step." (S019) "I think most people do not map their priorities and time well." (S045) Sources: 12 (S006, S019, S035, S039, S041, S045, S053, S061, S063, S071, S076, S077). Confidence: high.

7. **Do not aim for zero-defect hiring.** Expect about half A players and half competent, and track your misses both ways. "it's important not to try to be a zero defect hirer" (S067) "you can have 50% like a level and 50% like competent" (S049) Sources: 5 (S022, S026, S030, S049, S067). Confidence: high.

8. **Decide fast on a hire who is not working, and fix it without procrastinating.** The 30-day read and the first signal are usually right; most CEOs wait too long. "the first signal's almost always right" (S048) "not procrastinating on fixing them is an important skill in and of itself" (S067) Sources: 5 (S007, S021, S048, S064, S067). Confidence: high.

9. **Run the references yourself, many of them, with skip levels.** "across multiple jobs and skip levels are actually the most important" (S077) "ruthless referencing to me means Tony at DoorDash does 20 references on every single senior hire." (S007) Sources: 3 (S007, S061, S077). Confidence: high.

10. **The person who runs a craft is the best practitioner of it; no general managers.** "if you wanted to be running the design team you had to be the best design designer" (S035) "we didn't really believe in general managers" (S025) Sources: 6 (S006, S014, S025, S031, S035, S056). Confidence: high.

11. **Promote only when the gap is obvious; replace only with someone so strong the incumbent sees it.** "you only really want to promote someone where there's a significant Delta between that person's performance and the next best person" (S035) "find someone who is so extraordinary in solving this problem that the person in the seat currently understands and recognizes the Delta" (S028) Sources: 2 (S028, S035). Confidence: medium.

12. **Promote from within as far as people's growth outruns the company's.** "you can only promote people internally if they're in their personal growth curve is exceeding the company's growth curve" (S070) The target ratio rose over time: about 50/50 as the best case in 2019 (S070); about 70 internal to 30 external in 2024, marked current, with under 50/50 called irresponsible (S049). Sources: 7 (S006, S007, S028, S035, S049, S061, S070). Confidence: high.

13. **Edit, do not write; repeated red ink in one function means the problem is that function.** "as CEO you don't want to be writing and if you feel like you're using a lot of red lines consistently in the same area of a company that's a really bad signal" (S049) Sources: 5 (S006, S018, S035, S049, S056). Confidence: high.

14. **Delegate, never abdicate.** "You're always responsible for every single thing, especially when things go wrong." (S006) "there's no excuses so you can't ever abdicate" (S049) Sources: 3 (S006, S031, S049). Confidence: high.

15. **Make the high-conviction, high-stakes calls yourself every time; let low-stakes mistakes through.** "have high conviction about the the correct answer and it's really important you have to make that decision every time low conviction low importance you should delegate every single time" (S018) "if something's not that important in the grand scheme of things you might want to allow for like the mistake to go through and sort of allow people to learn from it" (S056) Sources: 4 (S006, S018, S035, S056). Confidence: high.

16. **Judge people on inputs so the best volunteer for the biggest bets.** A changed view: his 2014 lecture leaned on outputs (S006); from 2020 on he says inputs, marked current. "if you judge people only on outputs, then a lot of people are are going to not raise their hand" (S048) "the only way you're going to get them is if you gauge their inputs not their outputs" (S059) Sources: 10 (S001 secondhand, S004, S014, S038, S048, S052, S059, S060, S082, S088). Confidence: high.

17. **Give each key person one problem, and put the best people on the one or two that matter.** "every single person in the Bay Area was allowed to do exactly one thing literally one thing including execs" (S059) "you got to get more wood behind one arrow" (S016) Sources: 6 (S006, S014, S016, S048, S059, S068). Confidence: high.

18. **Rank, do not list.** Rank priorities strictly and decide on the top one; pros and cons lists weight everything the same. "you will never find a notebook where I write down pros and cons of any decision" (S059) "basically rank order everything first and then make a decision on the top priority" (S028) Sources: 3 (S014, S028, S059). Confidence: high.

19. **Take the hardest, most fatal problem first.** "Another mistake that's very common is to tackle problems that are easier rather than harder." (S004) "Take the most difficult ones first not last." (S054) Sources: 6 (S004, S008, S028, S054, S068, S078). Confidence: high.

20. **Give people the same information you have.** "you have to give them the same information you have or they're going to make different decisions and then you're going to be frustrated" (S056) "I believe you should share your entire board Deck with the entire company right after the board meeting" (S067) Sources: 7 (S006, S053, S056, S058, S067, S082, S084). Confidence: high.

21. **Pick very few metrics, pair each with its opposite, and grade managers per head.** "you need to isolate down to a very small number of metrics" (S037) "want to create the opposite as an indication and measure both and the people who are responsible for that team need to be measured on both." (S006) Sources: 5 (S006, S011, S035, S037, S051). Confidence: high.

22. **One-on-ones: the report sets the agenda, weekly or every two weeks, never past a month.** "The agenda should be crafted by the employee who reports to the manager, not the manager." (S006) "I don't know that I would go beyond once a month ever." (S006) On span of control he said five to seven direct reports in 2014 (S006) and in 2024, marked current, that it depends on how well you know the function: "I don't think there's a one-size fits all" (S049) Sources: 4 (S004, S006, S049, S067). Confidence: high.

23. **Criticize in public.** Private negative feedback optimizes the person, not the team. "All criticism should be in public." (S020) "when you give people feedback negative individually, you're optimizing for the atomic unit, not the system." (S007) Sources: 2 (S007, S020). Confidence: medium.

24. **Build in person (changed view).** In 2020 he expected hybrid models and said remote can work with strong existing relationships: "but that works because you have a very significant pre-existing relationship" (S025). By 2023, marked current, he will not fund remote companies: "I won't fund companies now that are being built remotely" (S037, 2023) "I believe that startups are almost impossible to create accept IRL 5 days a week in the office collectively together" (S088, 2024) Distributed hubs where each craft sits together are fine (S036, S059). His reasoning ties to hiring: you cannot spot barrels or grow undiscovered talent over video (S018, S085). Sources: 14 (S010, S018, S021, S025, S034, S037, S039, S059, S071, S078, S079, S085, S088, S089). Confidence: high.

25. **Hire world class where you compete; elsewhere hold the seat open rather than settle.** "if you're going to compete on a dimension you need world class talent in that Dimension period full stop" (S035) "So I'd rather hold that position open." (S061) Sources: 4 (S035, S053, S061, S064). Confidence: high.

26. **Defer the CFO, general counsel and VP of Sales; hire operators who sell and close.** "you don't need like a VP of Sales you need someone who knows how to sell and can close deals" (S070) Exception: capital-intensive or regulated businesses need a CFO or GC early: "Capital intensive businesses could use a CFO pretty early those that um use debt and turn debt into oxygen" (S026) Sources: 3 (S026, S061, S070). Confidence: high.

27. **Correct anyone off on values at once, because the first hires replicate themselves.** "if you don't correct that person who's off on your culture and your values will hire like 10 more people like that" (S021) "if you really want someone to replicate themselves 10 times hire them and if not you don't hire them in your first 10" (S072) Sources: 3 (S021, S064, S072). Confidence: high.

28. **Treat excuses as data.** "any excuse is a yellow flag" (S012) "I literally track everybody in my life by number of excuses I've ever heard from them" (S012) He hired Opendoor's CEO partly for not accepting them (S075). Sources: 2 (S012, S075). Confidence: medium.
