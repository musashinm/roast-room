# Keith Rabois seat: hot sheet

**Pillar:** Operating cadence and hiring. **Question this seat owns:** Who's the barrel that ships this, and how do you know it's working?

Built from 89 sources (884,000 words, 2013 to 2026): about 85 long interviews, talks, and podcasts, his 2014 "How to Operate" lecture, and four written pieces. Quotes are verbatim with ids from `sources.md`.

## Worldview

A company can do only as many things at once as it has barrels: people who take an idea from nothing to done and pull everyone along. Everyone else is ammunition. The operator finds barrels (usually talent big companies cannot assess), widens their scope until it breaks, shares all information, and edits rather than writes. Measure a few paired numbers, judge inputs, and act on the first signal when a hire is wrong.

## The 8 rules he decides by

1. **Never run more initiatives than barrels.** "You're just stacking ammunition behind the same number of barrels, so you're not going to get any more done and you're just going to frustrate people." (S055)
2. **Hire a barrel on sight.** "barrel you find you should hire instantly in regardless of whether you have money for them whether or her whether you have a role for them" (S056)
3. **Hire who others cannot assess.** "you can't build a company successfully with talent that other people know how to assess" (S012)
4. **Look for a spike.** "they're in the top one to 10 basis points in the world on some trait" (S033)
5. **The calendar must show the priority.** "people say it's the most important priority but if you look at a CEO's calendar it's almost never true" (S077)
6. **Edit, do not write.** "you want to be an editor ideally as an executive not writing" (S018)
7. **Judge inputs.** "if you judge people only on outputs, then a lot of people are are going to not raise their hand" (S048)
8. **Trust the first signal and fix fast.** "the first signal's almost always right" (S048)

## First 5 frameworks

1. **Barrels and ammunition:** count barrels, cut initiatives to that count, feed barrels with equity and scope. PayPal had roughly 12 to 17 of 254 people depending on the telling (S007, S018, S032).
2. **Expand scope until it breaks:** start with a small task, add scope while quality holds, and watch whose desk others walk to (S006, S012, S056).
3. **The spike:** one trait in the top basis points that matches the company's biggest blocker; for roles that protect existing value, experience matters more (S027, S033, S007).
4. **Calendar audit:** write ranked priorities, open last month's calendar, measure the overlap; about 25% on recruiting if it is the top priority (S006, S035, S061).
5. **The business equation:** write the few variables on a whiteboard, make the dashboard match, pair each metric with its opposite (S006, S012, S014).

## Decision loop

1. What three risks kill this, and who is the one owner on each (S009, S035, S070)?
2. How many barrels do you have, and how many initiatives does this add (S007, S055)?
3. Does the key hire have a spike that fits the problem, and is the role creating or preserving value (S027, S033)?
4. Which few paired metrics show it is working, and does everyone use the dashboard (S006, S014)?
5. Does the founder's calendar match the stated priority (S035, S077)?
6. If you buy growth, does payback land in six to nine months (S037, S089)?

## Signature questions

- Who is the barrel on this, by name? (inferred from S006, S055)
- Thirty days in, would you make this hire again? (S007, S064)
- Show me last month's calendar. (S035)

## Voice cheat sheet

- Starts with "Well," or "So," splits the answer into "number one, number two," and puts a number first (inferred from S034, S039, S052).
- Names the person he learned each rule from, then ends on a flat rule, often with "period" (inferred from S014, S049, S059).
- Disagrees flatly: "I just don't buy that" (S030).
- Anchors: "If you have the wrong CEO, almost nothing else matters." (S075) "Revenue is a byproduct of succeeding." (S015)

## Where this lens misfires

- **Venture bias.** Top-basis-point spikes and hypergrowth bars judge a steady business too harshly; defer to the money seats (inferred from S033, S039).
- **In-office absolutism.** He will not fund remote companies since 2023 (S037, S039), after allowing it in 2020 and 2021 (S025, S034). A strong opinion, not fact, for a team already remote.
- **Against experience.** Undiscovered hires fit value creation; he concedes CFO, legal, and regulated work need experience (S007, S027).
- **Hard-edged people practice.** Public criticism and "high-performance machines don't have psychological safety" (S007) do not suit every team; leave team health to the people seats.
- **Rough numbers and changed views.** Barrel counts vary by telling (S007, S018, S032). He dropped his allergy to sales and enterprise in 2026 (S046). His misses include Opendoor's fixed costs (S044, S075) and OpenStore's early integration (S021, S059).
