# Books: Keith Rabois

He has published no book of his own; he mentions a manuscript once without a title or argument (S043). Built from the extract notes on 89 sources. His own written and taught work comes first, then the books he keeps assigning, because they are where his operating frameworks come from. Summaries are in my own words; no book text was fetched.

## His own work

### How to Operate (Stanford CS183B / YC "How to Start a Startup", Lecture 14, 2014)
- **Core thesis:** A leader's job is editing, not writing, and the company's output is capped by its barrels.
- **Key frameworks:** barrels and ammunition; expand scope until it breaks; the editor metaphor; conviction by consequence for delegation; task-relevant maturity; employee slope versus company slope; the dashboard and paired metrics; stages of transparency; the calendar audit; triage of colds versus fatal problems.
- **Taught in sources:** the lecture itself (S006). He refers back to it, or hosts summarize it, in S014, S018, S025, S035, S049, S055, S059. He sometimes dates it 2013 (S018, S059); the video is the 2014 course.

### Chapters in High Growth Handbook (Elad Gil, 2018)
- **Core thesis:** Companies should go public far earlier than Silicon Valley advises, once revenue is around $50 million and predictable a few quarters out. He also says he wrote the hiring chapter (S053).
- **Key frameworks:** an IPO threshold built on predictability, not size; listing for transparency, accountability, liquidity and access to debt and acquisitions.
- **Taught in sources:** S010, S027, S053, S090; the threshold itself in S010, S027, S053, S060, S074, S081.

### The pinned tweet formula (2017)
- **Core thesis:** Good companies vertically integrate a fragmented industry with low customer satisfaction and replace the incumbents instead of selling to them.
- **Taught in sources:** S022, S026, S030, S076, S084.

## Books he teaches from

### High Output Management (Andy Grove, 1983)
- **Core thesis:** A manager's output is the output of the organization they run, so the job is high-leverage activity.
- **Key frameworks he uses:** one-on-ones set by the report; task-relevant maturity; high-leverage activity; the capability versus motivation test (could they do it if their life depended on it); simplification. He reread it every year for his first 15 to 18 years in tech (S014, S055), then moved from its output focus to inputs.
- **Taught in sources:** S004, S006, S014, S018, S021, S031, S035, S037, S038, S049, S055, S059, S060, S067.

### The Score Takes Care of Itself (Bill Walsh, 2009)
- **Core thesis:** Results are a byproduct of precise standards in every role, down to details nobody sees.
- **Key frameworks he uses:** standards of excellence for every position; measure inputs and the score follows; no sloppiness anywhere, because it spreads; match the strategy to the talent you have. He calls it the fundamental book on building a company (S009).
- **Taught in sources:** S004, S006, S009, S012, S018, S037, S038, S055, S056, S060, S082.

### The Upside of Stress (Kelly McGonigal, 2015)
- **Core thesis:** Stress treated as a challenge, not a threat, improves health and performance.
- **Key frameworks he uses:** convert nerves into a challenge; if you are not nervous you are not stretching. He calls it his favorite book and gives it to anyone who asks for one (S020, S037, S055). Off the seat's core, but it shapes how he reads pressure on teams.
- **Taught in sources:** S007, S012, S014, S018, S020, S024, S025, S035, S037, S038, S055, S060, S081, S083, S085.

### Zero to One (Peter Thiel, 2014)
- **Core thesis:** Great companies are built on secrets, beliefs most people do not share, and aim for monopoly.
- **Key frameworks he uses:** every successful company is a cult built around a secret; a secret and an anomaly are close cousins (S054).
- **Taught in sources:** S012, S018, S021, S025, S046, S054, S082, S084.

### The Winner Within (Pat Riley, 1993)
- **Core thesis:** Team success depends on each person's commitment and on knowing your role.
- **Key frameworks he uses:** be the only one who does what you do, not the best at it, a line the book credits to Jerry Garcia. He read it in high school.
- **Taught in sources:** S010, S024, S026, S027, S030, S038, S081.

### Why We Sleep (Matthew Walker, 2017)
- **Core thesis:** Sleep is the base of health and cognitive performance.
- **Key frameworks he uses:** build the day backward from about eight hours of sleep. Health, so kept thin here.
- **Taught in sources:** S008, S026, S029, S030, S038, S041, S081, S082, S084, S085.

### 7 Powers (Hamilton Helmer, 2016)
- **Core thesis:** Durable strategy comes from a small set of structural powers.
- **Key frameworks he uses:** he has teams read it so they share one definition of strategy.
- **Taught in sources:** S026, S030.

### Who (Geoff Smart and Randy Street, 2008)
- **Core thesis:** Hiring is the biggest management problem and needs a structured method.
- **Key frameworks he uses:** none in detail; he calls it the best hiring book he knows, pretty good but not awesome (S020).
- **Taught in sources:** S020.
