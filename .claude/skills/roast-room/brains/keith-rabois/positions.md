# Positions: Keith Rabois

Built from the eleven extraction batches over 89 sources (S001 to S090). 40 topics, weighted to operating cadence and hiring. Each stance has a verbatim quote, source id, and date. Dates are the real year where the notes give it (S006 is 2014; S008 and S009 are 2019; S022 is about 2020; S071 is 2021); otherwise the upload date in `sources.md`. S001 and S002 were written by his chief of staff from his feedback, so they count as secondhand. Where he changed his mind, both views sit side by side and the newer one is marked.

## Who ships it: barrels and ammunition
- **Stance:** A company can only run as many important initiatives at once as it has barrels: people who take an idea from conception to the finish line and bring everyone with them. Everyone else, even great people, is ammunition. Hiring more ammunition behind the same barrels adds burn and frustration, not output. Hire a barrel on sight.
- **Evidence:**
  - 2013: "you can add all the ammunition you want but if you only have five Barrels in your company you can literally only do five things simultaneously" (S056)
  - 2013: "barrel you find you should hire instantly in regardless of whether you have money for them whether or her whether you have a role for them" (S056)
  - 2014: "most people, most great people even are actually ammunition. But what you need in your company are barrels." (S006) For engineering: "you don't need more engineers until you have more barrels." (S006)
  - 2024: "of the 254 people that worked at PayPal like headquarters, let's say, um maybe somewhere between 13 and 16 were barrels" (S032) and "two, three billion dollar companies, they might have two to four barrels. That's it." (S032)
  - 2025: "You're just stacking ammunition behind the same number of barrels, so you're not going to get any more done and you're just going to frustrate people." (S055)
  - 2026: "If you have three and you try to do 10 things, you may break everything." (S015) and "the number of people that can independently drive an initiative from beginning from inception to success is very limited" (S007)
- **Sources:** 13 (S006, S007, S012, S013, S015, S018, S020, S027, S032, S055, S056, S082, plus S065 secondhand).
- **Read:** his most used idea and unchanged from 2013 to 2026. The first question he asks of any plan is how many barrels it needs and whether you have them.

## Spotting a barrel
- **Stance:** Give a new person a tiny task, then keep widening the scope until it breaks, and watch whose desk people from outside their reporting line walk to. You have to see them inside your own culture.
- **Evidence:**
  - 2014: "expand the scope of their responsibilities until it breaks. And it will break." (S006) and "if you see that consistently, those are your barrels. Just promote them, give them more opportunity as fast as you can." (S006)
  - 2021: "you constantly expand the scope of responsibilities and then you measure the quality of performance" (S082)
  - 2024: "the signals you usually see about potential barrels are almost impossible to identify on a zoom" (S018)
  - 2026: the barrel escalates early on their own: "I will going to come back to you proactively with the issues I'm confronting, what I've already tried, the diagnosis of the root causes, and ask for your help with sufficient time for you to intervene" (S007)
- **Sources:** 7 (S006, S007, S018, S026, S032, S056, S082).

## Undiscovered talent over proven talent
- **Stance:** A startup cannot outbid big companies for proven people, so hire the young, odd, and unproven whom standard processes mis-score, and grow them. He learned it from Peter Thiel in his first week at PayPal.
- **Evidence:**
  - 2021: "you want to hire as many inexperienced people as possible that have high potential high upside and that other larger companies don't know how to evaluate correctly" (S034)
  - 2021: "find people that were too odd too weird too immature for large companies to digest" (S082)
  - 2023: "you absolutely have to hire undiscovered Talent we can't afford to hire proven Talent at the end of the day" (S059)
  - 2026: "it's not because you need young people, it's that younger people have by definition less data." (S007) And on pay: "you probably have 1/10 the salary cap of the people you're competing with." (S007)
- **Sources:** 11 (S007, S008, S012, S022, S030, S034, S038, S040, S048, S059, S082).
- **Read:** this is the root of his remote-work, promotion, and in-person views. Undiscovered talent only works if they can learn by watching.

## Experience and domain expertise
- **Stance:** Against domain experts for building something new; for experience in roles that protect value (CFO, general counsel), and as a mix in enterprise.
- **Evidence:**
  - 2019: "typically I don't like domain expertise" (S009)
  - 2022: "On enterprise there are benefits of experience and maybe you mix the team together." (S061)
  - 2023: "I don't fund people with domain expertise" (S014)
  - 2024: "if you're trying to do something that nobody else on the planet has ever done expertise is usually not your friend" (S035)
  - 2026: "If you're hiring for value preservation, typically some experience is useful. On the value creation side, it's probably not." (S007)
- **Sources:** 7 (S007, S009, S014, S035, S044, S061, S067).
- **Read:** consistent. The 2024 to 2026 versions add the value creation versus value preservation split, which is the usable rule.

## Interviews, references, and the 30-day check
- **Stance:** Hiring is a muscle learned by trial and error. Read someone's writing, run many references across jobs including skip levels, and trust the first signal. Ask yourself 30 days in whether you would make the hire again.
- **Evidence:**
  - About 2020 or earlier: "I could see how his brain worked so was much better than like a 20-minute interview" (S026)
  - 2024: "across multiple jobs and skip levels are actually the most important" (S077)
  - 2024: "would you make this higher again" (S064)
  - 2025: "the first signal's almost always right" (S048)
  - 2026: "ruthless referencing to me means Tony at DoorDash does 20 references on every single senior hire." (S007) and "if you ask yourself 30 days after any hire, would you make the same decision?" (S007)
  - 2026: "the way I've had to learn this is like many other skills in life, trial and error. It's like a muscle." (S020)
- **Sources:** 8 (S007, S020, S026, S048, S061, S064, S077, S079).
- **Read:** he admits his own early hiring was "Probably 50/50, you know, some good people, some mediocre people." (S007)

## Internal promotion versus outside executives
- **Stance:** Grow most leaders from inside and add a selective minority from outside. The target ratio has risen over time.
- **Over time:**
  - 2019: about half: "up at best with a ratio of 50/50 of 50% internal promotions that's very healthy" (S070)
  - 2022: "you might be able to get to about 70% internal promotions." (S061) He pushed 70 at a Square board and was cut to 50 (S061).
  - 2024: "if you're below 50/50 then you're irresponsible in a different way" (S049); he adds that almost no outside executive thrived at PayPal (S049).
  - 2026 (newer): "We're just going to promote from within." (S007) as his advice on senior hiring, with named exceptions.
- **Sources:** 7 (S007, S028, S035, S049, S061, S070, S081).
- **Read:** moved from "50/50 is healthy" to "promote from within" as the default. When you do hire outside early, he prefers operators over titles: "typically you're better off at that stage hiring operating executives" (S070).

## Promoting
- **Stance:** Promote the best practitioner of a craft to run that craft. No general managers. Promote only when the gap to the next person is obvious, and only people whose learning slope beats the company's growth.
- **Evidence:**
  - 2014: "So always track like the individual slope of an employee and the company's growth rate." (S006)
  - 2019: "you can only promote people internally if they're in their personal growth curve is exceeding the company's growth curve" (S070)
  - 2022: "you never want General management ability you want to promote People based upon their discipline and their craft" (S031)
  - 2023: "believed in promoting the single best person designer engineer to run whatever the craft is" (S014)
  - 2024: "you only really want to promote someone where there's a significant Delta between that person's performance and the next best person" (S035)
- **Sources:** 9 (S006, S014, S025, S028, S031, S035, S056, S070, S082).

## Firing and replacing people
- **Stance:** Decide fast. You rarely fix a struggling person in the role they are in: move them to a better-fit boss, shrink their scope to one clear goal, or replace them. A leader who is not running six months ahead of the business gets replaced.
- **Evidence:**
  - 2024: "one bad hire just one can really set back and organization and undermine the momentum pretty severely" (S049)
  - 2024: the six-months-ahead test: "not every lever can be tuned in a week most can't not every lever can be even tuned in a month" (S049)
  - 2023: edit the team every year for that year's problems: "make sure that you have the right person to tackle the three challenges in front of you that year" (S028)
  - 2025: "I don't think you can fix the problem in the role the person's in." (S048)
  - 2023: excuses are the tell: "any excuse is a yellow flag" (S012)
- **Founders:** at Founders Fund he would not replace founders: "we you know ideologically philosophically and effectively legally just don't do that" (S052, 2022). As a board member the one real decision is whether to fire the CEO (S074, 2020).
- **Sources:** 7 (S012, S028, S048, S049, S052, S056, S074).
- **Read:** he accepts hiring mistakes as the cost of the undiscovered-talent model: "you will absolutely make some mistakes like there's no way to hire on this model and be perfect" (S056).

## First senior hires: CFO, COO, general counsel
- **Stance:** Defer the CFO and general counsel unless regulation forces them. The founder masters the numbers personally first. Hire a COO only as a complement to what the founders lack.
- **Evidence:**
  - 2019: "CFO's you don't need in most businesses for a very long time until you're like roughly ten million dollars in revenue partially because most CFOs are only gonna add about 10% value" (S070)
  - 2019: "typically you would defer general counsel or CFO" (S070)
  - 2022: "whatever the founder and the founding team has, you subtract that and then what distills is what you need." (S061)
  - 2023: "I believe specifically it's the Founder's responsibility to really Master this" (S079), on contribution margin.
  - 2025: "promoted this completely mediocre CFO which is almost always a bad idea to be CEO" (S044)
- **Sources:** 5 (S044, S056, S061, S070, S079).

## Sales teams and enterprise
- **Stance (newer, 2026):** He dropped his long allergy to sales and enterprise. Enterprise can work, but a founder has to sell top-down to executives, and long sales cycles are an excuse.
- **Over time:**
  - 2022: "I have a significant long-standing aversion to sales teams so I kind of throw sales people on problems after I've tried everything else in the world" (S031)
  - 2025: "sales people often end up running the show at the end of the day in my experience" (S048) and "even in enterprise sales a long sales cycles are an excuse" (S048)
  - 2025: "going to go to market first to enterprises fortune 500 companies first companies almost never has worked" (S051)
  - 2026 (newer): "I had this allergy to sales people, which led to an allergy to enterprise." (S046) and "No, I've changed my view completely." (S046) With AI, "top down sales can work extremely well" (S023), but only if you are on the buyer's CEO's top three; otherwise "top-down sales is not going to be your friend." (S046)
  - 2026: "one of the founders has to be able to hold his or her own with high business vis acumen execs." (S046)
- **Sources:** 5 (S023, S031, S046, S048, S051).

## Product managers
- **Stance (newer, 2025 to 2026):** The classic PM with a roadmap is obsolete in AI. What is left of the role is CEO-like: deciding what to build and why.
- **Over time:**
  - 2023: "I have this kind of moderate allergy to product managers" (S012). A good one "simplifies basically translates ambiguity into Clarity" (S012).
  - 2025: agrees with Peter Fenton that product management is obsolete (S051).
  - 2026 (newer): "the idea of PMS does not make sense in a rapidly emerging technology field" (S023) and "The skill is more like being a CEO now, which is what are we building and why?" (S007)
- **Sources:** 4 (S007, S012, S023, S051).

## Team size and headcount in the AI era
- **Stance (newer, 2025 to 2026):** Do not build a company that has to hire at the pace it grows. Judge every company on revenue and contribution profit per employee, and use AI to take headcount out of fixed cost.
- **Over time:**
  - 2022: under-hiring can also hurt; he cites "twitter which under hired engineers initially" (S011). But "i think over the last decade tech companies became very bloated" (S011) and "we haven't divided um accomplishments by employees for a very long time" (S011)
  - 2022: "an employee is an ingredient in cooking a dish" (S085)
  - 2023: "every company in technology overbuilt their infrastructure of people" (S039)
  - 2025 (newer): "basically revenue per employee and contribution profit per employee" (S051) and "you're have to be disciplined about the leverage on every single employee and every dollar you spend on compensation" (S051)
  - 2025: at Opendoor, "use technology to substitute for humans" (S075)
  - 2026: buyers now ask "how do I reduce the number of people I need in accounting or supply chain or others" (S023)
- **Sources:** 8 (S007, S011, S023, S039, S051, S075, S085, plus S032 on headcount without barrels).
- **Read:** the barrel rule makes this consistent: headcount was never the lever. AI makes the cost of over-hiring visible sooner (inferred from S007, S051).

## Roadmaps and adapting to AI
- **Stance (2025 to 2026):** Speed now means speed of adaptability, not only execution. Rip up the roadmap often. Scaled companies have the most to unlearn.
- **Evidence:**
  - 2025: "you have to rip up your road map like every week or every month or maybe every other month" (S051)
  - 2025: "you have to build an organization that can take advantage of speed basically not speed of execution but speed of adaptability" (S057)
  - 2025: "the companies that have to make the biggest adjustments are actually successful uh uh scaled companies like call it ramp." (S057)
  - 2026: "it makes no sense to have a year-long road map." (S007) and "should we rip up everything we've learned or which pieces should we rip up" (S023)
  - 2026: a sign of an AI-ready company: "the number one consumer of tokens is the CMO." (S007)
- **Sources:** 5 (S007, S023, S051, S057, S073).
- **Read:** he admits he does not know when this reverses: "But I don't know what the early like warning signal would be." (S057)

## AI as a business bet
- **Stance (newer, 2025 to 2026):** AI is the main event; growth rules are repealed and old moats like integrations are gone. In 2023 he called it a bad venture bet that would favor incumbents.
- **Over time:**
  - 2023: "AI is a sustaining Innovation not a disruptive innovation" (S012); "as a VC I think it's a horrible investment" (S039); "I think the incumbents will benefit almost all the time from disruption and Ai" (S079)
  - 2025 (newer): "the laws of physics of growth have been completely repealed" (S051); of Google, "Personally, I think more likely disruptive right now" (S057)
  - 2026 (newer): "had I not rejoined KV, I think I would either have missed the whole wave and been completely irrelevant or been reckless" (S023), about 70 percent of his recent deals being AI. And "the ease of creating integrations, which were historically a significant moat, um those have all changed in the last year or two." (S047)
- **Sources:** 9 (S012, S015, S023, S039, S047, S051, S057, S079, S088).
- **Read:** a clear reversal. Use the 2025 to 2026 view.

## Remote versus in office
- **Stance (newer, 2023 onward):** He will not fund or run a remote company. Early and inventive companies must be in person. Exceptions: open source, a single star, and distributed hubs where each craft sits together.
- **Over time:**
  - 2019: depends on the shot; "managing the people sitting next to you is pretty damn hard" (S009)
  - 2020: "personally i'm not the biggest fan of remote only i think there are cases where it can work and be successful" (S010); "but I think it's a conscious decision to make it work" (S025)
  - 2021: allowed, with a different hiring mix, because "in a remote environment it's extremely difficult if not impossible to teach through osmosis" (S034); "i don't think there's a one size fit all" (S027); expects hubs: "i believe that most companies that thrive and are irrationally successful will not be purely remote" (S081)
  - 2022: "i really want to invest in only companies that are in person so i think there's a lot of alpha there" (S052)
  - 2023 (newer): "I won't fund companies now that are being built remotely" (S037); "we don't believe in remote work we won't fund companies that work remotely" (S039); "you can't build a company with undiscovered tolerance and give people internal promotion opportunities if you're using any version of remote work" (S014)
  - 2024: "I'm completely against investing in startups that want to build a remote first company" (S032); hubs are fine: "all the designers have to be in New York all the engineers have to be in Australia" (S078)
  - 2025: at Opendoor, "the company will be in person" (S075)
- **Exceptions:** open source: "I think you can build an open source company predicated on open source software and distribute away because you have thousands of contributors" (S021). A star: "if you're the single best person in the planet I'll consider it" (S064).
- **Sources:** 24 (S009, S010, S014, S018, S021, S025, S027, S032, S034, S037, S039, S040, S052, S059, S064, S071, S075, S078, S079, S081, S085, S088, S089, S090).
- **Read:** the 2021 opening closed by 2023. His reason is the hiring model: undiscovered talent learns by osmosis. He concedes productivity held up in his companies during COVID but "satisfaction per employee is down pretty dramatically" (S078).

## Hours and work ethic
- **Stance:** Long hours are required to build a company that matters, and he expects leaders to set the example. The end goal is a company that no longer needs heroics. In investing, he is less sure hours pay.
- **Evidence:**
  - 2014: "That's why people work 80, 100 hours a week is that heroic efforts is actually necessary to keep this thing together" (S006)
  - 2023: "there's no substitute for like a 996 culture where you're working at least 12 hours a day six days a week" (S012); at Traba "it's 996 in the office every single every single person every single day" (S084), which he says attracts talent.
  - 2023: "I am not sure you make more money by putting Faster by putting more hours in" (S039), on investing.
  - 2025: "hopefully over time you turn this company into a machine that no longer requires heroic effort" (S053)
  - 2026: "No days off. Hashtag no days off. I don't believe in taking days off for workout." (S007), alongside "you must sleep eight hours a day, you must prioritize sleep even when you're very busy." (S007)
- **Sources:** 15 (S006, S007, S012, S013, S018, S021, S024, S037, S039, S043, S053, S055, S057, S076, S084).
- **Read:** consistent and extreme. He pairs hours with sleep, never with all-nighters.

## Vacation, stress, and burnout
- **Stance:** Stress you can control is good; chronic stress without control is bad. If you like the work you do not need vacation. Founding is lonely.
- **Over time:**
  - 2021: as an operator he rarely took vacation; as a VC he does, because he is almost never the one responsible (S041).
  - 2023: "the more stress you have in your life the better" (S012)
  - 2025: "If you like what you do, you don't need vacation, by the way." (S057), while admitting "I've changed a little bit" (S057).
  - 2026: "Chronic stress which means constant pressure where you don't have um you don't have a feeling that you can control the outcome is bad for you." (S020)
  - 2026: "founder being a founder is extremely lonely" (S054)
- **Sources:** 9 (S012, S018, S020, S024, S025, S029, S041, S054, S057).
- **Read:** no recorded position on burnout as a team problem; he treats it as a fit question (inferred from S035, S060).

## Speed and operating cadence
- **Stance:** Velocity is the best early predictor of a company. A problem raised at one board meeting should be diagnosed, fixed, and measured by the next. Decide fast, about 70 percent sure.
- **Evidence:**
  - 2023: "one way you get velocity is like you just use all the time that's available and you turn it into traction" (S037) and "velocity occasionally decays but it never improves" (S037)
  - 2023: "you should be shipping 70 connection you should be deciding yep most of the time" (S037)
  - 2025: "from board meeting one to board meeting two they had identified the root causes of what wasn't working and had already shipped fixes and then that became a pattern" (S019)
  - 2026: "I think the subtle signal, let's say very early, is speed." (S007) and "That speed really does compound." (S007). Open each board meeting with a day counter (S007, S084).
- **Sources:** 9 (S007, S012, S019, S037, S057, S069, S073, S075, S084).

## Metrics: inputs versus outputs
- **Stance (newer, 2020 onward):** Judge people on the quality of their inputs and treat outputs as a byproduct, or your best people will not volunteer for risky bets. He used to say the opposite.
- **Over time:**
  - 2014: "you want to measure outputs, not inputs." (S006); grade managers by output per head: "divide by the number of people on their team and tell them that this is going to be their grade in their performance review." (S006)
  - 2020 (newer): "you need to manage people by inputs, not outputs." (S004), borrowed from Bezos. The secondhand S001 post says the same.
  - 2023: "I've shifted to mostly valuing inputs in in looking at the outputs as a byproduct" (S014); "the only way you're going to get them is if you gauge their inputs not their outputs" (S059)
  - 2024: "one of our four key values is sweat the inputs" (S088)
  - 2025: still metric-heavy: "Square was top down design driven on steroids, but we had to be obsessive on lots of metrics" (S057)
- **Sources:** 9 (S001, S004, S006, S014, S048, S052, S057, S059, S088).
- **Read:** inputs for judging people, outputs on the company dashboard. Both views survive; the 2014 one is about the company number.

## Dashboards and OKRs
- **Stance:** The founder writes the business equation on a whiteboard, builds a dashboard that mirrors it, and pushes it to everyone. A useful dashboard is used daily by nearly everyone. He hates OKRs.
- **Evidence:**
  - 2014: "what fraction of your employees use that dashboard every day. If it's actually useful, it should be close to 100%." (S006)
  - 2022: "you want to broadcast that information to literally everybody in the company as often as you can" (S031)
  - 2023: "literally go to whiteboard and write out the business equation and then make the dashboards reflect that" (S014) and "I do hate okrs" (S014)
  - 2025: "So you have a standard vocabulary, standard metric and then everybody can that that makes a lot of sense." (S069)
- **Sources:** 6 (S004, S006, S008, S014, S031, S069).

## Focus and prioritization
- **Stance:** Few priorities, ranked strictly. One challenge per executive. Name the three to five core risks and put one owner on each. Side projects and conferences are junk food.
- **Evidence:**
  - 2019: "put a name against each of those five things" (S070)
  - 2020: "the key is though to invest in the two to three to four or five that can meaningfully accelerate the business" (S060)
  - 2023: "you will never find a notebook where I write down pros and cons of any decision" (S059); he ranks priorities strictly instead, a rule from Reid Hoffman he has used since 2002.
  - 2023: "I'm only going to allow you to look at One Challenge" (S014), Peter Thiel's rule at PayPal.
  - 2023: "they're kind of like eating junk food they make you they taste good they make you feel good but they're not actually healthy" (S016)
  - 2024: on side projects, "Yeah, generally hate them." (S032); a monopoly can afford them, a startup cannot (S021, S032).
- **Sources:** 9 (S014, S016, S021, S032, S033, S041, S059, S060, S070).

## The CEO's job: edit, decide, set standards
- **Stance:** The CEO edits rather than writes, owns every result, and sets standards others copy. Make the high-conviction, high-importance calls yourself; delegate the rest. Push hardest when things go well.
- **Evidence:**
  - 2014: "an editor is is the best metaphor for your job." (S006)
  - 2024: "as CEO you don't want to be writing and if you feel like you're using a lot of red lines consistently in the same area of a company that's a really bad signal" (S049)
  - 2024: "you can't really delegate things without abdicating" (S035) and "there's no excuses so you can't ever abdicate" (S049)
  - 2024: "low conviction low importance you should delegate every single time" (S018)
  - 2025: "your job at founder mode is to set consistent standards and then people learn to emulate them all the way through the organization." (S063)
  - 2026: "The more success you have, the more complacent the organization tends to get, and the single role for the CEO is offsetting that complacency." (S007)
  - 2023: "that's by far the most important responsibility of the CEO" (S040), about building the team.
- **Sources:** 11 (S006, S007, S009, S018, S035, S040, S049, S056, S063, S083, S086).

## Span of control and org design
- **Stance:** Flat and wide works if the CEO can context switch; he ran about 11 direct reports. What breaks first at 3x to 5x headcount is how decisions are divided. Culture changes past a few hundred people.
- **Evidence:**
  - 2016: "at 50 employees if you're the CEO you should know everybody's name you should know what they're working on" (S067)
  - 2024: "I'd run pretty hot like with 11 or so uh direct reports" (S035); "the canonical view from Andy Grove was like 5 to seven but obviously Jensen's done extremely well with 60" (S049)
  - 2024: "what worked historically sometimes often doesn't work when you multiply the number of people by three four five" (S049)
  - 2025: "after you pass about 250 to 500 employees, you tend to hire people with different goals." (S048)
  - 2024: put two conflicting metrics under one executive so the CEO is not the weekly tiebreaker: "I don't as CEO really want to be making that trade-off decision every week" (S035)
- **Sources:** 6 (S003, S035, S048, S049, S061, S067).

## Culture
- **Stance:** Every good startup is a cult with a shared secret. Culture sets early like concrete. High performance beats comfort; he rejects psychological safety. Values only count if they rule something out.
- **Evidence:**
  - 2014: "every good startup is a cult" (S006)
  - About 2020: "values are only meaningful when they say what you're not going to do like values that everybody who subscribed to are actually completely useless" (S022)
  - 2023: "once the culture is formed that dye is cast the metaphor I like to use is concrete" (S037) and "I actually think a well-run company is partially chaotic" (S037)
  - 2024: "process repels the most talented people and so that if you want a critical density of talent you can't use process" (S058)
  - 2026: "high-performance machines don't have psychological safety, they're about winning." (S007)
- **Sources:** 12 (S006, S007, S014, S018, S021, S022, S037, S055, S056, S058, S061, S084).
- **Read:** he admits he was too passive on culture in OpenStore's first year (S021), so he knows the cost of setting it late.

## Feedback and criticism
- **Stance:** Criticize in public when the lesson is for the system, not just the person. He thinks annual reviews are a waste. Track people by their excuses.
- **Evidence:**
  - 2023: "I literally track everybody in my life by number of excuses I've ever heard from them" (S012)
  - 2024: agrees with Patty McCord on annual reviews: "exactly we can stop there I'm totally on board with this" (S058)
  - 2026: "when you give people feedback negative individually, you're optimizing for the atomic unit, not the system." (S007)
  - 2026: "in public which is very contrarian to how most people teach people to do management but it makes so much sense once you understand it and decompose the logic" (S068)
- **Sources:** 5 (S007, S012, S020, S058, S068).
- **Read:** public criticism is a view he absorbed from a founder he works with, and he grants great coaches do both (S007 limits).

## Transparency
- **Stance:** Show everyone the metrics and the board deck. More information leaks less. Pay transparency is the one open question.
- **Evidence:**
  - 2014: "Metrics are the first step. So everybody in your company absolutely should have access to every single thing that's going on." (S006)

## Boards

## Raising money: milestones, not runway
- **Stance:** Raise to hit the milestones that show an inflection, not to buy months. Runway is a constraint, not a goal.

  - 2023: "Runway is an irrelevant consideration it's like how much fuels in your tank it's a constraint" (S039); "I always think in terms of Milestones inflection points accomplishments proof points around risk reduction" (S040)
  - 2024: "don't worry about Runway worry about lift" (S064)
  - 2026: "and then you kind of sequence your financing around validating those inflection milestones." (S046)

- **Sources:** 8 (S009, S039, S040, S046, S052, S060, S064, S086).

## How much capital to take
- **Stance:** Capital follows success; it does not create it. Too much early money hurts, and over-raising crowds out the next round. Once it works, more fuel helps.
- **Evidence:**
  - 2021: "there's a lot of disadvantages of too much capital but once you're on the path to success often more oxygen is good" (S034)
  - 2023: "I don't think capital is decides who wins and loses I think it follows success" (S037)
  - 2023: "I think there's a point when you raise incremental money that you do crowd out the next round to make the next round more complicated" (S084)
  - 2023: "if you have momentum perceive momentum actual traction no yellow flags you can control your destinated process really easily" (S084)
  - 2023: raising opportunistically is fine: "we did raise money fairly frequently part of that was opportunistic it wasn't necessarily so strategic or intentional" (S040)
- **Sources:** 7 (S007, S014, S034, S037, S040, S066, S084).

## Valuations
- **Stance:** At seed, price matters only across broad zones. Later it decides returns. Companies are always valued on profit in the end. After 2021 he told founders to expect a reset.
- **Over time:**
  - About 2020: "there's probably like zones and they're they're broad zones but as long as you're in these zones the price really doesn't matter" (S022)
  - 2022: "you're always eventually valued on your profitability and the potential profitability" (S011); "valuation is always a trap" (S052)
  - 2023: "they're going to be in for rude awakening on pricing of the terms" (S016); "I would just divide any private valuation by probably three maybe four" (S028)
  - 2023: "it's really rare to be appreciated at more than 10x" (S028), on revenue multiples.
- **Sources:** 8 (S011, S013, S016, S022, S028, S052, S062, S085).
- **Read:** as an investor his most repeated regret is being too strict on price at seed (see stories). As advice to founders he is strict on price after 2021.

## Down rounds and burn

## Downturns and cost cuts
- **Stance:** Cut decisively when capital turns scarce, keep fixed costs low before the cycle turns, and plan for several scenarios with hiring plans you can edit every quarter. How you operate should not change between war and peace.
- **Evidence:**
  - 2022: "a lot of companies were like looking into the abyss and they decided to make decisive changes" (S011)
  - 2023: "have hiring plans that can be edited you know on a quarterly basis at a minimum" (S016)
  - 2023: "actually pretty much nothing should have changed but uh people are getting a lot of bad advice" (S084)
  - 2025: "all that gna is pure burn and they almost went bankrupt" (S044), on Airbnb in COVID; at Opendoor, "has too much of a bloated GNA" (S075)
  - 2025: "we want to do it all in parallel speed" (S075), recruiting, building, and cutting at once.
- **Sources:** 7 (S011, S016, S044, S075, S084, S085, S089).
