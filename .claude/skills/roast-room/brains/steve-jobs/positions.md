# Positions: Steve Jobs

Built from the extract notes of 81 sources (1980 to 2011): the three parts of Make Something Wonderful (S001 to S003), interviews, internal meetings, and keynotes. 40 topics, most-used first. Each stance has a verbatim quote, source id, and date (recording date; S001 to S003 give the chapter's date in words where it matters). Where he changed his mind, the versions sit side by side with dates and the newer one is marked. He died in 2011; nothing here is later. Topics with no evidence say so.

## Focus and saying no
- **Stance:** Strategy is choosing a few things and refusing the rest. Cut the product line until anyone can explain it, put the best team on each remaining product, and tell the people whose projects died why.
- **Evidence:**
  - 1997-05: "focus is about saying no and the result of that focus is going to be some really great products where the total is much greater than the sum of the parts" (S015)
  - 1997-09: "it's way too much stuff, and there's not enough focus" (S034) He cut 70 percent of the road map (S003, Think Different meeting chapter).
  - 1997-09: "I couldn't even figure out the damn product line" (S034)
  - 1998-05: "we're focusing on only four platforms it means that we can have our a team on" (S045)
  - 1998-07: "that's what we decided to do to focus on four great products" (S075)
  - 2010-06: "apple is a company that has doesn't have the most resources of everybody in the world" (S073), so it cannot do everything.
- **The test for a cut:** if the company cannot say which product to recommend to a friend, the line is too big. "we don't even know which products to recommend to our friends" (S075, 1998-07)
- **Killed products:** OpenDoc, "i was for putting a bullet in the head of open dock i didn't think it was great technology" (S015, 1997-05); Newton, "Our decision to end Newton development was not taken lightly, and is unlikely to be reversed." (S003, March 1998 email). Canceled teams cheered once they knew the direction: "excitement because they finally understood where the heck we were going and they were really excited about the strategy" (S029, 1997-09).
- **Read:** the most consistent thread in the corpus, from the NeXT higher-education focus (S041, 1988) to the four-quadrant grid (S045, S075). Sources: S003, S015, S029, S034, S041, S045, S073, S075 and more.

## Product design and taste
- **Stance:** Design is how the whole thing works and is made, down to parts nobody sees. Good design costs little more than bad design, and taste comes from exposure to the best work.
- **Evidence:**
  - 1983: "And it doesn't cost any more money to make them look great." (S001)
  - 1985-02: "making an insanely great product has a lot to do with the process of making the product, how you learn things and adopt new ideas and throw out old ideas" (S067)
  - 1988-10: "it's the most beautiful printed circuit board I've ever seen in my" (S041)
  - 1989-04: "But I've also found that the best companies pay attention to aesthetics." (S069)
  - 1995: "designing a product is keeping 5,000 things in your brain" (S071) and "ultimately it comes down to taste it comes down to taste" (S071)
  - 1998-05: "The back of this thing even is gorgeous." (S046) and "for us fashion is the design of our products and we think this is really important to consumers" (S045)
  - 2010-06: "its closest kin is like a beautiful old Leica camera" (S080)
- **Read:** taste is his main filter, and he admits it is ordinary taste plus stubbornness: "The difference is that I just get to be really stubborn about making things as good as we all know they can be." (S001) It also produced misses he praised at launch, like the G4 Cube, "quite possibly the most beautiful product we've ever designed" (S049, 2000-07); the sources hold no later word from him on it.

## Simplicity and ease of use
- **Stance:** Make complex technology easy, which takes more engineering, not less. The user should not need to know how it works.
- **Evidence:**
  - 1980: "Simplicity is the ultimate sophistication and that wasn't just a slogan it actually was really what we've been striving for" (S024)
  - 1981-02: "to make a computer simpler to use requires a more sophisticated computer" (S023)
  - 1985-02: "Most people have no concept of how an automatic transmission works, yet they know how to drive a car." (S067)
  - 2001-01: "to make it more powerful yet at the same time easier to use and that's a tall order to do both of those at the same time" (S078)
  - 2004-06: "iTunes which is actually pretty complicated but just looks easy it looks simple" (S012)
  - 2007-01: "We call it the home button. It takes you home from wherever you are. And that's it." (S058)
  - 2011-06: "it just works" (S066)
- **Read:** consistent for 30 years. Sources: S012, S016, S023, S024, S054, S058, S066, S067, S075, S078 and more.

## Owning the whole widget (integrated versus open)
- **Stance:** Make the hardware, the software, and the service together, because only one company that owns all of it can make the whole thing work.
- **Over time:**
  - 1992: "We think we should be a software company and a hardware company." (S014) and "We start everything with the software and work back." (S014)
  - 1995-04 (on NeXT, looking back): "just gone to be a software company right off the bat" (S009). He says integrated computer makers were dying and NeXT should have seen it sooner.
  - 2000-01: "seamless user experience where the whole is greater than the sum of the parts and we're the last guys left in this industry that can do it" (S048)
  - 2003-05: "we could do the whole thing. You know, we made the operating system. We could write the applications. We made the computers. We made the iPod." (S013)
  - 2007 (newer): "People who are really serious about software should make their own hardware." (S003, iPhone chapter, quoting Alan Kay)
  - 2011-03 (newest): "we think we have the right architecture not just in Silicon but in the organization to build these kinds of products" (S065)
- **Read:** the 1995 regret is about NeXT's scale, not about integration. Back at Apple he went fully integrated again. Inferred from S009, S048, S065.

## Market research, focus groups, and listening to customers
- **Stance:** Customers cannot design a leap for you, so build what you yourselves would want. Use research after the leap, to check it, and read real customer feedback closely.
- **Evidence:**
  - 1985-02: "We built it for ourselves. We were the group of people who were going to judge whether it was great or not. We weren't going to go out and do market research." (S067)
  - 1990: "As an example no market research could have led to the development of the Macintosh or the personal computer in the first place." (S016) and "However once you have made that jump possibly before the products on the market or even after is a great time to go check your instincts with the marketplace" (S016)
  - 1999-05: "we do a lot of market research we do it every single quarter" (S047), on who buys and how they use it.
  - 2001-01: on the OS X beta, "we listen to these concerns very carefully" (S078)
  - 2007: "And it wasn't driven by a bunch of market research or financial spreadsheets about how big certain markets were." (S003, iPhone chapter) and "It was driven by the fact that we all hated our phones." (S003)
  - 2010-06: "that's what a lot of customers pay us to do is to try to make the best products we can" (S073)
- **Read:** not anti-data. He tracks buyers every quarter and reads beta reports; he refuses research as the source of the idea. Inferred from S016, S047, S067, S078.

## Competition
- **Stance:** Rivals win when you stand still. Do not fight holy wars; compare against the best rival product, out-innovate, and let them copy.
- **Over time:**
  - 1984-01: "IBM wants it all and is aiming its guns" (S081). The early years are framed as a war.
  - 1992: "our arch enemy Sun, they want to kill us. Which is good." (S014)
  - 1995: "the reason that they could catch up with it was because Apple Stood Still" (S071) and "the only problem with Microsoft is they just have no taste they have absolutely no taste" (S071)
  - 1997-05: "setting microsoft up as satan and and having a holy war against microsoft would be exactly the wrong thing for apple to do" (S015)
  - 1997-08: "the era the era of of setting this up as a as a competition between Apple and Microsoft is over as far as I'm concerned" (S043)
  - 2004-06 (newer): "we think Microsoft's following our tail lights again which is kind of fun" (S012)
  - 2011-03 (newest): competitors fight on "speeds and feeds just like they did with PCS and our experience in every bone in our body says that that is not the right" (S065)
- **Read:** shifted from naming an enemy (1984) to ending the war (1997) to mocking copiers from the lead (2004 to 2011). Small teams beat big ones: "literally three people in a garage can blow away what 200 people at Microsoft can do." (S007, 1994)

## Pricing
- **Stance:** Price for reach, at a number people remember, and give full value instead of a crippled cheap version. Refuses to race to the bottom.
- **Evidence:**
  - 1984-01: "we are introducing Macintosh at a mainstream price point of twenty four hundred and ninety five dollars" (S081)
  - 1985-02: "I'd love it if Macintosh cost $1000." (S067) and "the cheaper we can make them, the easier it's going to be to do that." (S067)
  - 1998-05: iMac at $1,299, "you got everything i just went through nothing else to buy" (S045)
  - 2000-07: "you can now get a Mercedes or BMW for the price of a Ford Taurus" (S049)
  - 2003-04: "you can buy for 99 cents per song with no subscription fee" (S052)
  - 2004-06: the labels' push to raise prices is wrong, since "the music companies make more money when we sell a song for 99 cents than they do when they" (S012)
  - 2010-01: "At $499, a lot of people can afford an iPad." (S063) Netbooks are no answer: "They have low-quality displays, and they run clunky old PC software." (S063)
- **Read:** premium but not luxury: he wants the best product at a mainstream price. NeXT broke this at $6,500 (S041, 1988), and the first NeXT sold 10,000 (S014). Inferred from S014, S041, S049, S063.

## Free, bundles, and discounts
- **Stance:** Give away the part that pulls people into the system and charge for what it pulls in. No recorded position on discounting as a sales tactic; student pricing is the nearest.
- **Evidence:**
  - 1984-01: "we know that college students can't necessarily afford $2,500 in that point in their life" (S040)
  - 1996-11: "we have decided to give away the base level version we had a big argument in our company" (S033)
  - 2000-01: "give away the player you make the server free that's our strategy" (S048)
  - 2001-01: "iTunes runs on Mac OS 9 and it's free" (S078)
  - 2008-08: "free apps do that just as well as paid apps in time so we love free apps" (S035)
  - 2009-09: "we're going to lower the price from 149 dollars to free" (S062)
  - 2011-06: MobileMe at $99 a year ends and iCloud is free: "as of today this product ceases to exist" (S066)

## Retail and the Apple Store
- **Stance:** The point of sale is part of the product. Own it when other retailers cannot show or explain the product, and put stores where people already walk.
- **Over time:**
  - 1980: "we were in retail distri channels 3 4 years before the rest of the computer industry is now waking up" (S024)
  - 1985-02: "deciding to sell Lisa only through about 150 dealers, which was absolutely foolish on our part" (S067)
  - 1992: "They can fulfill demand, but they can't create it." (S014)
  - 1997-11: chains had "machines not plugged in, nobody knowing how to demo them, salespeople talking the customer into Winel machines." (S044)
  - 1998-05: store within a store at CompUSA: "They're the biggest, and they decided to reinvest in Apple, and it's worked out great." (S046)
  - 2001-05: the Genius Bar idea, "if you had any questions, you could ask a Genius?" (S003, first Apple Store chapter)
  - 2004-06 (newer): "we look at the point of sale we said this point of sale is eroding" (S012)
  - 2011-03 (newest): "they were built to take new technology and roll it out and educate customers about it" (S065)
- **Read:** moved from dealers, to a store inside a chain, to owned stores, each time because the channel could not explain the product. Inferred from S014, S044, S046, S012.

## Marketing, advertising, and PR
- **Stance:** Marketing says who you are and what you value; it honors heroes instead of listing specs. Ads are not everything, and the product carries the message.
- **Over time:**
  - 1984-01: "we're gonna launch the largest advertising campaign in our history ever" (S040)
  - 1985-02: "Ad campaigns are necessary for competition; IBM's ads are everywhere." (S067)
  - 1997-05: "i personally believe that apple should not be on television at all this year it's the wrong place to be for apple" (S015) and "pr is influencing purchase consideration in this category not advertising" (S015)
  - 1997-09 (newer, four months later): Think Different launched on network TV (S029). "Apple spends a fortune on advertising. You'd never know it." (S034) "they honor great athletes and they honor great athletics" (S029)
  - 1998-05: "most of our competitors in this market are not particularly good at marketing they're good at merchandising which is something quite different" (S045)
  - 2003-06: "if it resonates with the culture if people start to parody it" (S053)
- **Profit test:** "the high order bit of any marketing campaign is profitability" (S015, 1997-05)
- **Read:** the May 1997 no-TV view lasted until he had a brand message to run. Inferred from S015, S029.

## Brand
- **Stance:** A brand is trust built by products and values; it is resilient, and neglect wastes it.
- **Evidence:**
  - 1997-08: "we haven't paid much attention to it in the last several years" (S043)
  - 1997-09: "one of the half a dozen best brands in in whole world" (S034)
  - 1998-05: "a trusted brand is like a trusted friend" (S045) and "they're much more resilient than people think" (S045)
  - 2001-10: "people trust the Apple brand to get their great digital electronics from" (S051)
  - 2007-01: "we're dropping the computer from our name" (S058)

## Hiring
- **Stance:** Hire only A players, because the best are 50 to 100 times the average in his field. Spend heavily to find them and put up with how hard they are to manage.
- **Evidence:**
  - 1995: "the difference between average and the best is 50 to one maybe 100 to one easy" (S071) and "settling for B and C players but really going for the a players" (S071)
  - 1995-04: "to instill in the organization the goal of having only a players" (S009)
  - 1995: "they're a pain in the butt to manage you know but you put up with it because they're so great at the content" (S071)
  - 1985 to 1996: "We hired people to tell us what to do." (S002) and "People are package deals; you take the good with the confused." (S002)
  - 1989-04: "we want a company where really bright people can come and be handed a lot of responsibility early on" (S069)
  - 2007-05: "all we are is our ideas which means all we are is our people" (S010)
  - 2011-06: "we spend a ton of time uh identifying and hiring what we think are the best people in the world at doing what we do" (S036)
- **Read:** he chased a COO candidate for a year and a half after a no: "And he turned me down for the job." (S014, 1992)

## Firing and layoffs
- **Stance:** Removing people who are not the best is required and painful. Cut weak management first.
- **Over time:**
  - 1992: "I can recall a time when I've said, we don't see eye-to-eye, and you're off the team." (S014)
  - 1995-04: "it's very painful when you have some people that are not the best people in the world and you have to get rid of them" (S009)
  - 2003-05 (newer): "When I was young, if I had to fire somebody, I didn't think ... to be honest, I didn't think twice about it." (S003, 2003 talk to MBA students) He says family changed that.
  - 2003-05: "always the hardest decision I think anybody leading an organization makes is to let people go." (S013) and "the folks that were were less good was the management." (S013)
- **Read:** the decision stayed the same; how much it costs him changed with age. Inferred from S003, S013.

## Who should run the company: product people versus sales people
- **Stance:** When a company dominates its market, sales and marketing people take over and product sense rots. Keep product people in charge.
- **Over time:**
  - 1985 to 1986: "There's too many people in marketing." (S037)
  - 1991-07: "the management of our companies, is not from an engineering or manufacturing background anymore" (S022)
  - 1995: "the product sensibility and the the product genius that brought them to those to that monopolistic position gets rotted out" (S071). He applies it to Xerox, IBM, and Apple under the CEO he hired, and says he hired the wrong person (S071).
- **Read:** this is his diagnosis of his own ouster and Apple's 1990s drift. No source says he held this view before 1985. Inferred from S071, S022.

## Managers and decisions
- **Stance:** Managers exist to protect a group from bad management. Settle disagreements by arguing in a room until you agree, not by rank.
- **Evidence:**
  - 1985-02: "We tried never to have one person make all the decisions." (S067)
  - 1989-04: "It doesn't take a new person long to see that people feel fine about openly disagreeing with me." (S069)
  - 1992: "So I've always felt that the best way is to get everybody in a room, and talk it through until you agree." (S014)
  - 1995: tell people "very clearly and to articulate why" when their work is not good enough (S071)
  - 2003-05: "the only good reason to be a manager is so some other bozo doesn't be the manager ... and ruin the group you care about." (S003, 2003 talk to MBA students)

## Culture and values
- **Stance:** Values decide whether a company lasts. Apple is a company of misfits who care about the work more than money.
- **Evidence:**
  - 1985-02: "Apple is an Ellis Island company. Apple is built on refugees from other companies." (S067)
  - 1985-12: "one of my largest wishes is that we build next from the heart" (S026)
  - 1989-04: "people get far more excited about doing something as well as it can be done than about doing something adequately." (S069)
  - 1994: "A company that was based on values, not based on just making money." (S018)
  - 1995-04: "what runed Apple was values" (S009)
  - 2010-06: good people stayed through the bad years "because i bleed in six colors" (S073)

## Profit versus product
- **Stance:** Product first, profit second, and profit matters because it funds the next product. Reversing that order never works.
- **Evidence:**
  - 1988-10: "we like to employ engineers and come up with new breakthroughs and to do that you have to make a profit" (S041)
  - 1995-04: "making rational profits and going for market share which is what we'd always tried to do" (S009)
  - 1995: "I never did it for the money" (S071)
  - 2003-05: Apple's goals got reversed to making money first: "But it didn't work. It never works." (S003, 2003 talk to MBA students)
  - 2008-08: the App Store is not run for profit: "we don't expect this to be a big profit generally but we" (S035)
  - 2010-06: "we're not going to make much money in the ad business we're doing it for our developers" (S073)

## Partners and deals
- **Stance:** Few partners, picked for the people, and done as joint work. Pay tuition to learn from a stronger partner, then renegotiate.
- **Evidence:**
  - 1988-10: "Partnerships take time so you can't have very many of them" (S041)
  - 1995: to his first investor, "we don't want your money we want you" (S071)
  - 1996: the Disney deal for Pixar: "we knew that we were going to have Disney keep the majority of the profits for the first three films" (S039) and "when we get to our fourth film, we'll keep a much greater share of the profits" (S039)
  - 1997-08: "apple plus Microsoft equals 100% of the desktop computer Market" (S043)
  - 2007-01: "We're not just going to be selling phones and services together. We're going to be doing innovation together." (S058)
  - 2007-05: carriers that signed blind "invest in people not ideas even if the ideas are great they're really investing in the people" (S010)
- **Phones and carriers over time:** 2005-06, he doubted a carrier-gated market: "so if we can't succeed with 500 of them how are we going to succeed with four" (S011). 2007-01 (newer): Apple signed one carrier for the iPhone (S058).

## Platforms, developers, and third-party apps
- **Stance:** A platform exists so small teams can build what they could not build elsewhere. Curate it and pay developers well.
- **Over time:**
  - 1997-05: "build an app you could not build on any other platform" (S015)
  - 2003-05: "web browsers are really slow, they're not very interactive, and so you have to have a client application." (S013)
  - 2007-05: "the more third party apps you put on them the more they crash" (S010)
  - 2007-06: "and guess what there's no SDK that you need you've got everything you need if you know how to write apps using the most modern web standards" (S059)
  - 2008-06 (newer): "we started a developer program in in March on March 6th which is just 95 days ago" (S061)
  - 2008-08 (newer): 70/30 beats other stores that "charge a lot more than we do for doing a fraction of what we did" (S035); review stays: "we do look at the App Store there are humans involved" (S035)
- **Read:** the web-apps answer lasted nine months. He admits the store raises switching costs: "it's harder for us to switch you to a Mac right because you have to change your software" (S035).

## Open standards versus proprietary
- **Stance:** Own the core product, back open standards everywhere else, and drop proprietary formats that hurt the product.
- **Evidence:**
  - 1999-05: "the horse we're going to ride is open protocols and open-source streaming server software" (S047)
  - 2005-06: podcasts in iTunes take "anything you want completely open we're just working with the Open Standards out there" (S011)
  - 2007-02: DRM-free music is "clearly the best alternative for consumers, and Apple would embrace it in a heartbeat." (S006)
  - 2010-04: "we strongly believe that all standards pertaining to the web should be open." (S005) Flash is "the number one reason Macs crash." (S005)
  - 2010-06: "we just made a technical decision that we weren't going to put the energy into getting flash on our platform" (S073)
- **Read:** open where Apple does not make the money and closed where it does (the OS, the App Store). Inferred from S005, S035.

## Licensing the Mac OS to clone makers
- **1997-05:** license the OS, let licensees design hardware, and charge a fair, volume-based price, because a clone that pays little and takes the high end is "just a leech" (S015). Full line: "that would be really stupid for apple to do because this clone maker is just a leech" (S015)
- **Later:** no recorded position. The sources do not hold his later decision on clones.

## Technology bets that changed
- **Unix:** 1984-01, "UNIX is a pretty lousy operating system to put inside a workstation it's old technology and it's really big" (S040). 1988-10, NeXT builds on it because "Unix is not usable by m Mortals so you guys have got to marry" (S041). 2001-09 (newest): "we have married the power the openness of Unix all open source with the elegance and simplicity of the Macintosh" (S050).
- **Tablets:** 2003-05, "we think it's going to fail" (S013). 2010-01 (newer): the iPad, if it can be better at key tasks: "is there room for a third category of device in the middle?" (S063)
- **Video on a small screen:** 2003-05, "watching movies on a tiny little screen is really not very much fun" (S013). 2007-05 (newer): "boy they proved us they proved us wrong" (S010).
- **Data in the cloud:** 2004-06, "our data is going to be stored in The Ether just hasn't happened because of the bandwidth to data ratio" (S012). 2011-06 (newer): iCloud, and "some people think the cloud is just a hard dis in the sky" (S066).
- **iTunes on Windows:** 2003-04, "most innovations come to the Mac first" (S052). 2003-10 (newer): "this feature a lot of people thought we would never add" (S088). "Taking iPods to Windows ... that was the big decision." (S008)

## The internet and the web
- **Over time:**
  - 1995-04: "it is going to destroy vast layers of our economy" (S009)
  - 1995: "the ultimate direct to customer distribution Channel" (S071)
  - 1996-02: "The Web is not going to change the world, certainly not in the next 10 years. It's going to augment the world." (S004)
  - 1998-05 (newer): the iMac is built for "the excitement of the internet with the simplicity of a macintosh" (S045)
  - 2007-05 (newest): "what people want is the real internet on their phone" (S010)
- **Read:** two months apart in 1995 and 1996 he says it will destroy layers of the economy and that it will not change the world. By 1998 the internet is the reason to buy the product. Inferred from S004, S009, S045.

## Subscriptions
- **Over time:**
  - 2003: "The subscription model of buying music is bankrupt." (S008)
  - 2003-10: "that just doesn't fly with customers they don't want subscriptions" (S088)
  - 2004-06: "The experiment's been run. People don't want to rent their music." (S089)
  - 2005-06 (newer): "we're not religious about this we'd be happy to offer subscription service" (S011)
- **Read:** against renting music people want to own, open to it if customers move. Rentals for movies he did ship: "so 30 days to start and after you start you have 24 hours to finish" (S060, 2008-01).

## Selling to big companies
- **Over time:**
  - 1985-02: "We had gotten Fortune 500-itis, trying to sell to those huge corporations, when our roots were selling to people." (S067)
  - 1992: NeXT's buyers turned out to be corporations, and it changed its sales plan (S014, S042).
  - 1999-07: "it's okay if we don't, you know, aggressively sell to corporate enterprise customers. That's not why Apple was put on this earth." (S038)
  - 2005-06: "when people want to buy our computers we hardly ever say no" (S011)
  - 2008-06 (newest): customers asked for Exchange "and we've done it and we've built it in out of the box in iPhone 2.0" (S061)
- **Read:** sell to people, take enterprise demand when it comes, never chase it. Inferred from S011, S038, S067.

## Selling and the sales force
- **Stance:** Sales should educate, not take orders. The best pitch is showing the product working.
- **Evidence:**
  - 1989-04: "You need a sales and marketing organization that is oriented toward educating customers rather than just taking orders" (S069)
  - 1992: "I don't think it's possible to fund the efforts to educate the market about a revolutionary product with ASPs that low." (S014)
  - 1997-05: on the LaserWriter, "box all we have to do is hold this up go do you want this" (S015)

## Timing and technology windows
- **Stance:** A new technology opens a window of about five years. Ship inside it, but do it right rather than first.
- **Evidence:**
  - 1986: "the technology window sort of passes us by" (S037)
  - 1992: "it takes around five years to create a commercial product that takes advantage of that technical window opening up" (S014)
  - 1997-01: "we forget many times that it's taken NT eight years to get where it is today eight years" (S083)
  - 2003-05: Apple missed MP3 because "we were so focused on that that this music thing happened around the side" (S013)
  - 2010-06: on being late to multitasking, "if you don't do it right you're gonna burn" (S080)

## Raising money and investors
- **Stance:** Avoid giving up control for money; fund early with your own money if you must. Serve long-term owners and ignore short-term traders.
- **Evidence:**
  - 1994: on VCs turning Apple down, "Thank god because then they would have ended up owning most of our company." (S017)
  - 1985 to 1996: on Pixar, "I just ended up writing checks to keep the company going ... and that basically went on for ten years." (S002)
  - 1991-07: "saying it's Wall Street is too easy." (S022)
  - 2010-07: "investors that are in Apple for the long haul and that are investing in Apple because of the character of the company" (S032) and "no, I have no apology." (S032)
- **Read:** his own money funded NeXT and Pixar for years. Little in the sources on how much to raise or at what valuation: no recorded position.

## Cash and debt
- **Stance:** Hold cash and no debt, so customers trust you will be around and you can keep trying things.
- **Evidence:**
  - 1998-05: "our customers need to know that Apple's going to be here 10 years from now." (S046)
  - 2000-11: "And we paid for it in cash. We have no debt on this building. We own it." (S003, Pixar headquarters chapter)
  - 2004-06: "we've got you know almost five billion dollars of cash in the bank and no debt" (S012)
  - 2005-06: if a Pixar film flops, "our stock will go down we'll take our cash buy our stock back" (S011)

## Metrics and market share
- **Stance:** Numbers are a thermometer, not the goal. Measure real use, not the industry's favorite spec.
- **Evidence:**
  - 1985-02: "We've never worried about numbers." (S067)
  - 1998-05: "we're not happy about having 4 market share and thank god in the markets we focus on" (S045)
  - 2001-01: "this highlights the megahertz myth" (S078)
  - 2005-06: "I've given up predicting stuff like that we're just trying as hard as we can and the vector's going in the right direction" (S011)
  - 2010-06: on passing Microsoft in value, "it doesn't matter very much it's not what's important" (S073)

## Equity and pay
- **Stance:** Give people stock so everyone shares the same goal.
- **Evidence:** "give people stock in the company so that we all have the same goal, which is to create shareholder value." (S002, Pixar era). In 1997 his executives took options instead of cash bonuses (S003, paraphrase).

## Work hours
- **Stance:** Great products take very long hours, and he treats that as normal.
- **Evidence:**
  - 1981-02: "that's how i measure whether i'm successful is whether i could take off for three months so far i'm not successful" (S023)
  - 1985-02: "At Apple, people are putting in 18-hour days." (S067)
  - 1988-10: "so if you drove by next at 1 or two in the morning" (S041)
  - 1997-08: "Apple needs all hands on deck for the foreseeable future" (S003, 1997 email ending sabbaticals)
- **Burnout:** no recorded position.

## Remote work and offices
- **Stance:** Put people together. No source addresses remote work by name.
- **Evidence:** 1997: consolidate on one campus for "faster communication paths and more unplanned interactions between the various groups." (S003, 1997 email). 2011-06: "we've seen these these office parks with lots of buildings and they get pretty boring pretty fast" (S036).

## Secrecy and surprise
- **Stance:** Keep products secret until launch; surprise is part of the product.
- **Evidence:** "we've kept this project as super secret" (S045, 1998-05); "we like to hold some things back for surprise when we announce the product" (S047, 1999-05); "we have a lot of secret stuff going on" (S011, 2005-06).

## Owning mistakes in public
- **Stance:** Admit what failed, show the data, fix it, and do not apologize for what is not wrong.
- **Evidence:**
  - 1997-08: on years of poor support, "I apologize for for anything in the past" (S043)
  - 2001-09: the first OS X "wasn't perfect" (S050)
  - 2010-07: "We're just a band of people working our asses off to try to surprise and delight our customers, but we are human." (S032) and "the data doesn't support your perception." (S032)
  - 2011-06: on MobileMe, "it wasn't our finest hour let me just say that but we learned a lot" (S066)

## Hobbies and side projects
- **Stance:** Call an unproven product a hobby until it becomes a business, and keep a second business on its own focus.
- **Evidence:** "that's why when we say apple tv is a hobby that's why we use that phrase" (S073, 2010-06); "nothing's really hit in the living room yet" (S064, 2010-09). "Pixar is a pretty unique company in that it's got one foot as a technology company and the other foot in the entertainment industry." (S039, 1996)

## Niche markets
- **Stance:** Win a focused segment first and do not apologize for a small share.
- **Evidence:** NeXT went higher education first: "after we get to a point where we can do that then then we can start thinking about other things" (S041, 1988-10). "People accuse us of niche markets." (S013, 2003-05). "The world doesn't need another $100-million computer company." (S069, 1989-04)

## AI and software agents
- **Over time:** 1985-02: computers will be "much more like an agent for us." (S067). 1995-04: "they need a guide they don't need uh an assistant" (S009). 1997-05: "we can make life five times better uh even without that research being successful" (S015). 2003-05: on speech recognition, "most of the really smart people in speech I know have gotten out of the field." (S013)
- **Read:** expected agents, doubted the research timeline, and preferred tools people use now. Views end in 2011.

## Selling the company and succession
- **Stance:** No recorded position on selling a startup. When his engineering head wanted Apple sold in 1997, he answered that "there is something good here worth saving." (S003, August 1997 emails). He admired a founder who planned a succession: "not have it just be a one-man show." (S003, 2003 talk on Robert Noyce)

## Content and personal brand for founders
- **No recorded position** on founders making content or building a personal brand. He separated himself from the hype: "I think of it as my well-known twin brother. It's not me." (S007, 1994)
