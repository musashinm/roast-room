# Stories: Steve Jobs

Built from the extract notes of 81 sources (1980 to 2011). 20 stories and cases he reuses, with the lesson he draws, most-told first. Source ids in parentheses; S030/S031 and S017/S018 count as one source each.

1. **Xerox PARC, 1979.** He saw the graphical interface, half finished, and knew within minutes that every computer would work this way. He was so struck by it that he missed the other two ideas in the room, objects and networking, and took years to come back to them. "And it was just instantly obvious to anyone that this was the way things should be." (S016) Lesson: a leap is visible early in rough form, and the idea that dazzles you can hide the one next to it (S004, S007, S009, S016, S069, S071, S083).
2. **Cutting the product line in 1997.** Weeks after returning he could not say which Mac to recommend to a friend, and customers could not either. Apple cut 70 percent of the road map down to four products in a two-by-two grid, and the engineers whose projects died cheered once they understood the strategy. "what i found when i got here was a zillion and one products" (S045) Lesson: confusion inside the company is the signal to cut (S003, S015, S029, S034, S045, S075).
3. **The Mac team.** Pushed off the Lisa, he took a small group of artists back to the garage, in his words, and built the Mac, reinventing the factory and the marketing too. "Macintosh was developed by a small team of incredibly talented artists" (S081) At the 1984 shareholder meeting the team cried, because "From that day forward it was no longer ours." (S016) Lesson: a small team of the best people, building for itself, beats a big division (S016, S040, S067, S071, S081).
4. **Selling the Apple I to the Byte Shop.** The store wanted 50 fully assembled computers, not boards. They got about $10,000 of parts on credit and paid it back in 29 days. "convinced the electronics parts Distributors around here to give us about $10,000 were the parts on thin air just on enthusiasm" (S024) Lesson: selling and distribution are part of making the product, and the business showed up after the thing was built (S001, S024, S067, S071).
5. **Fired from Apple.** He says he hired the wrong CEO (S071), lost the fight, and stopped going in to work. Later he called it the best thing that happened to him, since it led to NeXT, Pixar, and his family. "It was awful-tasting medicine, but I guess the patient needed it." (S003, 2005 Stanford speech) In 1992 he was blunter: "I think everybody lost. I think I lost." (S014) Lesson: keep building and do the work you love (S001, S003, S014, S071).
6. **Pixar's ten years of checks.** He bought the Lucasfilm graphics group, tried to pay the bills with hardware and commercials, failed, and funded it himself for a decade until Toy Story. "I just ended up writing checks to keep the company going" (S002) John Lasseter taught him the business was story: "John's right he educated me that what we are really about is storytelling" (S021) Lesson: find the real product (story, not technology) and narrow to one pure play (S002, S009, S020, S021).
7. **Disney University.** Pixar took a three-picture deal in which Disney kept most of the profit, as tuition to learn how to make and sell animated features, then renegotiated from strength. "we knew that we were going to have Disney keep the majority of the profits for the first three films" (S039) Lesson: a lopsided first deal can be worth it if it teaches you what you cannot buy (S020, S039).
8. **NeXT: the honeymoon is over.** Six months after the founding retreat he opened the second one by killing the mood and faulting his own lack of hustle. "I don't see that that startup hustle." (S037) The first NeXT Cube sold 10,000: "We came out with this cube. And we sold 10,000 of them. Why? Because we weren't quite there yet." (S014) He later said NeXT should have gone software-only sooner (S009). Lesson: a great machine outside its technology window still fails, and the founder sets the urgency (S009, S014, S026, S037).
9. **Corporate America found NeXT.** NeXT thought its customer was shrink-wrap developers and higher education; big companies building custom apps found it first, and NeXT rebuilt its sales plan around them (S014, S027, S042). Same lesson as PageMaker on the Mac: "we never ever anticipated pagemaker and the revolution that would cause but we were smart enough to see it happening out in the marketplace 9 to 12 months later" (S042) Lesson: you may find your real market after launch, so watch for it and move fast (S014, S027, S042, S083).
10. **We bleed six colors.** At his return Apple was weeks from bankruptcy. The individual contributors who stayed said they bled six colors; the weak part was management, so he removed most of it and promoted young staff. "the folks that were were less good was the management." (S013) Lesson: shared values keep a company alive, and fixing management comes first (S003, S013, S073).
11. **Think Different.** Apple had fired its agency and was running a 23-agency review. He scrapped it, brought back the agency behind the 1984 ad, asked Yoko Ono for John Lennon, and finished the ad at 3 a.m. "we blew that up" (S029) Lesson: a brand campaign says who the company is and what it honors, not what the box does (S029, S034, S075).
12. **The 1997 Microsoft deal.** He ended the rivalry framing and took $150 million of non-voting stock, five years of Office for Mac, and a patent cross-license, while the Macworld crowd booed. "apple plus Microsoft equals 100% of the desktop computer Market" (S043) A year later he compared it to "Nixon goes to China" (S075). Lesson: turn a fight that is killing you into a shared stake (S043, S048, S075).
13. **The iMac.** One box, everything included, for getting on the internet, at $1,299, against "pretty crappy" rival PCs (S045). In the Simplicity Shootout a 7-year-old got an iMac online in 8 minutes 15 seconds while a 26-year-old took 27 minutes 39 seconds with an HP (S075). He dropped the floppy and was called crazy (S073). Lesson: design and simplicity are a reason to buy, and a bold removal looks mad until the market follows (S045, S046, S073, S075).
14. **Late to music, then the iPod.** Apple was so focused on video that MP3 passed it by. "we were so focused on that that this music thing happened around the side and we weren't paying enough attention to it" (S013) The team then built iTunes and the iPod within about a year, "thousand songs in your pocket" (S051). Lesson: admit the miss and move fast with the whole widget (S013, S051).
15. **The iTunes Store and the record labels.** The labels first said Apple was all wet, then came back nine months later when their own services failed. He tried piracy himself as research, and its flaws became the store's spec (S089). "their real competitor was Kazaa" (S013) Lesson: be right, stay patient, and beat free with a better experience at a fair price (S006, S008, S013, S052, S089).
16. **iTunes for Windows.** Putting iPods and iTunes on Windows was the big decision, against the fear of cannibalizing the Mac. "this feature a lot of people thought we would never add" (S088) Lesson: put a hit where the customers are, even on a rival's platform (S008, S012, S088).
17. **The Apple Store.** Chains left Macs unplugged and talked buyers into Windows machines (S044). Apple built its own stores and added the Genius Bar. Customers waited three hours in line at the first one, more than he expected (S087). "Every product we make is in this first 25 percent of the store." (S003, 2001 store tour) Lesson: when the channel cannot explain the product, own the point of sale (S003, S012, S044, S087).
18. **The iPhone.** It came from hating their own phones, not from research (S003). The company fought over whether OS X could fit in a phone, and he backed the team willing to try (S070). Cingular signed without seeing it: "they invest in people not ideas even if the ideas are great they're really investing in the people" (S010) Lesson: build what you want to use, and settle expert fights by backing the people who will try (S003, S010, S058, S070).
19. **Antennagate.** Three weeks after the iPhone 4 launch he called a press event, put the data against the press reports, sent engineers to customers' homes, and refused to apologize to short-term investors. "We're just a band of people working our asses off to try to surprise and delight our customers, but we are human." (S032) Lesson: own the problem, answer with data, fix it for customers, and do not grovel (S032).
20. **MobileMe.** Apple charged $99 a year for a service that did not work well. He admitted it, rewrote it as iCloud, and made it free. "it wasn't our finest hour let me just say that but we learned a lot" (S066) Lesson: when a product fails, say so, rebuild it, and stop charging for the old one (S066).
