# Books: Steve Jobs

Built from the extraction notes and the source table. He wrote no books. The one book of his own words is a posthumous collection, listed first; his written thinking otherwise lives in a few open letters and speeches, listed after it. Then the books he cited when deciding. Summaries are in my own words; no book text is reproduced.

## Make Something Wonderful: Steve Jobs in His Own Words (Steve Jobs Archive, 2023)
- **What it is:** Not written by him as a book. The Steve Jobs Archive collected his speeches, interviews, and emails from 1976 to 2011, published after his death. In this brain it is split into three sources: Part I, 1976 to 1985 (S001), Part II, 1985 to 1996 (S002), and Part III, 1996 to 2011 (S003).
- **Core thesis:** Make the best thing you can, for its own sake, with the best people you can find; profit and reputation follow the work, not the other way round.
- **Key frameworks:** great work costs time rather than money (Michael Moritz interview, 1984, S001); telegraph to telephone, the case for the Mac (S001); recruiting as the main job and testing candidates under pressure (In the Company of Giants interview, S002); stick versus carrot for keeping talent and editing a film before you make it (Pixar interviews, S002); outside the mainstream you must be far better (Terry Gross interview, 1996, S002); the four great products and cutting 70 percent of the roadmap (return to Apple, 1997 and 1998, S003); management by values and the San Diego test, and retail by ambush (Stanford Graduate School of Business speech, 2003, S003); the editing function (D5 interview, 2007, S003); only as good as the next product (email to Apple employees, S003); find the work you love and use mortality to choose (Stanford commencement, 2005, S003).
- **Taught in sources:** S001, S002, S003. Many of its interviews also appear in full elsewhere in the corpus (the Playboy interview, S067; the D5 conference, S010).

## Open letters and major essays (no further books)
- **Thoughts on Music (February 2007, S006):** three options for music copy protection (keep proprietary systems, license them, or drop them), tested against data, ending in a call to abolish DRM. Shows his method of laying out options and arguing each with numbers.
- **Thoughts on Flash (April 2010, S005):** six numbered reasons Apple left Flash off the iPhone and iPad, ending with the most important: no third-party layer between the platform and its developers. The clearest written statement of owning the platform.
- **Stanford commencement address (June 2005, in S003 with its drafts):** connecting the dots looking backward, love and loss, and death as the tool for deciding what matters. Shapes how he chose work, not how he ran products, so it stays thin in this brain.

## Books he cited when deciding
- **The Mythical Man-Month (Fred Brooks):** cited in 1997 for the idea that past a certain team size, adding people slows a project down because communication costs more than the person adds. Taught in S015.
- **The Business Value of Computers (Paul Strassmann):** cited in 1992 for the finding that successful companies spend IT money on operational productivity, not management productivity, which he used to position NeXT. Taught in S014.
- **Maslow's hierarchy of needs:** borrowed in 1998 as the shape of his Apple Hierarchy of Skepticism, where doubters move up one level each time the last doubt is answered. Taught in S003 and S075.
- **The Whole Earth Catalog:** named in the 2005 commencement as a bible of his generation; the source of "Stay hungry. Stay foolish." Taught in S003.
- **Books from his youth (Moby Dick, Shakespeare, Dylan Thomas) and the early seventies (Be Here Now, Diet for a Small Planet):** named in 1985 to explain why he pulled toward the humanities alongside electronics, which feeds his liberal arts view of product design. Taught in S067.
- **On books in general:** a book gives you Aristotle's thinking directly, but you cannot ask Aristotle a question; he saw that gap as something computers could close. Taught in S001, S031, S067.
