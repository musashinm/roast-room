# Principles: Bill Gates

30 decision rules, built from the extract notes on 73 sources (1987 to 2026). Rules lean on his own text and oral history (S001 to S003, S076 to S078) and long interviews; keynotes are used only where he states a rule. "Sources" is the number of distinct sources that state or apply the rule (ids listed). Confidence: high = 3 or more sources, medium = 2, low = 1. Where his view changed, the newer view is marked.

1. **Win the developers; applications decide a platform war.** "The key to the competition in operating systems was getting lots of applications." (S001) "by having a platform out there that is on millions and millions of machines we can enable lots of applications to be written that could never have been justified in the past" (S005) Sources: 7 (S001, S005, S006, S009, S013, S020, S063). Confidence: high.

2. **Hire the smartest people you can find; talent, not money, is the constraint.** "we're not capital constrained" (S023) "we are hiring IQ like mad" (S034) He says taking 30 top people away would erase Microsoft's product lead (S062). Sources: 9 (S001, S015, S016, S018, S023, S034, S049, S062, S072). Confidence: high. **Later correction:** he says hiring only for raw IQ was an illusion he dropped early, and that young Microsoft was poor at drawing out people with different styles: "we weren't that good at drawing out the talents of people who had different, behaviors, styles." (S053) (also S035, S039).

3. **Keep teams small and give them excellent tools.** "pick good people, use small teams, give them excellent tools" (S001) He contrasts Microsoft's small teams with IBM's roughly 1,400 developers on OS/2 (S001). Sources: 4 (S001, S018, S025, S053). Confidence: high.

4. **License to everyone; never give one partner exclusivity.** "we wanted our software to be used on all the machines" (S001) "you didn't pay us enough for for to keep us happy the rest of our lives you don't own our company so we have to be able to sell it to other people" (S017) Sources: 5 (S001, S015, S017, S036, S045). Confidence: high.

5. **Price low for volume; software is fixed cost with no marginal cost.** "we have a scale economic business with huge fixed R&D costs and no marginal cost" (S025) "there are a lot of desks. I don’t have to make that much money per desk." (S024) Sources: 7 (S001, S024, S025, S045, S053, S068, S071). Confidence: high.

6. **Software never wears out, so you only get paid for real breakthroughs.** "software has this interesting property that unfortunately it never wears out" (S071) "if if we don't make the next version exciting enough and easy enough to get to then they'll just they'll just stick with what they have" (S020) Sources: 3 (S013, S020, S071). Confidence: high.

7. **Missing a huge shift is the mistake that counts; being early is survivable.** "the biggest mistake uh is where we miss uh something that's coming along is going to be huge" (S063) On IPTV: "we got in probably five years before we needed to it's just now starting to happen" (S063) Sources: 5 (S018, S020, S045, S062, S063). Confidence: high.

8. **Run scared, most of all when you are winning.** "I think the scariest thing is that the company would become successful enough that it would get complacent and we'd stop worrying about what we're missing" (S020) "we were always running scared." (S024) Sources: 8 (S020, S024, S025, S034, S036, S039, S045, S062). Confidence: high.

9. **Obsolete your own products before someone else does.** "make sure that that they obsolete their own products" (S008) "Any company that stays the same will be passed by very quickly and there are lots of fine examples of that." (S001) Sources: 4 (S001, S008, S071, S078). Confidence: high.

10. **Put the whole company behind one strategy.** "Microsoft is betting its entire future on this Windows approach as somebody might say we have all of our wood behind one arrow" (S005) "I want every product plan to try and go overboard on Internet features." (S078) He learned it from the OS/2 split, which he calls one of the most famous failures in software (S005). Sources: 4 (S003, S005, S020, S078). Confidence: high.

11. **When everyone agrees, look for the contrarian view; frenzies hide bad bootstraps.** "if everybody's saying yes watch out if there's no contrarian view" (S020) "when something's hot you get kind of those bubbles" (S064) Interactive TV was his example of groupthink (S020). Sources: 4 (S017, S020, S063, S064). Confidence: high.

12. **Be patient with a platform bet; hard platform software takes about ten years.** "if you find an idea that requires three or four years of improvement and patience and really sticking with it that we're very good at that" (S020) "that's where things take 10 years to get done" (S066) Sources: 6 (S005, S008, S012, S019, S020, S066). Confidence: high.

13. **Do not attack an installed base; carry it forward.** "if anybody puts themselves in the position of attacking that i think they're making a mistake" (S022) "And that's because of the uh commitment to backwards compatibility." (S009) Sources: 4 (S009, S013, S020, S022). Confidence: high.

14. **Formats and standards lock in early, so compete before they set.** "Once a format gets established it is extremely difficult for another format to come along and even become equally popular." (S078) "it's very hard to take a leading software product and replace it you know it doesn't happen it shouldn't happen very often" (S034) Sources: 4 (S010, S025, S034, S078). Confidence: high.

15. **Use the new thing yourself and start with the facts.** "I encourage everyone on the executive staff and their direct reports to use the Internet." (S078) "first start with the facts then proceed from there" (S019) After 10 hours of browsing he found almost no Microsoft file formats on the Web (S078). Sources: 5 (S006, S019, S022, S024, S078). Confidence: high.

16. **Ask for the numbers.** "Give me the numbers. Will it be cheap?" (S067) He argues by rates, costs and thresholds and pushes others to do the same (S026, S054, S057). Sources: 4 (S026, S054, S057, S067). Confidence: high.

17. **Hold a year of payroll in cash and keep leverage low.** "i wanted to have enough money in the bank to pay a year's worth of payroll" (S062) "leverage is a very dangerous thing" (S064) Sources: 7 (S015, S017, S039, S045, S061, S062, S064). Confidence: high. In S039 (2023) he says two years; the other sources, including S045 (2025), say one.

18. **Do not take money you do not need; take investors for their advice.** "our business was profitable enough, we really didn't need to do that, but we wanted people on our board to give us advice." (S061) "we don't take up front money you know development money from our customers we don't don't need that" (S015) Sources: 4 (S001, S015, S039, S061). Confidence: high.

19. **Hold a huge vision but plan only the next doubling.** "my plan is to double you know clearly we can double" (S017) He says each doubling felt like the last one when the 1975 goal was written (S066). Sources: 2 (S017, S066). Confidence: medium.

20. **Fund long-term research even when managers see no return, and keep the lab open to better outside work.** "ought to invest more in R&D this is defining the future of the company it is our competitive edge is that we're out there on the frontiers" (S072) "even if we're pursuing in one way of doing AI internally if there's another way that's making better progress outside I want to know about it" (S072) Sources: 7 (S011, S034, S047, S048, S071, S072, S073). Confidence: high.

21. **Do not dismiss a new capability for flaws that are not fundamental.** "Every example they give of a specific thing it doesn't do, wait a few months." (S044) "Whatever limitations it has today will be gone before we know it." (S076) Sources: 3 (S039, S044, S076). Confidence: high. **Changed view:** he held the opposite for decades. In 2004 he called the shipped AI "pretty low level. Uh, better than nothing." (S071), in 2013 he put general AI "at least five times as long as Ray Kurzweil says" (S069), and in 2022 he expected the AP Biology bar to take OpenAI two or three years (S076). Newer view from 2023.

22. **Choose ease of use and reach over raw power.** "we can't just deliver power we have to deliver ease of use" (S005) "So simplicity really is a moving target." (S010) Sources: 6 (S005, S007, S009, S010, S013, S022). Confidence: high.

23. **When features and trust collide, choose trust.** "So now, when we face a choice between adding features and resolving security issues, we need to choose security." (S003) "If we don't do this, people simply won't be willing -- or able -- to take advantage of all the other great work we do." (S003) Sources: 4 (S003, S010, S012, S019). Confidence: high.

24. **Choose what not to do, and say no outside your focus.** "we've chose not to be in the services business" (S023) "fortunately you can say no because focuses is key to philanthropy" (S018) Sources: 6 (S017, S018, S023, S037, S039, S069). Confidence: high.

25. **Do not get angry at piracy or free software; make yours better and price to the market.** "being upset about it is never effective" (S070) "they have to make it better than what it's ever free and be able to charge for that" (S070) In 1998 he said of China: "as long as they're going to steal it we want them to steal ours" (S062). Sources: 3 (S019, S062, S070). Confidence: high.

26. **Pick partners whose skills differ from yours and who will check you.** "picking picking a partners is crucial" (S062) "I've always built teams that have the skills that that I'm not bringing to the picture." (S067) He names Paul Allen (vision) and Steve Ballmer (people) as his best decisions (S024, S061, S062). Sources: 6 (S024, S039, S061, S062, S063, S067). Confidence: high.

27. **Fund many shots and expect most to fail.** "for every crazy thing we need to solve we're finding at least three or four people most of them will end up being wrong" (S058) "there's many different approaches being used which gives us the likelihood of one or multiple of them working is very high" (S074) Sources: 7 (S046, S048, S055, S058, S059, S064, S074). Confidence: high.

28. **Talk to governments before they come to you.** **Changed view:** in the 1990s he boasted Microsoft had no Washington office and thought being right on the law was enough. Later: "eventually I came to regret that statement because it was kind of almost like taunting Washington DC" (S018) "that I didn't have to talk to politicians. Well, that was a mistake." (S038) "let's constantly be in touch with the governments around the world, make sure they're good with what we're doing" (S045) Sources: 5 (S018, S037, S038, S039, S045). Confidence: high. Newer view from 2019 on.

29. **Once an industry has many competitors, do not expect it to regulate itself.** "you cannot expect an industry to regulate itself. That doesn't work." (S033) "once you have multiple competitors, the level of responsibility can't be with the companies." (S027) He applies this to AI, where he wants required safeguards rather than voluntary review (S026, S028). Sources: 5 (S026, S027, S028, S033, S038). Confidence: high.

30. **Measure one hard number, and share failures as well as wins.** "is to measure the impact of the work and to share that, success or failure, so that others can learn from the efforts" (S060) "our metrics had us focus on the easy stuff" (S059) He scores his own record this way: global health beat its goals, US education "has not had the impact we hope for" (S037). Sources: 5 (S017, S037, S049, S059, S060). Confidence: high.
