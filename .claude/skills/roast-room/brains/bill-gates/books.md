# Books: Bill Gates

Built from the extract notes on 73 sources. Three books of his own appear in the corpus, plus the books he cites that bear on business, technology, or how he decides. Book text was not fetched; summaries are in my own words from what he says about each book in the sources. Business Adventures (John Brooks) is not mentioned in any source in this corpus, so it is not summarized here. His 1999 book Business @ the Speed of Thought is also absent from the corpus.

## His own books

## The Road Ahead (1995; paperback revision 1996)
- **Core thesis:** Over the next 20 years PCs turn from tools for one person into communication devices, and software has to lead the way. He describes a 10-year-out world of cheap devices in every size, high-speed networks, digital money, and agents.
- **Key frameworks:** backcast from the pervasive end state; the four building blocks (fast networks through deregulation, cheap hardware, authoring tools for non-technologists, applications from other industries); a common architecture across form factors; agents that act for the user.
- **What changed:** For the 1996 paperback he rewrote about 5 of 12 chapters for the Internet and played down interactive TV (S020). By 2010 he said it did not fully anticipate the internet and called information superhighway "the stupid term at the time" (S017). In 2023 and 2025 he points back to it for agents, digital money and video conferencing (S077, S050).
- **Taught in sources:** S008 (the 1995 Comdex keynote built on it), S016, S017, S020, S050, S077.

## How to Avoid a Climate Disaster (2021)
- **Core thesis:** Getting to zero emissions needs innovation across every sector, because brute force and asking poor countries to cut back will not work. Progress is measured by the green premium, the extra cost of the clean product.
- **Key frameworks:** the 51 billion tons ruler (judge any proposal as a share of it); the five emission categories, none optional; the green premium by sector; supply of innovation plus demand for green products; three camps holding back action (deniers, those who think it is easy, those who think it is impossible).
- **Why it matters for this seat:** it is his clearest written method for picking and measuring long-horizon hard-tech bets.
- **Taught in sources:** S055, S056, S057 (book tour), with the same frameworks in S046, S051, S059, S067.

## Source Code: My Beginnings (2025)
- **Core thesis:** A memoir of his childhood and Microsoft's first years: early programming with real feedback from adults, the bet that computing would become free, and the founding of the software industry.
- **Key frameworks:** scarce to free (computing then, intelligence now); the software factory against single-product rivals; run scared; complementary founders (his technical view, Ballmer's people view).
- **Taught in sources:** S024, S025, S034, S035, S038, S053.

## Books he cites on business and technology

## Only the Paranoid Survive (Andrew Grove, 1996)
- **What he says it argues:** that a company must stay alert to shifts that can end it. He says it puts into words a theme already in Microsoft's culture: wake up every day and ask what you are missing.
- **Taught in sources:** S020.

## Alfred Sloan's account of General Motors (title not named in the source)
- **What he says it argues:** how to organize a large company, with empowerment inside a coherent policy. He says Sloan is a better model than Henry Ford for running a company.
- **Taught in sources:** S016.

## Fumbling the Future (Douglas Smith and Robert Alexander, 1988)
- **What he says it argues:** how Xerox failed to profit from PARC, which had the best concentration of talent of its time. He uses it as the case that research without a path to product captures no value, and notes Microsoft hired more PARC people than Xerox kept.
- **Taught in sources:** S048 (with related remarks in S073).

## Superintelligence (Nick Bostrom, 2014), Life 3.0 (Max Tegmark, 2017), A Thousand Brains (Jeff Hawkins, 2021)
- **What he says they argue:** the three books he says shaped his thinking on AI, while adding that he does not agree with everything in them and they do not agree with each other. He credits Life 3.0 with asking what role intelligence plays in future scenarios, and says its picture of computers doing easy tasks first turned out wrong in order, since white-collar work fell before blue-collar work. He says the purpose question under superintelligence has not been written about well.
- **Taught in sources:** S076, S041, S051.

## Superagency (Reid Hoffman, 2025)
- **What he says it argues:** he points to its chapter on AI and mental health as one he is using to push his own thinking on AI companions and the shortage of therapists.
- **Taught in sources:** S050.

## The Infinity Machine (Sebastian Mallaby)
- **What he says it argues:** a history of Demis Hassabis, DeepMind, and the founding of the AI industry.
- **Taught in sources:** S026.

## Nexus (Yuval Noah Harari, 2024)
- **What he says it argues:** when the printing press arrived, books on finding witches sold best, so a new capability is not necessarily used well first. He ties it to algorithms rewarding outrageous content and AI heightening that.
- **Taught in sources:** S038.

## The Long Now (title as he gives it)
- **What he says it argues:** institutions change at different speeds: religions slowest, then governments, then individuals and businesses, because those who can lose a job or go out of business get feedback. He uses it to argue that governments will lag AI.
- **Taught in sources:** S039.

## Vaclav Smil: Energy Myths and Realities and Energy Transitions (2010), Energy and Civilization (2017), How the World Really Works (2022)
- **What he says they argue:** with numbers, how slow and hard it is to change an energy system; that intensifying energy use is how societies escaped subsistence; and that most people do not understand how much the economy rests on steel, cement, electricity and food. He calls How the World Really Works the best place to start, names Smil as an author who shaped his thinking, and says "We absolutely have to surprise Vaclav on the upside" (S067). This is the source of his habit of checking a bet's timeline against build cycles and installed bases.
- **Taught in sources:** S039, S044, S054, S057, S058, S067. S054 also cites David MacKay's book on UK energy (title not named), which adds up energy use and supply choices.

## Redefining Health Care (Michael Porter and Elizabeth Teisberg, 2006)
- **What he says it argues:** he calls it the best single book on health costs; its point for him is that fixing the system needs measurement and a framework for competition.
- **Taught in sources:** S049.

## World Development Report 1993 (World Bank)
- **What he says it argues:** the world was not prioritizing cheap ways to save lives, and some diseases got no work at all because the people who had them could not pay. It is the origin of his market-failure test for picking where the foundation bets.
- **Taught in sources:** S065, with the same story in S060 and S068.

## Also mentioned, outside this seat's scope

- The Better Angels of Our Nature (Steven Pinker): violence and hardship have declined over time; his book for keeping perspective (S038, S051, S069).
- The Feynman Lectures on Physics: proof that a motivated learner can learn without a university (S049).
- Steven Pinker and Thomas Sowell as authors who shaped his thinking (S044); These Truths (Jill Lepore, S018); Salt Becomes Breath, The New Jim Crow, a Churchill biography and a Fareed Zakaria book (S074); The Correspondent and Into the Woodchipper (S026).
