# Books: Mark Zuckerberg

He has written no books. His written thinking lives in letters, essays and memos, which are in the corpus; they are listed below, with the books he cites in talks after them. Summaries are in my own words.

## His major letters, essays and memos

### The Hacker Way, letter to investors (2012)
- **Core thesis:** Facebook was built as a mission first and a company second; money funds better services, and the company runs on continuous iteration.
- **Key ideas:** ship small iterations and learn; prototype instead of debating; the best idea wins, not the best lobbyist; five values (focus on impact, move fast, be bold, be open, build social value), with move fast and break things as the speed line that he retired in 2014.
- **Taught in sources:** S001; the values reappear in S009, S026, S047, S070, S071.

### Building Global Community (2017)
- **Core thesis:** After connecting friends and family, Facebook's next job is social infrastructure for communities: supportive, safe, informed, civically engaged and inclusive.
- **Key ideas:** measure meaningful groups, not groups overall; content standards set by personal settings plus regional norms, enforced with AI; moderation errors are operational scaling problems; surface more perspectives rather than ban misinformation.
- **Taught in sources:** S085; echoed in S046, S073.

### Harvard commencement speech (2017)
- **Core thesis:** Purpose comes from big projects, a broader idea of equality that lets people try many things, and community.
- **Key ideas:** ideas only become clear as you work on them; the obvious change nobody makes is yours to make; give others a sense of purpose, a lesson from losing his management team after he refused to sell.
- **Taught in sources:** S012.

### A Privacy-Focused Vision for Social Networking (2019)
- **Core thesis:** Communication will shift from the public town square to the private living room: encrypted, less permanent messaging.
- **Key ideas:** six principles (private interactions, encryption, reducing permanence, safety, interoperability, secure data storage); start from the most private use case and build outward; do not store data in countries with weak rule of law, even at the cost of the market.
- **Taught in sources:** S084; the same plan is presented in S073.

### Four Ideas to Regulate the Internet (2019)
- **Core thesis:** Governments should set rules in four areas (harmful content, election integrity, privacy, data portability) so companies do not decide alone.
- **Key ideas:** report harm quarterly like financial results; GDPR as a common global baseline; data portability so developers can compete.
- **Taught in sources:** S003; related views in S059.

### Standing for Voice and Free Expression, Georgetown speech (2019)
- **Core thesis:** When in doubt, err toward more expression, and police the authenticity of accounts rather than an ever-wider list of banned speech.
- **Key ideas:** three threats to expression (laws, platforms, a culture that calls more speech dangerous); judge enforcement by prevalence and the share found before anyone reports it; an oversight body his own team cannot overrule.
- **Taught in sources:** S004; his 2025 policy reset in S014 returns to it.

### Founder's Letter, Meta (2021)
- **Core thesis:** The metaverse is the successor to the mobile internet, and the company becomes metaverse-first under a new name.
- **Key ideas:** devices at cost or subsidized; side-loading and PC streaming kept; low creator fees and open standards; report two segments (family of apps and future platforms); his views were shaped by living under Apple's rules. He reversed the order of this bet in 2026, putting AI first.
- **Taught in sources:** S005; presented in S077; revisited in S037 and S079.

### Update on Meta's Year of Efficiency (2023)
- **Core thesis:** Become a better technology company and improve financial performance so the long-term vision can be funded.
- **Key ideas:** flatter is faster; leaner is better; keep technology the main thing; invest in tools; in-person time helps; indirect costs of low-priority projects compound; no more than ten direct reports per manager; around 10,000 roles cut.
- **Taught in sources:** S006; framed in S080 and S081, reasons given in S016.

### Open Source AI Is the Path Forward (2024)
- **Core thesis:** AI will follow the path from closed Unix to open Linux, and Llama should become the industry standard.
- **Key ideas:** developers need to control their own destiny and avoid lock-in; selling model access is not Meta's business, so opening costs little; Open Compute saved billions; judge harm against what a search engine already gives; wide deployment lets larger actors check smaller bad ones.
- **Taught in sources:** S007; argued in S035 and S041. He grew more selective about open-sourcing from 2025 (S008, S082).

### Personal Superintelligence (2025)
- **Core thesis:** Superintelligence should go into individual hands to serve each person's own goals, not be run centrally to automate all work.
- **Key ideas:** glasses become the primary computing device because they see and hear what you do; the rest of this decade decides the path; be careful about what gets open-sourced as capability rises.
- **Taught in sources:** S008; extended in S031, S038, S079 and S083.

### Internal emails on acquisitions (2012, published 2020)
- **Not a published essay,** but his own written reasoning on buying rivals: nascent networks can be disruptive before they are big businesses, and buying one buys time.
- **Taught in sources:** S011.

## Books he cites

- **The Aeneid (Virgil):** he reads it as the model entrepreneurship story, a clear mission, a team and perseverance through disaster (S065). He studied Classics and Latin; the Augustus parallels recur (S018, S026).
- **Einstein, a biography (he names Walter Isaacson's in S038):** people forget a great mind's wrong theories and remember the contributions, so mistakes on the way matter less than what you get right (S038, S063, S065).
- **Ender's Game, Snow Crash, Ready Player One, Rainbows End:** science fiction on VR and AR; he notes they picture dystopias and he does not (S026).
- **The Way of the Warrior Kid (Jocko Willink):** a bedtime book for his daughters (S026).
- **1984 (George Orwell):** he says the fact-checking program came to feel like something out of it (S014).
