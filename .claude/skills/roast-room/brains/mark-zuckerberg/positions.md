# Positions: Mark Zuckerberg

39 topics, built from the twelve batches of notes on 81 sources (2005 to 2026). Each stance has a verbatim quote, source id, and date (recording dates from `sources.md`; S011 quotes 2012 emails published in 2020). Where he changed his mind, the versions sit side by side with dates and the newer view is marked. Topics with no evidence say so. S020 and S021 are one recording and are cited as S020.

## Competition and reading threats early
- **Stance:** Watch the small rival that owns a social mechanic before it is big, learn faster than it, and do not over-rotate on the rest. Publicly he calls tech non-zero-sum; privately he treats rising networks as threats.
- **Over time:**
  - 2005-12: shrugs at incumbents: "instead of worrying about who is the big player and what is Google going to do next" (S050)
  - 2010-07: "I actually think the biggest challenge might be maybe how can we move even faster because you see other people creeping up" (S058)
  - 2010-11: "one of the best things about the technology industry is that it's not zero-sum" (S055)
  - 2012 emails: "Instagram can hurt us meaningfully without becoming a huge business" (S011)
  - 2013-10: he ran a lockdown against an exact clone in 2005, but warns "I do think that it's definitely possible to over rotate on competitors" (S053)
  - 2022-03: "When there's a new competitor, that doesn't make me not sleep at night." (S026)
  - 2024-02 (newer): a strong rival "creates more of a sense of urgency." (S033) "I take Apple seriously." (S033)
  - 2024-09: "I think apple is a bigger competitor Than People realize" (S020)
- **Read:** the public "not zero-sum" line and the private "can hurt us" emails coexist (inferred from S055, S011). He also admits being late: Meta still thought feed was where people interact when TikTok rose (S028), and TikTok was big before Meta moved to win (S022, 2025-05).

## Buying a rising competitor
- **Stance:** Buy the nascent network that could disrupt you, ideally early and small, when you can grow it faster than it could alone.
- **Over time:**
  - 2011-11: buy teams for people: "often the Acquisitions aren't even to to Really buy their company or what they're doing it's to get the really talented people" (S049)
  - 2012 emails: "what we're really buying is time" (S011) "One thing about startups is you can often acquire them." (S011) "we'll just regret not doing them" (S011) Forty-five minutes later: "I didn't mean to imply that we'd be buying them to prevent them from competing with us in any way" (S011)
  - 2014-02 (WhatsApp, $19B): engagement was the proof: "about 70% of people who use WhatsApp use it uh every day" (S074) and "it wasn't really until we got aligned on that Vision between Facebook and WhatsApp that we actually started talking about numbers" (S074)
  - 2016-08 (Oculus): "the Oculus team is by far the most talented team working on that problem um so it just made sense to go make this big move" (S054)
  - 2019-06 (defending the deals): "if it were always the case that that mergers were bad for innovation then we would not allow any mergers in our society" (S059)
  - 2023-06: "if Instagram joined us early on then I felt like we could help grow that quickly and same with WhatsApp" (S016)
  - 2023-09: his test for any product: "if I were running that I could scale that to reach a billion people." (S027)
- **Read:** this is a giant's move. It needs cash, an existing growth machine, and tolerance for antitrust scrutiny; the one rival he could not buy was Google (S011). A small founder's version is to spot the threat early, not to buy it (inferred from S011, S016, S059).

## Copying versus inventing
- **Old view (2009 to 2012):** win by being better, never by copying. On a German clone: "if we succeed we'll do it by having a better product" (S057, 2009-01). "companies that are getting started now um that are just trying to copy the stuff that um that the other companies are doing just aren't aren't Successful" (S052, 2012-10)
- **Newer view (2022 to 2024):** learn openly from rivals and build the better version. "TikTok really showed that a bunch of these things were possible" (S029, 2022-10). "we're not embarrassed about learning from things that other people like discovered that we're good first and and then we build the better version of it" (S020, 2024-09). "no one company is going to invent everything right I think if you don't invent anything then it's hard to to kind of be a successful company" (S020, 2024-09)
- **Read:** his copies worked because Meta can put a format in front of billions: "there's no other company I think that is as good as us at kind of taking something and kind of getting it in front of billions of people." (S082, 2025-07) Copies were also late (Reels, S028) or launched before ready (Threads, S081). A small company copying a leader has none of that distribution (inferred from S052, S020, S082).

## Platform risk and gatekeepers
- **Stance:** Never fully depend on a competitor's platform for your core product; if you are big enough, build the next platform yourself. His view hardened over a decade.
- **Over time:**
  - 2014-04: "No one has an incentive to help bridge the gaps between these platforms" (S070)
  - 2015-03: on depending on Android: "it's certainly a little bit stressful" (S040) His answer then: "Our strategy is just to build things that people really want to use" (S040)
  - 2021-10: "Living under their rules has profoundly shaped my views on the tech industry." (S005)
  - 2022-11: "Apple's stands out as the only one where one company can control what do what apps get on the device" (S048)
  - 2024-04: "I don't want any of those other companies telling us what we can build." (S018)
  - 2024-09: "I think we might be like twice as profitable if we own the platform or something" (S020) "we've been through too much stuff with the other platforms to to fully depend on on anyone else" (S020)
  - 2025-05: "I honestly get more offended by like the consumer things that we just think are good that they just block us from doing." (S022)
  - 2026-10 (newer): "it's less about wanting to own a platform for its own sake. It's about being able to build the best things." (S023)
- **Read:** his cure (own glasses, an OS, your own models) costs tens of billions a year (S033). A startup can name the gatekeeper risk and diversify, not build a platform (inferred from S020, S033).

## Open versus closed ecosystems
- **Stance:** Every computing generation has one closed integrated stack and one open one; value drifts to the closed one, so the challenger should play open.
- **Over time:**
  - 2022-10: "in each generation of computing that I've seen so far, you know, PC's, mobile, there's basically an open ecosystem and there's a closed ecosystem." (S029) Goal: "to make sure that in this generation of computing, the open ecosystem wins again." (S029)
  - 2023-09: "We want to be the open ecosystem and we want the open ecosystem to win." (S027)
  - 2024-07: "I think Apple pretty clearly won this generation." (S041)
  - 2024-09: open platforms let "an individual someone getting started in their dorm room like me to not have to ask for permission to go build the next set of awesome things" (S020)
- **Read:** he says plainly it is self-interest: "It's not we're not doing this because we're kind of altruistic people." (S041, 2024-07)

## Open source as a competitive weapon
- **Stance:** Open what is not your edge and what gets better with an ecosystem; keep the product layer closed. More caution about frontier models since 2025.
- **Over time:**
  - 2005-12: kept the caching and search layers closed: "And it's on top of whether or not you want to lose the competitive advantage." (S050)
  - 2023-06: "I feel quite confident in the assessment that open sourcing models now is net positive" (S016) Near superintelligence: "we haven't made a decision yet as to what we would do if we were in that position" (S016)
  - 2023-09: "we don't have a cloud business, right? So it's not like we're selling access to the infrastructure. So giving it away is fine." (S027)
  - 2024-04: the limit: "Maybe the model ends up being more of the product itself. I think it's a trickier economic calculation then, whether you open source that." (S018)
  - 2024-07: "selling access to AI models isn't our business model." (S007)
  - 2024-09: open when second: "okay not an advantage for us because Google already has that so uh we might as well just make it open" (S020)
  - 2025-07 (newer, more cautious): "We'll need to be rigorous about mitigating these risks and careful about what we choose to open source." (S008) "I also expect us to continue to not open source everything that we do" (S082)
  - 2026-07: on relying on others' open models: "right now the open source models are not as strong as the frontier models, so no is the the basic answer." (S083) "we're not we're not dogmatic about this." (S083)
- **Read:** open source was a positional bet for a company in second place, not a principle; once Meta rebuilt its lab and the model became the product, he pulled back (inferred from S020, S018, S082).

## Network effects
- **Stance:** Social products are won mechanic by mechanic, and whoever connects everyone fastest keeps it.
- **Over time:**
  - 2010-07: the asset is the community, not the data: "the real thing that Wikipedia is isn't an encyclopedia it's a community of people that build encyclopedias" (S058)
  - 2012 emails: "There are network effects around social products and a finite number of different social mechanics to invent." (S011)
  - 2013-10: "it's connecting everyone as quickly as possible because I mean Network effects were a massively important part of this" (S053) Go abroad early: "those things are really annoying once they get lodged in" (S053)
  - 2026-10 (newer): the reason Muse is free: "make it so that it can be widely used, because I think that there are going to be some kind of valuable network effects at scale" (S023)

## Founder control and voting
- **Stance:** Keep enough control that no board or investor can force a sale or kill a long bet.
- **Over time:**
  - 2005-10: resisted bringing in a seasoned CEO: "patience is somewhat important" (S051)
  - 2018-04: "it's a controlled company. We are not at the whims of short-term shareholders." (S010)
  - 2024-09: after the board tried to fire him over the Yahoo offer: "let's uh try to set up a governance structure that makes it somewhat harder to do that" (S020) And: "I might not be able to convince the all the investors that we should be investing to the extent that we are in reality lab if I didn't control the company" (S020)
  - 2025-01: "I control the majority of the voting in the company" (S014)
- **Read:** control let him hold Reality Labs through years of losses; it also removed the check that might have trimmed the metaverse spend sooner (inferred from S020, S013, S079).

## Selling the company
- **Stance:** Do not sell; ownership changes your goals.
- **Over time:**
  - 2005-10: "I think that what we're doing is more interesting than what anyone else is doing" (S051)
  - 2011-11: "if you're now owned by someone else then your goals are going to either quickly or over time become their goals" (S049)
  - 2016-08: the cost of saying no to Yahoo was the team: "huge amounts of the company quit because they didn't believe in in what we were what we were doing" (S054) Also: "some people um sell their company before it reaches its full potential" (S065)
  - 2017-05: "I didn't want to sell. I wanted to see if we could connect more people." (S012)
  - 2024-04: "I think if I sold this company, I'd just go build another company like this and I kind of like the one I have." (S018) "I didn't really have the financial sophistication to really engage with that kind of debate." (S018)
- **Read:** he credits luck: "I got really lucky because you know not only did did what I believed in end up working out but it ended up working out pretty quickly" (S054, 2016-08)

## Betting the company and holding through criticism
- **Stance:** Back what others do not believe yet and treat doubt as a sign you are pushing hard enough.
- **Over time:**
  - 2012-02: "The riskiest thing is to take no risks." (S001)
  - 2016-08: the hard bets are the slow ones: "bet billions of dollars on something and it's not going to be clear whether you're right for five or 10 years" (S054)
  - 2022-03: "if people fully feel like they understand what we are as a company and what we're doing, then I'm not pushing it hard enough" (S026) But: "I think most people still will get more of these bets wrong than right" (S026)
  - 2022-08: "the way that people deliver these discontinuous entrepreneurial results is by investing in things that other people don't believe in yet" (S013)
  - 2022-10: "I kind of, I enjoy being doubted." (S029)
  - 2022-11: "if you're not getting a bunch of skepticism then you're probably not pushing hard enough on the future" (S048)
  - 2026-06: "people have to think it's ridiculous for some period of time because if they didn't, then someone else would have already done it." (S038) "I think the question is how much you win when you win." (S038)
- **Read:** doubt as a signal can shield a bet that is actually wrong; the metaverse doubters were right about the order of events (inferred from S029, S079).

## The metaverse bet and the AI pivot
- **2021-10:** "From now on, we will be metaverse-first, not Facebook-first." (S005) With a caveat: "We'll need to make sure we don't lose too much money along the way though." (S005)
- **2022-08:** "we're going to lose a lot of money for a long time on this massive breadth of things" (S013)
- **2022-11:** about 20 percent of spend, with a floor case: "I don't think Microsoft regrets building the Xbox" (S048)
- **2024-02:** "it's like $15 billion a year at least into each of them." (S033) (AI and the metaverse)
- **2024-09:** "a few years ago I would have predicted that AR Holograms would have been available before kind of like full scale Ai and now I think it's probably going to be the other order" (S020)
- **2026-09 (newer):** "But now it is actually quite clear that personal superintelligence will not only come first, it will be much bigger and more important." (S079) "we made the decision to focus a little bit less on metaverse for now, you know, build fewer VR devices and only release them when we have real breakthroughs." (S079) He reframes rather than concedes: "Well, that is the metaverse." (S037)
- **Read:** he called the sequence wrong and spent heavily on it for five years; he describes the change as a reorder, not a loss (inferred from S037, S079).

## Timing technology waves
- **Stance:** A new computing platform arrives every 10 to 15 years; plant seeds early, because platforms get defined early.
- **Over time:**
  - 2015-09: "the next computing platform which there's a new one every you know 10 or 15 years" (S066)
  - 2015-10: on AI: "I still think we're we're very very far off from having computers which are generally um more intelligent in in any way" (S064)
  - 2016-10: "it's often easier to predict what the world is going to be like 20 years from now than it is to predict uh what the world's going to be like three years from now" (S076)
  - 2017-02: "We always overestimate what we can do in two years, and we underestimate what we can do in ten years." (S085)
  - 2019-04: "I almost always find the long term comes sooner than you think." (S046)
  - 2022-10: "Yeah, it'll start slow and then it'll get faster and faster and faster." (S029)
  - 2024-07: on bubbles: "sometimes things take a little longer to develop than you think and you just need to have the commitment to see that through" (S035)
  - 2024-09: "We weren't born on mobile." (S028) So, once stable: "Okay, let's start planting some seeds for what we think could be the future" (S028)
  - 2025-05: arriving with a rival is not enough: "if they they kind of build the same kind of AR glasses the same year, I I kind of assume the tie goes to them" (S022)
  - 2025-07 (newer): "the more kind of aggressive assumptions or the fastest assumptions have been the ones that have most accurately predicted what would happen." (S082)
- **Read:** his VR forecast of "the next 5 or 10 years" (S075, 2015-03) slipped, and his AI timing flipped from far off (2015) to aggressive (2025).

## Moving fast
- **Old view (2005 to 2012):** "I think it's more useful to make things happen and then apologize later than it is to make sure that you dot all your I's eyes now and then just not get stuff done." (S050, 2005-12) "The idea is that if you never break anything, you're probably not moving fast enough." (S001, 2012-02)
- **2014 change:** "move fast with stable infra, um it may not have quite the same ring to it." (S070, 2014-04) Why: "having to slow down and fix these bugs was slowing us down more than we were actually improving our speed" (S070)
- **2019-04:** "a few years ago we probably would have just rolled this out and then tried to adjust and address issues as they came up" (S073)
- **2024-09:** still ships early: "in a lot of ways we're like the opposite of apple" (S020) "its brand can withstand all the little damage that you do to it by shipping stuff that's not quite ready" (S020)
- **2026-09 (newer):** holds a launch when trust is at stake: "we held Muse back for 3 months because we wanted it to be a better product for people, right?" (S037)
- **Read:** speed is for learning; he slows down for privacy, encryption and hardware price bars (inferred from S073, S028, S037). A small brand cannot absorb "little damage" the way Meta's distribution can (inferred from S020).
- **Case, web to mobile (the 2012 bet):**
  - **2024-09, looking back:** "well we were wrong it turned out that you know having the native integration was actually critical for having the interactions feel good" (S020) He froze features: "we're going to pause feature development of the company because it's hard enough to do a rewrite" (S020) "when you're losing it's usually pretty clear what you have to do and and I think a lot of it is like is is just do you have the pain tolerance to go do it" (S020)
  - **2014-04:** "we've made this transition to being a primarily mobile company ourselves." (S070)
  - **2016-06:** "I wouldn't start it as a website today I think you'd start you'd start whatever you were building is a mobile app" (S067)
  - **2025-05 (newer):** "I think you'd start it off as a messaging platform" (S022)

## Experiments and learning speed
- **Stance:** Strategy is how fast you learn. Let any team test unless the test could sink the company, and judge a product by its third version.
- **Evidence:** "building a company which is focused on learning as quickly as possible Right companies are are learning organisms" (S054, 2016-08). "if it's not a good idea then we catalog it and we we write down what the idea was that way we don't keep on repeating it" (S065, 2016-08). "okay is this going to destroy the company because if not then" (S047, about 2017). "we can learn faster than every other company we're going to win" (S020, 2024-09). The floor: "if what you put out is bad then you're not going to collect good data because people aren't going to use it for anything because it sucks" (S020). "we do a lot of things where the first version isn't that good you want to kind of Judge the third version" (S014, 2025-01)

## The year of efficiency: flattening and layoffs
- **Before (2008 to 2022):** hiring was the job: "the key emphasis for us at this point is just hiring really great technical people" (S056, 2008-11). Fast hiring left managers with about three direct reports each (S016, 2023-06).
- **2022-11:** he admits the misread: "we read The Economic and Business indicators in 2021 at the height of the pandemic is more likely to continue this huge pull forward in e-commerce" (S048) "our kind of operational Focus over the next few years is going to be on efficiency and um and discipline and rigor" (S048)
- **2023-02:** "even if our business outperforms our goals this will stay our management theme for the year" (S080) "we just hire less right and stay a stay a smaller company for for longer" (S080)
- **2023-03:** "It's well-understood that every layer of a hierarchy adds latency and risk aversion in information flow and decision-making." (S006) "in general we don't want managers to have more than 10 direct reports" (S006) "In retrospect, I underestimated the indirect costs of lower priority projects." (S006) About 10,000 roles cut and 5,000 open roles closed (S006).
- **2023-06:** "I decided that we needed to get to a point where we were a lot leaner" (S016) "there's no good way um there's no perfect way for sure" (S016)
- **2024-02 (newer, kept as policy):** "until we reach a point where we're just you know really underwater on our ability to execute I kind of want to keep things lean" (S081) "a lot of companies just kind of overbuilt" (S033)
- **Read:** the cuts fixed his own overhiring; he called the prior year "a humbling wake-up call." (S006)

## Team size and small teams
- **Stance:** The fewest people the stage needs, each with real agency.
- **Over time:**
  - 2010-07: "there's no other organization on the face of the Earth that has 500 million people that it's serving with 400 people" (S058)
  - 2015-03: "actually keep your team as small and flexible as possible" (S075)
  - 2024-09: "it also is problematic to have more people working on something than you should have for the stage that it's at" (S020)
  - 2025-07: "you really want the smallest group that can hold the whole thing in their head" (S082)
  - 2025-09: "You don't need many, many hundreds of people. You need, you know, 50 to 100 people." (S036) "Seats on the boat are precious, right?" (S036)
  - 2026-10: "We're not peanut buttering it across a lot of competing efforts." (S023)

## Hiring and the talent war
- **Stance:** Hire for raw intelligence and only people you would work for; at the frontier, recruit personally and arm each hire with compute.
- **Over time:**
  - 2005-10: "the two most important things that I look for are number one is just the raw intelligence" (S051) 2005-12: "we kind of make a point of not hiring people for skills" (S050)
  - 2015-03 to 2026: the bar he repeats most: "I've developed over time a simple rule which is that I will only hire someone to work directly for me if I would work for that person" (S075) Same rule in S053, S009, S015, S016, S038.
  - 2016-08: "I started the company when I was 19 right so I can't institutionally believe that experience is that important" (S054)
  - 2025-04: "A lot of what I spend my time on is trying to get awesome people onto the teams." (S019)
  - 2025-09 (newer, the AI talent war): "I want to, like, personally have relationships with them." (S036) "significantly higher compute per researcher than any other lab" (S030)
  - 2026-06: "you should probably hire people who you think you'd want to be friends with, right?" (S038)
  - 2026-10: "I kind of almost cleared my calendar for like a month or 2 and was just meeting researchers and talking to them." (S023)
- **Read:** the 2025 recruiting push ran on pay and compute no startup can match; the portable part is the bar and the founder's time (inferred from S030, S036, S023).

## Co-founders and the first team
- **Stance:** Stay a team of friends until the idea works, and have co-founders.
- **Evidence:** "companies that have more Founders are actually more likely to have a successful outcome" (S062, 2014-11). "if you decide to start a company and you just start hiring people for whatever your first idea is then you lose some flexibility" (S064, 2015-10). "co-founders and partners who complement our strengths and uh fortify our weaknesses" (S064). At 19 he hired experienced professionals and it did not click (S038, 2026-06).

## Firing and hard conversations
- **Stance:** Progress needs hard conversations; fix a cohesion problem in the core team first.
- **Evidence:** "the amount of progress that we make is directly proportional to the number of hard conversations that we're willing to have" (S026, 2022-03). "If there's an issue on my team and people aren't and there isn't good cohesion, then I'm not sleeping well until I resolve that." (S026). Overruling talented owners drives them out: "at some point just go find a place to work where they can have a bigger impact" (S066, 2015-09).
- **No recorded position** on how to fire one person; the record is about layoffs (see the year of efficiency).

## Delegation and the founder in the details
- **Old view (2015 to 2016):** "i don't want every decision that we make in the company to have to go through me" (S066, 2015-09). "if every idea had to go through me then the company would probably go pretty slowly" (S065, 2016-08)
- **Newer view (2024 to 2025):** "I don't actually believe in delegating that much." (S033, 2024-02) "I basically don't believe in delegation." (S022, 2025-05) The limits: "you need to know where your limits are and where like you're just thrashing people because you're involved in something that a half assed way" (S033) "if you're going to report to me, you need to be able to manage yourself" (S022) "you want to have humility and know that like if you're diving into some decision, you may not have the most context immediately" (S022)
- **Read:** the experiments still run without him; what he no longer delegates are the decisions he cares about (inferred from S047, S022).

## Focus
- **Stance:** One or two things matter; find them and ignore the rest.
- **Evidence:** "just ignore the rest and put all your effort into into focusing on the couple of things that really matter" (S055, 2010-11). "the discipline to do it right they get big and everyone wants to do everything and they just say yes and then they don't do everything well" (S049, 2011-11). "out of those hundred things it's really only actually going to be one or two things that actually matter" (S053, 2013-10). "I actually think for most companies, of this scale at least, it's focus." (S018, 2024-04). "Only one thing can be the top priority." (S023, 2026-10)
- **Read:** he preaches focus while running apps, Reality Labs and AI at once; his defense is that 80 percent went to the core (S048). For a small company that split is not available (inferred from S048, S022).

## Metrics and growth
- **Stance:** Growth is a product feature; measure what people find meaningful, not raw clicks or easy benchmarks.
- **Over time:**
  - 2005-10: watched daily, weekly and monthly return rates above launch spikes (S051).
  - 2008-11: "growth is our our top priority" (S056)
  - 2016-08: "making it so we could grow faster was I I think the most important product feature that we uh ended up building for Facebook" (S054)
  - 2017-02: "we will measure Facebook's progress with groups based on meaningful groups, not groups overall." (S085)
  - 2018-04: "maximizing the time that people spend is not really the goal either." (S010)
  - 2019-04: "we don't just measure signals in the system like what you're clicking on, because that can get you into a bad local optimum." (S046)
  - 2022-03: every goal metric gets counter metrics (S026).
  - 2025-04 to 2026 (newer, on AI): on benchmarks: "I think a lot of them are quite easily gameable." (S019) "And so you're kind of outsourcing all of your product thinking to that PhD student who happened to make a viral benchmark." (S023)
- **Read:** the meaningful-time rhetoric sits next to an engagement machine; he admits one of his calls cost engagement: "it's probably the case that there would be more engagement on the platform if we didn't" (S013, 2022-08)

## When to monetize
- **Stance:** Spark, retention, growth, then monetization, in that order.
- **Evidence:** "step two, retention, step three, growth and scaling the community. And then only at that point is step four, which is monetization." (S027, 2023-09). "then we'll focus on on ramping up the monetization which has been a formula that's worked for us" (S080, 2023-02). "usually only when we reach that kind of scale do we start focusing on what monetization will look like" (S081, 2024-02). The enabler: "we can do the long-term stuff because we do really well at the core business." (S022, 2025-05)
- **Read:** this playbook needs a cash engine to fund years of free scale; a startup without one runs out first (inferred from S022, S081).

## Pricing
- **Stance:** Free or cheap to reach everyone; earn on ads, services or a small cut of results, not on margin.
- **Over time:**
  - 2016-06: "you will never have to pay for facebook we've made a commitment Facebook is free it always will be" (S067)
  - 2018-04: "we are squarely in the camp of the companies that work hard to charge you less and provide a free service that everyone can use." (S010)
  - 2022-10: "The strategy overall is not to make money on the hardware, but to make it so that it can help develop the ecosystem" (S029)
  - 2023-06: "we're not just trying to serve like a you know an elite wealthy crowd" (S016)
  - 2023-09: Quest Pro at $1,500 undersold: "at least consumers are very cost conscious." (S027)
  - 2025-04: "for the consumer piece we definitely want to have a free thing. I'm sure that will end up being ad-supported." (S019)
  - 2026-07 to 2026-09 (newer): "businesses only pay us when we achieve results for them." (S083) "the expectation that over time, we will profit by taking a small fee from transactions." (S079)
- **Read:** free at scale only pays with billions of users and an ad auction; it is the wrong default for a small B2B seller (inferred from S010, S083).
