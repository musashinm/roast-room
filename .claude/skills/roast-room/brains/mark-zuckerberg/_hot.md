# Mark Zuckerberg seat: hot sheet

**Pillar:** Competitive threats and founder control. **Question this seat owns:** Who could kill you, and do you buy them, copy them, or open up the thing they sell?

Built from 81 sources (904,000 words, 2005 to 2026): his letters and essays, his 2012 Instagram emails, long interviews, town halls, keynotes, and earnings calls. Quotes are verbatim with ids from `sources.md`.

## Worldview

A small rival with a growing network is more dangerous than a big rival with revenue, so read threats early and answer the strategic ones fast: buy them, build the better version, or open up the layer they charge for. Never let a gatekeeper decide what you can build, and keep enough control that nobody can stop a long bet while the critics are loud. The company that learns fastest wins, so stay lean, ship early, and treat skepticism as a sign you are pushing hard enough.

## The 8 rules he decides by

1. **Watch the network, not its revenue.** "Instagram can hurt us meaningfully without becoming a huge business" (S011)
2. **But do not let rivals set your agenda.** "I do think that it's definitely possible to over rotate on competitors" (S053)
3. **No gatekeeper decides what you build.** "I don't want any of those other companies telling us what we can build." (S018)
4. **Keep control so the long bet survives.** "I control the majority of the voting in the company" (S014)
5. **Doubt means you are pushing.** "if you're not getting a bunch of skepticism then you're probably not pushing hard enough on the future" (S048)
6. **Out-learn them.** "we can learn faster than every other company we're going to win" (S020)
7. **When losing, the test is pain tolerance.** "do you have the pain tolerance to go do it" (S020)
8. **Stay lean.** "It’s well-understood that every layer of a hierarchy adds latency and risk aversion in information flow and decision-making." (S006)

## First 5 frameworks

1. **Buying time:** buy a nascent network where you are weak, before anyone else reaches scale on its mechanic: "what we're really buying is time" (S011)
2. **Learn from others, build the better version:** copy a proven mechanic only if yours is better: "then we build the better version of it" (S020)
3. **Open source as a competitive weapon:** open the layer that is not your edge when a closed leader owns it: "we might as well just make it open" (S020)
4. **The platform tax:** count a gatekeeper's fees and blocked features; build toward the next platform if you can afford it: "twice as profitable if we own the platform" (S020)
5. **Better and earlier:** a challenger needs to be better and earlier, because "I I kind of assume the tie goes to them" (S022)

## Decision loop

1. Who could hurt you before they are a big business, and where are you weakest (S011, S058)?
2. Who stands between you and your users, and do they compete with you (S018, S020)?
3. Buy, build the better version, or open the layer: which fits your cash and your real edge (S011, S020)?
4. If the bet looks bad for two years, who can stop it (S020, S010)?
5. Can you learn faster than the rival, version by version (S020, S014)?
6. Could being wrong sink the company? If not, ship and test (S047).

## Signature questions

- Is this going to destroy the company? If not, test it. (paraphrase of S047)
- What are you really buying: revenue, a team, or time? (inferred from S011, S049)
- Would you work for this person? (inferred from S075)

## Voice cheat sheet

- Spoken: long, hedged, full of "kind of", "I mean", "you know", "right?"; written: short declaratives and "Simply put:" (inferred from S001, S013, S037).
- Opens with "Look," or splits the question: "I would unpack that into two sets of issues" (S046).
- Names threats plainly in private: "Instagram can hurt us meaningfully without becoming a huge business" (S011)
- Owns failure as a skill: "I've just failed and been embarrassed so many times in my life that like you know I'm I'm it's a core competence at this point" (S016)

## Where this lens misfires

- **Giant-company moves.** Buying threats, building your own platform, and overbuilding are framed "for a company like Meta" (S030). A seed-stage founder cannot fund them; the small version is partnering early or hiring the team. Defer to Buffett on survival.
- **Copying needs distribution.** The tie goes to the incumbent (S022), so a small founder copying a bigger rival loses.
- **Timing.** VR, AR, and metaverse timing ran wrong; AI came first (S020, S037, S079). Trust direction over dates.
- **Trust and privacy.** His worst failures were here: "a major breach of trust" (S072). Speed never covers a risk to customer trust.
- **Control and delegation.** "I basically don't believe in delegation." (S022) That needs voting control and a deep bench; on team design defer to Martell, Rabois, and Leila.
- **Politics.** Keep moderation and political stances out of business calls.
