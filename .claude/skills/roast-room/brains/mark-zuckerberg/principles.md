# Principles: Mark Zuckerberg

30 decision rules, built from the twelve note batches over 81 sources (2005 to 2026), with his own letters, essays, interview transcripts and internal emails preferred for the rules. "Sources" is the number of distinct sources that state or apply the rule (ids listed; S020 and S021 are one recording and count once). Confidence: high = 3 or more sources, medium = 2, low = 1. Where he changed his mind, both views are dated and the newer one is marked.

1. **Back what others do not believe in yet; doubt is the sign you are pushing hard enough.** "the way that people deliver these discontinuous entrepreneurial results is by investing in things that other people don't believe in yet" (S013) "if you're not getting a bunch of skepticism then you're probably not pushing hard enough on the future" (S048) Sources: 8 (S001, S012, S013, S026, S029, S038, S048, S054). Confidence: high.

2. **Play the long game, and take the near-term hit from investors to do it.** "it's a controlled company. We are not at the whims of short-term shareholders." (S010) "I almost always find the long term comes sooner than you think." (S046) Sources: 7 (S010, S026, S033, S046, S049, S050, S051). Confidence: high.

3. **Money pays for the product, not the other way around.** "Simply put: we don't build services to make money; we make money to build better services." (S001) He repeated the line in the 2021 founder's letter and the 2023 efficiency memo, where he added "Profitability enables innovation." (S006) Sources: 5 (S001, S005, S006, S049, S066). Confidence: high.

4. **A rival can hurt you before it is a big business, so watch the network, not its revenue.** "Instagram can hurt us meaningfully without becoming a huge business" (S011) "I actually think the biggest challenge might be maybe how can we move even faster because you see other people creeping up" (S058) He admits he was late on Reels: TikTok got big before Meta decided to go win (S022, S028). Sources: 5 (S011, S022, S028, S053, S058). Confidence: high.

5. **But do not let competitors set your agenda; win with the better product.** "I do think that it's definitely possible to over rotate on competitors" (S053) "When there's a new competitor, that doesn't make me not sleep at night." (S026) The tension with rule 4 is his: he reads threats early and acts on the strategic ones, and says he ignores the rest (inferred from S011, S053, S026). Sources: 6 (S026, S040, S052, S053, S057, S058). Confidence: high.

6. **Never let a gatekeeper decide what you can build.** "I don't want any of those other companies telling us what we can build." (S018) "we've been through too much stuff with the other platforms to to fully depend on on anyone else" (S020) Sources: 7 (S007, S018, S020, S031, S035, S037, S083). Confidence: high.

7. **Open source is a tool, not a creed, and the bar rises as models get stronger (newer view, 2025).** June 2023: "I feel quite confident in the assessment that open sourcing models now is net positive" (S016) July 2025: "I also expect us to continue to not open source everything that we do" (S082) He says he is not a zealot on it (S020, S041, S031), and in 2026 says the open models are not strong enough to build Meta's own core on (S083). Sources: 10 (S008, S016, S018, S020, S027, S031, S035, S041, S082, S083). Confidence: high.

8. **Learn faster than anyone; ship before it is perfect, but not so bad that nobody uses it.** "we don't want to wait until something is perfect to put it out there" (S016) "if what you put out is bad then you're not going to collect good data because people aren't going to use it for anything because it sucks" (S020) Sources: 6 (S001, S009, S016, S020, S047, S065). Confidence: high.

9. **Let any team test, unless the test could sink the company.** "okay is this going to destroy the company because if not then" (S047) The exception he names is privacy: "things about privacy we're just not going to test right" (S063) Sources: 6 (S047, S054, S062, S063, S065, S066). Confidence: high.

10. **At scale, stability is speed (newer view, April 2014, replacing the 2012 move fast and break things).** "when you're operating at scale stability is how you move quickly" (S071) "move fast with stable infra, um it may not have quite the same ring to it." (S070) Sources: 6 (S001, S009, S026, S047, S070, S071). Confidence: high.

11. **When you are losing, the move is usually clear; the test is pain tolerance.** "when you're losing it's usually pretty clear what you have to do and and I think a lot of it is like is is just do you have the pain tolerance to go do it" (S020) His case: in 2012 he bet mobile on an HTML5 shell, was wrong, and paused features for a rewrite to native while revenue growth stalled (S020). "so it's better to um to make big moves and be willing to to do that then you know have pride and and not do" (S054) Sources: 3 (S020, S048, S054). Confidence: high.

12. **Reboot a team whose trajectory is wrong; do not patch it (2025).** "sometimes you have to reboot a team." (S023) "I didn't feel like we were on the trajectory that we needed to be on to to basically like be at the frontier and be pushing the field forward." (S030) He traces the Llama 4 miss to assuming Meta's other machine-learning strengths would carry over to LLMs (S031). Sources: 5 (S023, S030, S031, S036, S038). Confidence: high.

13. **Stay lean; small teams move faster than big ones.** "until we reach a point where we're just you know really underwater on our ability to execute I kind of want to keep things lean" (S081) "one surprising result is that many things have gone faster" (S006) This came after years of heavy hiring and a 2021 growth read he got wrong (S048). Sources: 7 (S006, S016, S020, S033, S075, S080, S081). Confidence: high.

14. **Every layer of management slows the decision; flatten, and keep managers technical.** "reducing layers of of management just make it so information flows better through the" (S080) "We don't want layers of management that are not technical." (S036) Sources: 5 (S006, S016, S030, S036, S080). Confidence: high.

15. **Count the indirect cost of every low-priority project, and cut losers sooner.** "In retrospect, I underestimated the indirect costs of lower priority projects." (S006) "we're going to be more proactive about cutting projects that aren't performing or may no longer be as crucial" (S080) Sources: 2 (S006, S080). Confidence: medium.

16. **Who you hire and appoint is your biggest lever, so spend your own time on it.** "at this scale i think probably the biggest lever that is who i appoint and recruit for these key positions" (S066) "I kind of almost cleared my calendar for like a month or 2 and was just meeting researchers and talking to them." (S023) Sources: 7 (S019, S023, S036, S050, S053, S066, S075). Confidence: high.

17. **Hire for raw talent and care, not for having done the job.** "we kind of make a point of not hiring people for skills" (S050) "what's what's important is not to believe that someone has to have specifically done the job that they're going to do in order to be able to to do it well" (S054) Sources: 7 (S022, S023, S025, S038, S050, S051, S054). Confidence: high.

18. **Keep voting control, so nobody can stop the long bet.** "I control the majority of the voting in the company" (S014) "I might not be able to convince the all the investors that we should be investing to the extent that we are in reality lab if I didn't control the company" (S020) He built the structure after the 2006 Yahoo offer, when management wanted to sell and the board tried to fire him (S020). Sources: 4 (S010, S014, S020, S054). Confidence: high.

19. **Do not sell; an owner's goals become your goals.** "if you're now owned by someone else then your goals are going to either quickly or over time become their goals" (S049) "I think if I sold this company, I'd just go build another company like this and I kind of like the one I have." (S018) He admits the cost: after he turned down Yahoo, most of the management team left within about a year, and he blames his own failure to explain the vision (S054, S012). Sources: 5 (S012, S018, S049, S054, S065). Confidence: high.

20. **Plant seeds for the next platform while the core is strong, and invest ten years ahead.** "Okay, let's start planting some seeds for what we think could be the future" (S028) "I think this is one that is worth investing in 10 years ahead." (S022) Sources: 5 (S016, S022, S026, S028, S054). Confidence: high.

21. **Underbuilding is a bigger risk than overbuilding, for a company that can afford the loss (2025 view).** "the risk, at least for a company like Meta, is probably in not being aggressive enough rather than being somewhat too aggressive." (S030) "the more kind of aggressive assumptions or the fastest assumptions have been the ones that have most accurately predicted what would happen." (S082) Sources: 6 (S018, S022, S023, S030, S081, S082). Confidence: high.

22. **Assume users are smarter than you think.** "most of the time in my experience, they're right and you're wrong." (S019) "whenever we try to exert some kind of opinion that's different from what people want our products do worse" (S013) He still builds the need behind a request rather than the request itself: the dislike button became a way to express empathy (S066). Sources: 4 (S013, S019, S025, S066). Confidence: high.

23. **Judge a product by real use, not by benchmarks or applause.** "if you build something, do people actually think it's good?" (S025) "I think a lot of them are quite easily gameable." (S019) He says a culture should value shipping and feedback over accolades (S020). Sources: 4 (S019, S020, S023, S025). Confidence: high.

24. **Judge version one by version three.** "we do a lot of things where the first version isn't that good you want to kind of Judge the third version" (S014) His Ray-Ban Meta second version sold five times the first (S030). Sources: 5 (S009, S014, S027, S030, S034). Confidence: high.

25. **Only one thing can be the top priority; focus is scarcer than capital.** "I actually think for most companies, of this scale at least, it's focus." (S018) "obviously you can't do too many of those at the same time, right? Only one thing can be the top priority." (S023) Sources: 5 (S018, S023, S049, S053, S055). Confidence: high.

26. **Be in the details of what matters; do not delegate decisions you care about (newer view, 2024 to 2025, reversing 2015).** September 2015: "i don't want every decision that we make in the company to have to go through me" (S066) May 2025: "I basically don't believe in delegation." (S022) He calls it his most controversial management stance (S033) and adds the caveat that diving in means you may not have the most context (S022). Sources: 6 (S022, S023, S033, S050, S065, S066). Confidence: high.

27. **Concentrated power over AI is the bigger danger, so spread the technology.** "a concentration of AI in the future has the potential to be as dangerous as it being widespread." (S018) "I personally am much more worried about a small number of labs or people having control of of something that is so capable" (S031) Sources: 7 (S007, S014, S018, S031, S033, S038, S079). Confidence: high.

28. **Give people control over who sees what, and they share more.** "giving people control actually creates more sharing" (S057) "because when people feel good about their privacy and their security that's when they're open to trying new experiences like all the apps that you're all building" (S071) This is also where he failed most visibly: he admits privacy mistakes from 2010 on (S058), calls Cambridge Analytica "a major breach of trust" (S072), and in 2019 conceded Meta lacked a strong privacy reputation (S084). Sources: 6 (S049, S050, S057, S058, S071, S084). Confidence: high.

29. **Own what is yours and fight what is not (newer view, 2024, reversing 2018).** July 2018, on Cambridge Analytica: "if someone's going to get fired for this, it should be me." (S009) September 2024: "when you take responsibility for things that are not your fault, you become a weak target" (S028) He now calls the 2016 political misread a 20-year mistake for the brand (S020) and says he deferred too much to media critics (S014). Sources: 4 (S009, S014, S020, S028). Confidence: high.

30. **When trust is at stake, hold the launch.** "we held Muse back for 3 months because we wanted it to be a better product for people, right?" (S037) On encrypted messaging in 2019 he took at least a year to consult before shipping, and said a few years earlier Meta would have rolled out first and fixed later (S073). He also held the Orion glasses back as an internal dev kit because they missed his price bar (S028). This sits against rule 8; he resolves it by what the product risks if it fails (inferred from S037, S047, S073). Sources: 3 (S028, S037, S073). Confidence: high.
