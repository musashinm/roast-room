# Stories: Seth Godin

20 stories and cases he reuses, with the lesson he draws, built from the 12 note batches over S001 to S089. Most-used first; source ids in parentheses.

1. **800 rejection letters as a book packager (1986 on).** After selling his first book he got hundreds of rejections in a row, then learned he was pitching what he wanted, not what an editor could defend to her boss, and went on to package about 120 books. "I got 800 rejection letters in a row over the course of a year" (S027) Lesson: sell to the buyer's story, and let the buyer fund the work (S002, S005, S010, S011, S013, S021, S027, S075, S082).
2. **Yoyodyne: bootstrapped, capped, sold.** His permission email company stayed profitable every month against a rival that raised $70M, hired through group interviews, and stopped growing at about 70 people before selling to Yahoo in 1999. Lesson: he is not a scale builder, and a sale changes a company run like a family (S004, S014, S021, S033, S035, S041).
3. **Yahoo and Google.** While he was at Yahoo it could have bought Google for about $10 million and spent on Yahoo Kids instead; Yahoo's strategy was to keep you on the site, Google's was to send you away. Lesson: two opposed strategies cannot merge, and a company that will not change its game fades (S007, S008, S024, S027, S037, S043).
4. **Squidoo and the Google switch.** He built the 40th biggest US site with eight or nine people; Google turned off its traffic overnight, he spent two months deciding it was a situation, not a problem, then paid severance and closed. "throwing away another year and a half of your life because some selfish person at Google turned you off and lied about it, that's not doing anybody any good." (S025) Lesson: name a situation and stop fighting it (S004, S005, S006, S025).
5. **Survival Is Not Enough, then Purple Cow in a milk carton.** He skipped a Permission Marketing sequel (and Mailchimp), wrote a long book for himself that flopped around 9/11, then self-published Purple Cow in a milk carton, about 5,000 copies, which spread to more than a million because readers gained status by handing it to the boss. Lesson: write for a small set of readers who will tell others (S011, S012, S013, S019, S025, S035).
6. **Momofuku and the Brussels sprouts.** He asked for the sprouts without meat; the chef told him a vegetarian place was a few doors down. Lesson: the day it refused him is the day it stood for something (S019, S024, S046).
7. **Netflix kept the DVD people out of the room; Kodak did not.** Netflix built streaming with a group that excluded the division earning all the profit, while Kodak held digital patents and could not shrink film. Lesson: protect the new strategy from the people defending the old one (S008, S018, S034, S043).
8. **The contractor with the clipboard.** An HVAC or boiler technician took off his shoes and handed him a list of 25 to 40 neighbors who would take reference calls; Seth hired him and cancelled the other bids. A waterproofing firm and a pool installer won the same way by publishing every trade secret. Lesson: specific, generous proof beats being cheapest, and is worth more than an ad (S011, S029, S045, S046).
9. **Supreme and the line.** People who already own shirts queue for $90 to $120 ones that are never advertised. Lesson: the product is the story and the scarcity; the line is a tax that creates value (S003, S012, S044, S046).
10. **The Paris bakery with one bread.** Poilane (and his friend Apollonia's family bakery) sells a sourdough from flour, water, salt and starter, with a line around the corner and fans flying in from Japan; it will not sell you a baguette. Lesson: be the best in the world at one specific thing and say no to the rest (S011, S012, S028).
11. **Facebook on one campus.** It began at Harvard with a message that people were talking about you, then spread school by school. Lesson: start with a tight group and a status or fear trigger, and grow step by organic step (S008, S036, S040).
12. **altMBA designed against every online course.** No video of him, an application, a real price; after months of prototyping it got about 97% completion. Lesson: barriers that pick the right people make them tell others; if everyone likes the prototype it is probably banal (S003, S034, S049, S068).
13. **Little Miss Matched odd socks.** Socks sold only in odd numbers to 12-year-old girls with a fashion problem, so they showed them to each other, and the company built its own audience. Lesson: build the talking point into the product and choose a very specific buyer (S031, S049, S081).
14. **The fax machine.** One is useless, a thousand are vital, so every owner recruits the next. Lesson: the best spread comes from products that work better when your peers have them (S034, S039, S078).
15. **Jackson Pollock refused to be his own cover band.** At the peak he changed style, the new work did not sell, and he spiralled. Lesson: you cannot have the frontier and the safe audience at once; pick (S009, S013, S043, S047).
16. **Danny Meyer's crinkle fries and tipping.** Shake Shack switched to fresh-cut fries to please critics, then went back and profits rose; regulars revolted when he tried to drop tipping because it gave them status. Lesson: serve your people, ignore those you do not serve, and know what status the old habit gave them (S009, S011, S024).
17. **Firing the biggest client.** A client worth a third of revenue sent a lawyer to every meeting; he fired it and the team of nine replaced the business plus extra within 60 days. Lesson: the wrong customer costs more than the revenue (S042).
18. **Hallmark ornaments and Prodigy email.** Hallmark sold $10 collectible ornaments in July to heavy users who had given their names, for $100 million in sales; his own Prodigy game emails got 86% opens, and sponsors asking how to repeat it led to Permission Marketing. Lesson: a list of people who want to hear from you is an asset you own (S021, S031).
19. **Yahoo auctions lost to eBay.** Yahoo had better features and still lost. "eBay had something going for it that Yahoo couldn't get and it had absolutely nothing to do with technology" (S031) Lesson: the network and the story beat the feature list (S031, S078).
20. **The Harley check and the weed killer frisbees.** A buyer sweats for 30 seconds before writing an $18,000 check because the change creates tension; an organic lawn firm tossed frisbees on neighbors' lawns saying it was doing the house next door. Lesson: status and affiliation, not features, move the purchase (S039).
