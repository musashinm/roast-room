# Frameworks: Seth Godin

24 frameworks, roughly most-used first, built from the extraction notes on all 89 sources (podcasts, keynotes, interviews, and seths.blog posts, 2002 to 2026). "Sources" counts distinct sources where the idea is used (ids listed). Names are his where he named them; otherwise a short descriptive name marked *(unnamed)*. The How-to-score lines are scoring guidance for the council built from his stated thresholds and tests, not quotes.

---

## 1. Who's it for? What's it for?
- **What it is:** The two questions he asks before any project, product, post, meeting, or hour of work. "Who" means what they believe, dream of, and fear, not their names or demographics. "What" means the change you seek to make in them. He calls this intentional action, or design thinking in two questions.
- **Parts:** Who exactly is it for (beliefs, dreams, fears); what change is it meant to make; what you get out of it; how you will know it is working.
- **When to use:** At the start of every decision, and again whenever a plan feels muddled. When a founder asks "how do I get the word out", send them back here first.
- **How to score:** 5 if the founder can name a specific group by belief and a specific change, and can say who it is not for; 3 if the group is real but described by demographics or as "small businesses"; 1 if the answer is "everyone" or the question has not been asked. His own rule: if you cannot answer both specifically, start over.
- **Quote:** "The two questions are, who's it for and what's it for?" (S008) "If you can't answer those two questions very specifically, go back, rewind 30 seconds, and start over." (S014)
- **Sources:** 25 (S008, S009, S010, S013, S014, S015, S018, S022, S023, S025, S027, S032, S033, S034, S035, S038, S039, S041, S042, S046, S048, S051, S062, S064, S065)

## 2. Smallest viable audience
- **What it is:** Pick the smallest group that, if you changed things for them, would be enough. Delight them, forgive everyone else, and let them tell the others. A small, specific audience puts you on the hook, because they can say you broke a promise; a vague one gives you excuses.
- **Parts:** Name the minimum group that would be enough; check that the number matches what the work costs you; serve them so well they talk; accept that most people will never hear of you; grow from that pocket outward.
- **When to use:** Choosing a first market, a launch, a niche, a content plan, or deciding whether a project is worth doing at all.
- **How to score:** His sizes for "enough": 200 customers for an HVAC business (S004), 300 patients for a doctor (S011), 1,000 to 5,000 customers for a practice (S028), 1,000 backers at $200 for a Kickstarter product (S007). Score 5 if the founder has a named group of that kind of size and is serving it now; 3 if the group is named but too big to delight; 1 if the plan needs mass reach to work. He also calls aiming for both success and scale at once a mistake (S022).
- **Quote:** "If I can find that group, the smallest viable audience, and delight them, they will engage with me, and they will tell the others." (S003) "The smallest viable audience means you're on the hook" (S004)
- **Sources:** 24 (S002, S003, S004, S007, S009, S011, S013, S015, S017, S022, S023, S028, S029, S031, S033, S034, S036, S037, S039, S040, S046, S052, S054, S062)

## 3. Status, affiliation, freedom from fear *(the three things people want)*
- **What it is:** Once food and shelter are covered, people want three things: status (who is up and who is down), affiliation (who is to my left and right), and freedom from fear. Every story that sells delivers one of these. Fear sits under the other two, so when motivation is unclear, look for the fear. For business buyers he adds: avoid blame, claim credit, reduce uncertainty, which comes down to "what will I tell my boss?"
- **Parts:** Which of the three does this offer deliver; what status role is the buyer playing; are they acting for affiliation or dominance right now; what are they afraid of.
- **When to use:** Reading why a customer, employee, partner, or board member would say yes or no. Writing the story for a B2B champion.
- **How to score:** High if the offer clearly raises the buyer's status, lets them belong, or removes a fear they feel; low if the pitch is features and price only.
- **Quote:** "deep down people only want a few things. They want freedom from fear. They want status and they want affiliation. Those three things." (S011) "if you're in doubt as to motivation look for the fear." (S025)
- **Sources:** 19 (S007, S008, S009, S011, S015, S018, S019, S023, S024, S030, S033, S036, S039, S040, S043, S044, S053, S054, S055)

## 4. Tension, not stress
- **What it is:** Change only happens through tension, and marketers create it on purpose: the tension of being left out, left behind, or "this might work for me". Stress is wanting two things at once that cannot coexist. Tension is a pulled rubber band, and buying or acting relieves it. Products spread through tension, not enthusiasm.
- **Parts:** Name the gap between where people are and where they hope to go; create tension willingly (a start time, scarcity, status); make action the release; check the buyer will thank you afterwards.
- **When to use:** Launches, pricing a waiting list, any product that is good but nobody is acting on.
- **How to score:** High if there is a real reason to act now that the buyer will be glad of later (economic or social tension); low if the plan relies on people being enthusiastic. His ethics test: if the buyer would not thank you afterwards, it is manipulation.
- **Quote:** "Tension is at the heart of every art form and every innovation. Tension is it might not work." (S037) "What causes spreading isn't enthusiasm" (S056)
- **Sources:** 18 (S004, S007, S009, S010, S011, S012, S014, S017, S032, S037, S039, S040, S042, S045, S053, S054, S056, S057)

## 5. Permission, and trust over attention
- **What it is:** Earn the privilege of sending anticipated, personal, and relevant messages to people who want them. Attention without trust is worthless; trust with little attention is worth a lot. Over time permission becomes the company's core asset: own the platform and the list instead of renting eyeballs.
- **Parts:** A promise that earns a moment of attention; keeping it, which earns trust; trust, which earns permission; protecting the list and doing things for people, not to them.
- **When to use:** Choosing between paid reach and building an owned audience; any tactic that borrows or spends trust (pop-ups, scraped lists, fake urgency).
- **How to score:** 5 if the founder owns a list of people who would miss the messages; 3 if reach is mostly rented but trust is intact; 1 if growth depends on interrupting strangers or burns trust for attention. His email-era scoreboard: subscribers, and the share who opened the last message (S045).
- **Quote:** "Trust is way more powerful than attention. If you are burning trust to get attention, you are making a real mistake." (S023) "Over time, this permission becomes the core asset of the company." (S078)
- **Sources:** 15 (S012, S016, S019, S020, S022, S023, S029, S031, S045, S046, S047, S078, S081, S083, S086)

## 6. Remarkable (Purple Cow), with the spread engineered in
- **What it is:** Remarkable means worth making a remark about, and the person who touches it decides, not the maker. People talk because talking raises their own status or affiliation, so the reason to tell is designed into the product from the start (Carmine's: no table under six, huge garlic, double portions).
- **Parts:** Know in advance what you want people to say; build the line into the product; make it work better or look better when shared; give fans something to say; then let them tell each other.
- **When to use:** Any product that needs word of mouth, any plan that "needs marketing" to get noticed.
- **How to score:** High if the founder can say the sentence an early adopter will repeat and why repeating it helps them; low if spread depends on asking for favors or luck. His own launch test: what will an early adopter say about it (S062).
- **Quote:** "remarkable isn't a hack it's not a gimmick remarkable is simply a service that you offer to someone who is looking to increase their status or their affiliation and we'll use your idea to do so" (S027) "Most people just wait to get lucky. It's engineered in." (S011)
- **Sources:** 14 (S011, S012, S015, S016, S021, S027, S028, S031, S036, S037, S045, S046, S049, S053)

## 7. Price is a story, and the race to the top
- **What it is:** Price is not a number but a signal of who the product is for. Anything priced above the good-enough substitute is a luxury good, and the extra has to come from the story and the experience, not the cost to make it. You either race to the bottom (cheaper, faster, and you might win) or race to the top (you pay a lot and get more than you pay for).
- **Parts:** Know the substitutes, between markets and between competitors; ignore your cost when setting price; set a price norm that funds work worth talking about; ask the 10X question: if forced to raise prices ten times, what would you change?
- **When to use:** Pricing, discounting, competing with a bigger or cheaper player, deciding whether to cut price to win a deal.
- **How to score:** 5 if buyers pay a premium over the substitute and would not take the cheaper one; 3 if the price is defended by features; 1 if the plan is to be the cheapest. Low-income housing and mass health care are his only stated cases for lowering price (S003).
- **Quote:** "Price is a story. It is not an absolute number." (S003) "if you were forced to increase your prices by 10X, what would you do?" (S007)
- **Sources:** 14 (S003, S005, S007, S011, S016, S023, S026, S032, S033, S036, S044, S055, S069, S074)

## 8. False proxies
- **What it is:** A number that is easy to measure and worthless, or worse, one that pulls the company the wrong way. Likes, followers, bestseller rank, attendance, typing speed, interview skill, tokens burned. Platforms hand you the proxies that benefit them.
- **Parts:** Ask who gave you this feedback loop and who benefits; ask what problem you are actually trying to solve; measure what matters even when it is hard to count; block the rest.
- **When to use:** Setting metrics, reading a dashboard, hiring, judging whether content "works".
- **How to score:** High if the founder tracks something tied to the change they promised (referrals, retention, who did I help today); low if the main evidence is followers, views, or rank.
- **Quote:** "A false proxy is a number that's easy to measure and worthless." (S012) "who gave you the feedback loop you're using. Who's benefiting from that?" (S013)
- **Sources:** 13 (S005, S012, S013, S017, S027, S032, S045, S047, S048, S050, S051, S061, S070)

## 9. Strategy: systems, time, games, empathy *(This Is Strategy)*
- **What it is:** Strategy is a philosophy of becoming: a stated point of view about the change you seek and who you seek to change. It is a compass, not a map, and it is not tactics, goals, or planning. Four threads: systems (invisible forces and gravity), time (what today plants for later), games (any situation with players, scarcity, and outcomes, so a failed move is a move, not a verdict on you), and empathy (being clear who it is for and why they want it). Good strategy is mostly picking good waves.
- **Parts:** Name the system and where the gravity is; set a longer time horizon than competitors; know the game and how many aces are in the deck; ask what the people you serve believe; say the whole thing out loud like a proof with no "miracle" step.
- **When to use:** Any decision that is really about direction, not the next tactic.
- **How to score:** 5 if the strategy is simple to describe, hard to stick to, and has a clear answer for each thread; 3 if it names the customer but ignores the system or the game; 1 if it is a list of tactics or a plan that needs a miracle.
- **Quote:** "We're looking for systems, we're looking for time, we're looking for games, and finally, empathy." (S007) "it's a math proof. There has to be a QED. You're not allowed to insert and then a miracle happens." (S008)
- **Sources:** 12 (S007, S008, S012, S018, S019, S021, S024, S027, S030, S036, S040, S053)

## 10. Customer traction test
- **What it is:** The best early indicator of a startup (he credits Steve Blank): do the people who engage stick around, come back for more, and tell their friends? Look for traction, not scale. Start with ten people who would miss you.
- **Parts:** Find ten people with a real problem; see if it works for them; see if they come back; see if they tell others and what they say; only then go looking for a hundred.
- **When to use:** Deciding whether to scale, raise, hire, or spend on ads.
- **How to score:** His thresholds: ten people who would miss you and are telling friends (S008, S025); and the daily check, how many new customers came yesterday from a direct referral (S012). Score 5 if referrals are a meaningful share of new customers; 1 if every customer is bought.
- **Quote:** "Do you have ten people who would miss you if you were gone? These ten people, are they telling their friends?" (S008) "How many customers would miss you if you shut down tomorrow?" (S009)
- **Sources:** 12 (S002, S008, S009, S011, S012, S015, S020, S023, S025, S037, S040, S054)

## 11. Network effect test
- **What it is:** Does it work better for the user when their friends have it too? Growth that lasts comes from things that work better when others join (the fax machine, Facebook at Harvard), tied to an insatiable desire rather than a momentary gap. A network effect also protects you from a race to the bottom.
- **Parts:** Value from connection; easy recruiting; open enough to use, closed enough not to become a free commodity; a positive feedback loop whose next round works better.
- **When to use:** Product design, marketplace and software businesses, any plan that assumes viral growth.
- **How to score:** High if users gain directly when others join; low if there is no incentive to tell anyone. His blunt line: without a network effect built in, you are almost certainly going to fail (S037).
- **Quote:** "Where's the network effect? Why does it work better? Not better for you, but better for the user if their friends have it too?" (S007)
- **Sources:** 12 (S007, S018, S027, S031, S037, S039, S040, S055, S062, S064, S069, S078)

## 12. People like us do things like this
- **What it is:** His one-slide marketing talk and his definition of culture. People copy what their peers do; a product spreads when it becomes the ticket into an in-group. You do not invent the group (Nike did not invent runners); you find people already connected and lead them, then make the new thing normal for them.
- **Parts:** Find the people; learn what people like them do; build the product, culture, and rituals around that; move it from new to normal.
- **When to use:** Community-led products, category creation, any change you need a group to adopt.
- **How to score:** High if a defined group can say "people like us use this"; low if the product asks each buyer to decide alone.
- **Quote:** "marketing is People Like Us do things like this that's all it is it is a cultural signal to people who want affiliation and status to say this is what we do" (S024) "the goal if you want scale is to make what you're trying to shift from new to normal" (S024)
- **Sources:** 11 (S002, S011, S016, S020, S023, S024, S030, S033, S038, S051, S054)

## 13. Brand is a promise
- **What it is:** A brand is the promise you make, implicitly or explicitly, and the expectation people have about your next move. A logo is an outfit. The test: if Nike opened a hotel you would know what it was like; if Hyatt made sneakers you would not. A brand's value is the premium people pay over the substitute.
- **Parts:** State the promise; keep it every time, especially when it is hard; test new products against the promise, not against whether they could make money; make a bigger promise to get a bigger brand.
- **When to use:** Line extensions, rebrands, acquisitions, naming, any move that might muddle the story.
- **How to score:** 5 if customers would pay extra and miss you if you were gone; 1 if you have a logo and no premium.
- **Quote:** "A brand is a promise. It's what do I expect from you. It's would I miss you if you were gone." (S037) "the value of a brand is how much extra am I paying? above the substitute and if I'm not paying extra, you don't have a brand" (S033)
- **Sources:** 11 (S002, S010, S012, S014, S019, S022, S024, S033, S034, S037, S045)

## 14. Positioning: two good axes, and sending people to competitors
- **What it is:** Positioning is a generous map for busy people of who you are and who you are not. Draw two axes where both ends are good things (durable versus convenient, never good versus bad), stand in one corner, and point people who belong in another corner to the competitor who fits them. The written form is his fill-in-the-blank: for people who believe this and want that, this might be what you're looking for.
- **Parts:** Pick two valid axes; choose a corner; write the "for people who believe" sentence; send the wrong customer elsewhere politely, often to a worthy peer.
- **When to use:** Crowded markets, pitch decks, deciding what to say no to.
- **How to score:** His test: do you regularly and eagerly send business to competitors? If not, you have no position (S040, S007). Score low if the grid has one obviously bad quadrant.
- **Quote:** "it is about offering people who are busy a clear map of who you are and who you're not" (S040) "to be able to say we're not for everyone but we are for someone" (S019)
- **Sources:** 13 (S003, S007, S011, S012, S019, S023, S024, S029, S036, S039, S040, S042, S052)

## 15. See the system *(unnamed)*
- **What it is:** Systems are invisible forces that make one path the easiest and push back on anyone who leaves it (wedding costs, college admissions, the bestseller list). A system does what it is rewarded and measured for, so do not blame the people in it. Either help the system get what it wants and use its momentum, or move away and serve people outside it until the system chases you. Change agents (new technology or communication) are where systems crack.
- **Parts:** Name the system and its gravity; find what it rewards and measures; decide to work in it or on it; look for a change agent the existing players dislike; never protest a big system head on.
- **When to use:** Regulated or entrenched markets, B2B sales into institutions, any plan that assumes the industry will change because you are right.
- **How to score:** High if the plan rides a current the system already rewards or sits outside it; low if it requires the system to change on your schedule, or if the founder lacks the money for the scale of system they want to change (S027).
- **Quote:** "is what it is rewarded for is what it is measured for and if you're not happy with that change the reward system and the measurement system" (S018) "if you see something and you can't explain it it's probably because there's a system at work" (S036)
- **Sources:** 11 (S007, S008, S010, S018, S021, S023, S025, S036, S037, S040, S053)

## 16. The Dip, and sunk costs as a gift
- **What it is:** The Dip is the hard part between starting and getting good, and it has to exist or what lies beyond would not be scarce. Some hard parts are dips; others are cul-de-sacs or slopes that only get worse. Quit before you start or after the hard part, never in the middle. Sunk costs are a gift from your former self that you do not have to accept.
- **Parts:** Before starting, see the dip and pack what you need to cross it; when it comes, ask whether anyone has made it across and whether more input would get you there; knowing what you know now, would you take this on today?
- **When to use:** Quit or persist decisions, pivots, leases, long programs, a stalled product.
- **How to score:** Persist (high) if others have crossed this dip and you have the resources; quit (low) if there is no evidence more money or time gets you across. Set the deadline for calling it quits at the start (S062).
- **Quote:** "Never quit at the hard part. Either quit before you start or quit after the hard part." (S013) "a sunk cost is a gift from the you of yesterday to the you of today and you don't have to accept that gift" (S038)
- **Sources:** 14 (S004, S009, S013, S014, S017, S021, S022, S025, S035, S038, S043, S049, S062, S034)

## 17. Marketing is making change happen *(This Is Marketing)*
- **What it is:** Marketing is not advertising. It is telling a true story to the right people, in a way that helps them get where they want to go, so they tell their friends. If no change happens, no marketing happened. Marketing with people, not at them.
- **Parts:** His five steps (S017): invent a thing worth making with a story worth talking about; design it so a few people particularly benefit; tell a story that matches that group's dreams; let the customers spread the word; show up consistently and generously for years to earn permission.
- **When to use:** When a founder says "I just want to sell product", or treats marketing as the department that runs ads after the product ships.
- **How to score:** High if the plan names the change and the people and makes them the distribution; low if marketing is reverse-fitted to a finished product.
- **Quote:** "marketers make change happen if no change happens the marketer didn't do her job" (S039) "marketing is creating the conditions for an idea to spread and it doesn't spread because you're trying hard to push it" (S017)
- **Sources:** 12 (S003, S009, S010, S012, S017, S018, S021, S029, S039, S045, S046, S054)

## 18. Pick your customers, pick your future *(the four choices)*
- **What it is:** The customers you pick set your daily life: their budgets, fears, tempers, and language, and so the product. He lists four choices founders skip because they assume them as givens: customers, competition, source of validation, and distribution.
- **Parts:** Choose customers who demand quality and pay for it; choose who you compete with; choose whose opinion counts; choose how it reaches people.
- **When to use:** Segment choice, B2B versus consumer, picking a first big client, deciding which deals to walk away from.
- **How to score:** High if the founder chose customers on purpose and can say what kind of days that buys; low if they took whoever came, especially price-sensitive buyers.
- **Quote:** "So when you pick your customers, their stories, their fears, their desires, their budgets, what they use as fuel, you've just announced how you want to spend your days." (S014)
- **Sources:** 9 (S007, S012, S014, S023, S024, S037, S042, S045, S069)

## 19. Practical empathy: watch, don't interview
- **What it is:** No one knows what you know, wants what you want, or believes what you believe, and that is fine. You are not the customer. People lie in interviews and focus groups, so watch what they do, offer choices, see where they get stuck, and ask which part scares them. Sit next to the person who will use it.
- **Parts:** Find them by beliefs and frustrations; watch behavior; give them choices; rewrite where they get confused; follow their passion, not yours.
- **When to use:** Customer research, product design, testing a book or offer before launch.
- **How to score:** High if decisions come from watching real users; low if they come from surveys, a focus group, or the founder's own taste.
- **Quote:** "We have to watch what they do. We have to give them choices." (S011) "no one knows what you know no one needs what you need no one wants what you want no one believes what you believe" (S054)
- **Sources:** 10 (S011, S017, S018, S022, S023, S030, S033, S036, S053, S054)

## 20. Freelancer, soloist, or entrepreneur
- **What it is:** A freelancer is paid when they work and has one way up: better clients. An entrepreneur builds something bigger than themselves that earns while they sleep and can be sold. The soloist (impresario) builds the conditions and hires freelancers for everything else. The trap is the dead zone in the middle, where a talented freelancer hires the cheapest available person, themselves, for every job.
- **Parts:** Decide which one you are before setting an income target; as a freelancer, get better clients by saying no to bad ones; as an entrepreneur, invent jobs and hire people into them; ask what you own.
- **When to use:** Agency and services founders, solo businesses, first hires, "should I scale this practice?"
- **How to score:** Low if the founder is doing work they could hire out because they are cheapest and most available; high if they own an asset that works without them, or are a freelancer moving up by client quality.
- **Quote:** "Freelancers get paid when we work. Freelancers use our own skills and fingers to do the work. Entrepreneurs build things bigger than themselves. Entrepreneurs get paid when they sleep." (S019) "Because if you don't own anything, then you're simply hustling to get people to pay you for your time." (S020)
- **Sources:** 8 (S004, S005, S011, S014, S019, S020, S028, S042)

## 21. Kindling and logs *(match the project to your resources)*
- **What it is:** Money raised is kindling; the market you chase is the log. A small loan can run a hot dog cart, not a 40-city chain. Pick a project sized to your cash, time, and reputation, in an industry that pays up front if you can, and do not sign up for something that takes longer than you can afford.
- **Parts:** How much kindling (money, reputation, time) do you have; how big is the log; how long did people before you take; is time working for you or against you.
- **When to use:** Fundraising, choosing a first market, podcasts and content businesses, inventory-heavy launches.
- **How to score:** His markers: a podcast takes four years to make money, not four weeks (S036); needing a full container from China before selling means the wrong scale (S020); no search engine without $100 million (S020). Low if the log is far bigger than the kindling.
- **Quote:** "The money you're raising from investors is kindling, and the logs you're starting are the markets you're trying to get to." (S007) "Do I have enough kindling to burn this log?" (S062)
- **Sources:** 7 (S007, S008, S020, S021, S027, S036, S062)

## 22. Problem or situation, and the knot *(The Knot)*
- **What it is:** A problem has a solution, maybe one you dislike. A situation has no solution you are willing to pay for, like gravity, so accept it and move on. Being stuck almost always means wanting two things that cannot coexist: an "and" that is secretly a "but". Name the but, then drop one side or turn it into an and.
- **Parts:** Sort each issue into problem or situation; find the hidden but; say it out loud; tell the truth about the entanglements.
- **When to use:** Founders stuck between two goals (edge and mass appeal, frontier and applause, growth and the old way).
- **How to score:** Low if the plan quietly requires two incompatible wants; high if the founder has chosen.
- **Quote:** "If it's a situation, there is no solution that you are willing to pay for." (S025) "Because it's when we want two things at the same time that cannot coexist that we feel stuck." (S009)
- **Sources:** 8 (S009, S013, S015, S025, S043, S047, S051, S053)

## 23. Good decision versus good outcome
- **What it is:** Borrowed from Annie Duke and the Pete Carroll pass: judge a decision by what you knew and wanted at the time, and by whether a good decision-maker would make it, not by luck. Reward good decisions that did not work out.
- **Parts:** What did we know; what were we seeking; was it the best bet on the odds and the mission; separate that from the result.
- **When to use:** Post-mortems, reviewing a failed launch, deciding whether to repeat a bet.
- **How to score:** Guidance: score the bet on its odds and fit with the mission, then note the outcome separately.
- **Quote:** "Would a good decision-maker choose what I chose? If the answer is yes, then you made a good decision." (S014) "If you buy a lottery ticket and you win, it was still a bad decision." (S008)
- **Sources:** 6 (S007, S008, S014, S022, S038, S047)

## 24. Theory of profit for an AI business
- **What it is:** His 2026 test for a second-wave AI business. Four parts: it creates its own useful data stack, has a network effect built in, solves an expensive problem for people with resources, and is bootstrappable. Premium purchases come from the three drives (status, affiliation, freedom from fear), through a middle layer (legitimacy, transformation, control, trust), justified by an outer layer (access, time, convenience). Sell into a forcing function that already exists.
- **Parts:** Data stack; network effect; expensive problem with a budget; bootstrappable; a story for the buyer's champion; a forcing function (competitive, technology, or market upheaval).
- **When to use:** AI products, especially thin wrappers around a frontier model.
- **How to score:** Low if it sells cost reduction alone or is a bare gateway to a model ("naked AI is a trap", S055); high if all four parts hold.
- **Quote:** "An AI business worth building delivers something from the middle layer, justified by the outer layer." (S055) "sell into a forcing function that already exists, don't try to create one." (S055)
- **Sources:** 2 (S055, S056)
