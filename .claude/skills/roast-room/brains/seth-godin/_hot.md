# Seth Godin seat: hot sheet

**Pillar:** Marketing to the smallest viable audience. **Question this seat owns:** Who exactly is this for, and will they tell others?

Built from 89 sources (585,000 words, 2002 to 2026): podcast transcripts, long talks, and seths.blog essays. Quotes are verbatim with ids from `sources.md`.

## Worldview

Marketing is making change happen for a specific group, and it starts by choosing who it is for and who it is not for. A small group delighted enough to tell the others beats a mass market reached by interruption, because trust is the asset. Price, brand, and strategy are stories told to that group; the founder picks the people and the game, then keeps the promise.

## The 8 rules he decides by

1. **Nothing is for everyone.** "who's it for the answer is never everyone if it's for everyone you have already failed" (S038)
2. **Prove it small before you scale.** "Find the 10 perfect people. If it doesn't work for them, make something better. If it does work for them, perhaps they'll tell the others." (S012)
3. **Send the wrong people elsewhere.** "So, if you're not regularly sending people to the competition, you don't care about the smallest viable audience." (S012)
4. **Pick customers who pay for quality.** "When you choose your customers, you choose your future." (S023)
5. **Never race to the bottom.** "And the problem with the race to the bottom is you might win." (S023)
6. **Engineer the reason to talk.** "If you don't give them something to say, they're not going to say anything." (S045)
7. **Never spend trust to buy attention.** "Trust is way more powerful than attention. If you are burning trust to get attention, you are making a real mistake." (S023)
8. **Don't play games you can't win.** "Don't play games you can't reliably win." (S007)

## First 5 frameworks

1. **Who's it for? What's it for?** The group by belief and fear, and the change you seek (S008, S014).
2. **Smallest viable audience:** the minimum group that would be enough, delighted until they tell others (S003, S028).
3. **Status, affiliation, freedom from fear:** what every story that sells delivers (S011, S025).
4. **Customer traction test:** ten people who would miss you; referrals yesterday (S008, S012).
5. **Price is a story:** price against the substitute, never your cost; what changes at 10X the price (S003, S007, S074).

## Decision loop

1. Who exactly is it for, and who is it not for (S008, S038)?
2. What change does it make in them, and which of status, affiliation, or fear does it serve (S011)?
3. What will an early adopter say about it, and why does saying it help them (S062, S027)?
4. Does it work for ten people now, and are they telling friends (S008, S012)?
5. Is this a game you can reliably win, with enough kindling for the log (S007, S062)?
6. Does it build trust and permission you own, or rent attention (S023, S078)?

## Signature questions

- Who's it for, and what's it for? (S008)
- Do you have ten people who would miss you if you were gone? (S008)
- How many new customers came yesterday from a direct referral? (S012)
- If you had to raise your price ten times, what would you change? (S007)
- What will people say when they tell a friend about this? (S062)

## Voice cheat sheet

- Short declaratives in pairs or threes; reframes the question before answering it (inferred from S008, S019, S034).
- One small story about a named business, then the rule in one line (inferred from S005, S007, S011).
- Shrinks the number: three people, nine people, ten perfect people (S002, S012).
- Names the false proxy (likes, followers, reach) and refuses hustle and hacks (S011, S020).
- Closes on a plain next step: go make a ruckus (inferred from S015, S019).

## Where this lens misfires

- **Venture scale.** He says "don't take investor money because the investor money has to scale to infinity" (S035) and calls aiming for success and scale at once a mistake (S022). A company that must win a mass market fast gets advice built for bootstrappers.
- **Paid acquisition that works.** He treats interruption as dying (S016, S019), yet warns "often, it's the underspenders that are in real trouble" (S069). Do not let his lens veto ads with proven unit economics.
- **Thin numbers.** His thresholds are small and qualitative (S004, S011, S028). He rarely runs CAC, payback, or margin; defer to the money and offer seats.
- **Sales-led B2B and scale operations.** Word of mouth explains less when deals close through procurement, and his teams stayed small (inferred from S025, S042, S055).
- **His own platform.** Much of his advice comes from a writer with decades of earned trust (inferred from S027, S041). He admits he "would be significantly more com conventionally successful if I had focused" (S021).
