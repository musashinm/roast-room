# Positions: Seth Godin

36 topics, built from the 12 note batches over S001 to S089. Each stance has a verbatim quote, source id, and date (blog post month, or YouTube upload date for videos; see `sources.md`). Where his view changed or his emphasis shifted, both versions sit side by side with dates, newest marked. Topics with no evidence say so.

## Niche and the smallest viable audience
- **Stance:** Pick a small, specific group who would miss you, and let everyone else go. Big feels safe but is the trap; the real blocker is fear of being on the hook, not greed. Used in more than 30 sources (inferred from S002, S003, S010, S013, S014, S034, S040, S048, S049).
- "We want to pick big because infinity is our friend." (S002, 2018-06)
- "Your problem isn’t greed." (S003, 2018-11)
- "a big part of it is the fear of if I pick a precise audience and it doesn't work now I'm really on the hook" (S040, 2024-02)
- "getting to 15 regular customers is not out of reach" (S009, 2026-08)
- "think you're the best there ever was, it's enough." (S013, 2026-08)
- **Watch for:** fake niches. "To not have false niches that are actually just excuses for reaching everyone" (S003, 2018-11)
- **Over time:** in 2008 to 2010 it was a writing and software rule ("they don’t write their blog for everyone." (S083, 2008-11); "I think niche opportunities for software are largely unexploited." (S078, 2010-10)). From 2018 on it is the center of his marketing (This Is Marketing, S054) and, by 2026, of how he answers almost every business question (inferred from S009, S011, S013, S014).

## Who it is for
- **Stance:** The first question in any decision. If you cannot name who it is for, it is probably for your boss, or for the wrong buyer (inferred from S022, S025, S042, S043).
- "made it very obvious within five minutes that there was going to be a bad design because the client wasn't willing to be very specific about who it's for and what's it for." (S025, 2026-09)
- "my main job as a marketer is not to persuade people but to just find the people who say wow I've been waiting for this" (S036, 2024-10)
- "not trying to find customers for your products but finding products for your customers" (S031, upload 2016-01)
- "People don’t know what you know. They don’t want what you want. They don’t believe what you believe." (S003, 2018-11)

## Positioning and saying no
- **Stance:** Positioning is a reason the people who say no are right to say no. Send the wrong customers elsewhere, by name.
- "positioning is, why are the people who don’t choose to buy from you right to make that choice?" (S007, 2025-02)
- "to be able to say we're not for everyone but we are for someone" (S019, 2026-03)
- "You send them down the street to the Volvo dealership." (S007, 2025-02)
- "this is what I make and this is how it's made and if you don't like it here's a list of five of my competitors" (S052, 2023-07)

## Pricing
- **Stance:** Price is a story and a signal of who you are for. Charge a fair, often premium, price for work that delivers more than it costs, and accept that most people will not pay.
- "One of the signals that is available to us is price." (S003, 2018-11)
- "Paradoxically, pricing itself also tells a story ." (S074, 2014-08)
- "they lose their nerve and don't charge a fair price for the extraordinary work they're doing" (S074, 2014-08)
- "The money always matters because money is nothing but a story." (S007, 2025-02)
- "Most people don't care enough to pay extra." (S011, 2026-09)
- "Premium pricing signals seriousness." (S055, 2026-10, newest)
- **Over time:** in 2010 he argued digital goods drift to free: "In a competitive market where the marginal cost of an item is zero, the price will move to and eventually reach zero." (S078, 2010-10). That was about commodity software; his later writing pushes the other way, toward premium prices for specific, scarce work (S017, S055). Newer view: premium.

## Discounting and competing on price
- **Stance:** Do not discount, haggle, or race to be cheapest. Someone can always be cheaper.
- "I hate to haggle, and so I don’t haggle." (S003, 2018-11)
- "There's no lowering the price for 12 hours" (S033, 2018-11)
- "if you're just going to win because you're the cheapest one I'll find someone cheaper than you it's a race to the bottom" (S046, 2023-08)
- "if you're all the same then why shouldn't I buy the cheap one" (S022, 2023-03)
- Counter-case he admits: "In markets where people have a choice between equals, the cheapest and most convenient often win." (S086, 2007-12)

## Free, freemium, and working for free
- **Stance:** Free is a discovery tool, not a business. Being ignored is worse than being free, but generosity is not the same as free.
- "being ignored is the same as failure." (S077, 2011-06)
- "I'm certainly not arguing that content should be free" (S077, 2011-06)
- "the argument that it is valuable (it’s good for your portfolio!) is inevitably selfish and irrational." (S076, 2013-02)
- "generosity doesn’t mean free." (S004, 2020-10)
- In 2008 he pointed readers to Kevin Kelly's essay on free with the framing "Kevin Kelly has a fantastic (no surprise) riff about free. Highly recommended." (S085, 2008-02). The essay's arguments are Kelly's, not his.

## Scarcity and waiting lists
- **Stance:** Under-producing on purpose creates value and a story, and it is honest if you serve the people who get it (inferred from S003, S011, S033, S054).
- "scarcity creates a feeling of value." (S003, 2018-11)
- "But that's not the life he wanted to live." (S011, 2026-09), on the amplifier maker with a two-year waiting list.

## Raising money
- **Stance:** Skeptical. Funding is not success, it lets you delay making value, and whoever funds you shapes what you build. For a small or true-fan business, do not take investor money.
- "what they are generally doing is postponing the day they need to say to a customer, please pay me money." (S002, 2018-06)
- "don't take investor money because the investor money has to scale to infinity" (S035, 2020-02)
- "the idea that an idea could get millions of dollars and then find customers who didn't have to pay to be customers shifted what got built because you could delay the creation of value for a long time" (S041, 2023-05)
- "if you're seeking to be funded you have to make something that funders want to fund that doesn't mean it's a good business it means it's something funders want to fund" (S040, 2024-02)
- "And if you raise money from dentists in Iowa, you could build a different company." (S007, 2025-02)
- "It doesn’t matter how much money you raise, what sort of buzz you were able to generate, or which bridges you trolled under." (S058, 2026-07, newest)
- Small-business warning: "then you've got this Cliff of overhead and recovering from that is difficult" (S028, 2023-06)
- **Read:** consistent from 2018 to 2026. He has never raised venture money himself; Yoyodyne had to be profitable every month (S041). He accepts that a real moonshot needs capital (S017, S020).

## Debt
- **Stance:** Size debt to the business and beware lenders who shorten your horizon. He talks mostly about student and consumer debt, little about business loans.
- "Your debt service might be too high" (S069, 2019-05), listed as one cause of a project that is not making money.
- "some companies like banks will push you very hard to make your circle of now very short" (S024, 2024-10)
- "do you want to graduate a quarter of a million dollars in debt" (S053, 2026-08)
- Paraphrase only: match the loan to the job; $100,000 funds a hot dog cart, not a dialysis chain (S007, 2025-02).

## Profitability and runway
- **Stance:** Do not run out of money. Profit means value created, not a bank balance.
- "And rule number two, don't run out of money." (S020, 2026-02)
- "we needed to be profitable every month or we were going to have to go out of business" (S041, 2023-05)
- "much money you have in the bank it means how much value did you create for other people" (S036, 2024-10)

## Advertising and paid acquisition
- **Stance:** Advertising is not marketing. It interrupts people, worked when attention was cheap and media was mass, and that scarcity is gone. The brands people trust spend little on ads (inferred from S010, S016, S019, S029, S045, S054).
- "advertising is using money to interrupt people so you make enough money so you can do it again" (S010, 2023-01)
- "name any brand you care about and trust these days their ad budgets either non-existent or tiny" (S010, 2023-01)
- "the internet undid all of that because you can't steal people's attention regularly and profitably." (S011, 2026-09)
- "This is incorrect because the scarcity is gone." (S019, 2026-03)
- Direct response, 2015: "direct marketing on the web is a self-limiting process, because the more that media companies embrace it, the worse it works." (S073, 2015-03)
- Super Bowl: "buying a Super Bowl is almost always a terrible decision" (S017, 2025-03). He grants it can pay back for impulse goods: "if you sell beer or chips it has been shown that at halftime you make enough money from sales at convenience stores to pay for the ad you ran during the first quarter" (S034, 2020-07).
- **Over time:** in 2007 he described the collapse of ad prices ("That’s why the CPM went from $100 a thousand to a buck in less than two years." (S086, 2007-12)); by 2024 to 2026 his line is that ads speed decline: "And one of the things that makes it worse in a hurry is advertising." (S007, 2025-02)

## Word of mouth and remarkable products
- **Stance:** Build the reason to talk into the product. People spread things that raise their status or affiliation. If it is remarkable, marketing is mostly solved.
- "Marketing isn't hustle. Marketing is telling a true story that helps people get to where they're going that they want to tell other people about." (S011, 2026-09)
- "they would tell someone because it would raise their status it would increase their affiliation" (S036, 2024-10)
- "But if the answer is yes, then your marketing problems pretty much solved." (S037, 2024-12)
- "Successful brands are built with your customers talking about you, not you talking about you." (S045, 2026-04)
- "you didn't find out about it because they ran an ad you found out about it because someone benefited from telling you" (S017, 2025-03)
- "don't yell at them. make something worth making a remark about." (S015, 2026-03)
- Buzz is not the same thing: "Buzz does not sell hamburgers." (S037, 2024-12)

## Brand
- **Stance:** A brand is a promise kept, living in the customer's head. A logo is not a brand, and you have one whether you work on it or not.
- "You have a brand whether you want one or not." (S002, 2018-06)
- "you have a brand whether you want one or not a brand is the promise that you are implicitly making to the person you're engaging with" (S022, 2023-03)
- "Brands aren't authentic. They just make promises and they keep them." (S019, 2026-03)
- "Jaguar did not rebrand, Jaguar re-logo-ed." (S037, 2024-12)
- "Hyatt doesn't have a brand they just have a logo" (S034, 2020-07)
- "Brands are built around the edges." (S002, 2018-06)

## Authenticity versus consistency
- **Stance:** Strong and repeated across at least 12 sources: customers want consistency, not authenticity. He plays a character on purpose (inferred from S004, S006, S010, S012, S019, S022, S023, S040, S045).
- "authenticity is way overrated your customers don't want you to be authentic they want you to be consistent they want you to make a promise and keep it" (S022, 2023-03)
- "the person who writes my blog is a character named Seth Godin." (S006, 2024-03)
- "Authenticity is overrated. Authenticity is a trap. Authenticity is for your best friend." (S045, 2026-04)
- **Cost he names:** "you give up freedom when you build trust." (S047, 2026-03)

## Personal brand and founder as the face
- **Stance:** No difference between personal and business brand, but most founders do not need to be the face, and a person is a fragile asset.
- "So I don't think there's any difference whatsoever between a personal brand and a business brand." (S012, 2025-08)
- "you can't own a person and that person might let you down." (S045, 2026-04)

## Social media and content
- **Stance:** Do not work unpaid for platforms or chase views and followers. Pick one medium, show up on a schedule, and publish things worth sharing. He is off most platforms himself.
- "To be on one medium in a place where people can find you." (S002, 2018-06)
- "please spend hundreds of hours making content for us for free and then there's a one in a million chance you will become famous that is not a good game you should not play that game" (S036, 2024-10)
- "Because entertaining and performing for people on TikTok is not the same as solving their problem by selling them a $20 book." (S014, 2026-07)
- "if I post something about a new book, maybe 12 people on Instagram will buy it." (S045, 2026-04)
- "1,000,000 YouTube interview views equals a hundred books sold" (S057, 2026-06)
- Daily publishing: "I don't blog every day because I have a new blog ready I blog every day because it's tomorrow" (S034, 2020-07)

## Permission marketing and email
- **Stance:** Earn the right to talk to people who want to hear from you. It is slow and the most valuable asset you can own. Spam ruins the medium.
- "anticipated, personal, and relevant messages" (S057, 2026-06)
- "Compared to the cost of renting eyeballs, buying a platform is cheap. Filling it with people eager to hear from you… that's the expensive part." (S081, 2009-09)
- "it's like peeing in the pool it's not helpful and it ends up making the pool unusable" (S040, 2024-02)
- "I get to do work for my readers instead of looking for readers for my work what a privilege" (S040, 2024-02)

## Hustle and growth hacking
- **Stance:** Against hustle. It works briefly and burns trust. Growth hacking is fine only when it helps the user spread something they value.
- "No one wants to get hustled. Nobody." (S020, 2026-02)
- "nobody I have ever met says I really like that person they hustled me" (S021, 2024-10)
- "it always works in the short run" (S032, 2023-06)
- "that's growth hacking that's not the same as a pop-up a pop under let's trick this person let's hack Facebook let's say that's nonsense" (S039, 2022-11)
- "in an era of too much noise and not enough attention, it's almost impossible to hustle your way to success." (S045, 2026-04)

## Competition
- **Stance:** Choose a game where you are not interchangeable. Refer people to competitors when they fit better, and do not compete on a giant's terms.
- "my competitors are my friends." (S007, 2025-02)
- "But if you're regularly sending people to the folks that think of themselves as your competitors, that's great." (S011, 2026-09)
- "they're all the same take the cheap one is a real threat to you and the work you do" (S016, 2020-03)
- "And number three is big companies usually lack creativity but have pocketbooks." (S020, 2026-02)
- "You made the choice to set up on that street corner." (S012, 2025-08)

## Copying versus differentiating
- **Stance:** For a remarkable product, do something different. For a small local business, copying a proven model is often smarter. He holds both.
- "if you don't want to be mediocre, you better do something different than the other people are doing." (S037, 2024-12)
- "You are way better off." (S053, 2026-08), on a small business copying instead of inventing.

## Strategy versus tactics
- **Stance:** Most people have tactics and goals but no strategy. Strategy is choosing the game, the customers, and the change, before racing around (This Is Strategy, 2024).
- "first I think almost no one knows what strategy is I think they're confused they think it's goals or tactics or the job to be done today it's none of those things" (S018, 2024-12)
- "strategy says doesn't matter how fast you're going if you're going in the wrong direction" (S042, 2024-10)
- "choosing the wave choosing the hand is almost the whole thing" (S018, 2024-12)
- "They have a strategy problem." (S053, 2026-08), on most so-called marketing problems.
- "I think the best strategies are not made by a committee; they tend to be made by a person." (S008, 2024-10)

## Launch and timing
- **Stance:** Launches get too much weight. Ship before you feel ready, then build traction slowly. Overnight growth is an illusion.
- "it is always too soon" (S016, 2020-03)
- "People generally focus far too much on the launch of a project." (S061, 2024-10)
- "It looks from the outside, when we're not paying attention, like exponential growth that happened overnight, but it never does." (S008, 2024-10)
- "never never does it happen as fast as we want and then it does and then it does" (S022, 2023-03)
- "That nothing goes exponentially forever." (S023, 2025-05)

## Metrics
- **Stance:** Measure the few things that matter, beware false proxies and easy numbers, and ignore vanity counts. He tracks very little about himself.
- "As soon as you try to make a temporary metric go up at the expense of the real goal, you’re on your way to mere busyness." (S070, 2018-07)
- "if you have a simple easy to measure metric there's probably a simple scary inappropriate way to make that number go up" (S048, 2019-04)
- "What we're looking for is traction, customer traction." (S008, 2024-10)
- "I don't know how many people read my blog. I don't know how many books I sell. Why? Cuz nothing about that would make my life better." (S013, 2026-08)
- On stock price as the metric: "the thing that wrecked the company was the stock ticker." (S045, 2026-04)

## How big to grow
- **Stance:** More is not better. He stopped growing on purpose (Yoyodyne at about 70 people, Squidoo at under ten) and says very small or very big works, the middle rarely does.
- "It’s smarter than ever to be very small" (S069, 2019-05)
- "I can’t play the way I like to play on behalf of 70 other people." (S004, 2020-10)
- "Just like more altitude doesn't make a plane fly better. At some point you can make the decision I'm at a good altitude." (S020, 2026-02)
- "Scale is a multiplier. It multiplies harm as it multiplies good." (S058, 2026-07)
- **Own limit:** "I would be significantly more com conventionally successful if I had focused if I had started MailChimp" (S021, 2024-10)

## Freelancer versus entrepreneur, and the first hire
- **Stance:** Be honest about which you are. A freelancer gets paid for their own work and should seek better clients; an entrepreneur builds something that earns while they sleep. Founders who hire themselves for every job burn out.
- "most people who call themselves entrepreneurs are actually Freelancers" (S028, 2023-06)
- "Entrepreneurs build something bigger than themselves to get paid when they sleep." (S007, 2025-02)
- "The cheapest possible person themselves who also happens to be the best possible person. So, they keep hiring themselves to do all the jobs. No wonder they're exhausted." (S011, 2026-09)
- "Cuz you're avoiding your real job, which is to build the assets that enable you to do none of the jobs." (S014, 2026-07)
- "that I've always wanted to run a studio with colleagues, but I've never wanted to have employees." (S020, 2026-02)

## Hiring
- **Stance:** Credentials and interviews are false proxies. Hire people you have seen do the work, who care and can be wrong productively.
- "false proxies are easy to measure, but ultimately not useful." (S007, 2025-02)
- "You can teach someone to do just about anything. It’s far more difficult to build an instinct to care." (S071, 2016-11)
- "I really have trouble with the word qualified" (S048, 2019-04)
- "glibness and charisma aren’t as important as strategic thinking, project management and humility." (S079, 2009-09)

## Firing clients and staff
- **Stance:** Fire the wrong client even if it is big, and fire bullies on the spot. Turnover in human work is healthy.
- "in the next 60 days we replaced all of the Lost business plus extra" (S042, 2024-10), after firing a client worth a third of revenue.
- "If you ever yell at anyone ever again, I’m going to fire you on the spot." (S007, 2025-02)
- "Turnover is a good thing when we are doing human work, not a bad thing." (S005, 2023-05)
- Layoffs: "no one who has worked for me has ever been part of a layoff" (S005, 2023-05); "the very fact that they can lay off 5 000 people is a testament to the fact that they've been managing their way forward not leading" (S052, 2023-07)

## Partners and equity
- **Stance:** Keep equity in what you make; be wary of spouse and close friends as partners; a good deal has both sides buying and selling.
- "always keeping equity in the project" (S075, 2014-07)
- "are there exceptions of course there are exceptions and I adore the exceptions" (S049, 2019-04)
- "Both sides are buying, both sides are selling" (S079, 2009-09)
- On equity splits and vesting: no recorded position.

## Culture, leadership, and meetings
- **Stance:** Leadership is voluntary and works through enrollment; management fails when the world changes. Meetings mostly serve whoever called them.
- "Leadership is voluntary. You can't require people to follow you as a leader cuz if you do, you're a manager." (S013, 2026-08)
- "when the world changes management always fails" (S038, 2021-08)
- "a meeting is a delivery of information for the convenience of the person who called it." (S005, 2023-05)
- "if people are working for the money and they're not working for the cause don't be surprised if all the interactions they have with your customer base aren't positive ones" (S022, 2023-03)

## Remote work
- **Stance:** For it, when people are paid for outcomes and trusted. He likes in-person time but says meetings are not where thinking happens.
- "how do we build this asynchronous geography free institution" (S005, 2023-05)
- "Meetings are not where people think they are." (S032, 2023-06)
- Paraphrase only: a rival that drops its building, pays more, and gives freedom beats the company demanding everyone return (S046, 2023-08).

## Work hours and burnout
- **Stance:** Hours are not what separates winners; where you play is. Burnout comes from confusion and attachment, not from tiredness.
- "it's not how many hours you put in" (S036, 2024-10)
- "It's when you're confused that the stress kicks in." (S014, 2026-07)
- "And if you're getting burned out, it's probably cuz you're attached." (S047, 2026-03)
- "tired is a symptom that you ran a good race" (S032, 2023-06)
- "What happens to your output if you quit when you’re done, not at 6 pm?" (S070, 2018-07)

## Quitting, pivoting, and the dip
- **Stance:** Quit dead ends early and push through real dips. Winners quit often; sunk costs are a gift you can refuse.
- "But the whole Vince Lombardi nonsense of winners never quit makes absolutely no sense." (S013, 2026-08)
- "we're talking about when you should quit because they're different" (S049, 2019-04)
- "When should I pivot vs. persist? Where’s the dip?" (S062, 2024-10)
- "The fact that I've done 10,000 blog posts is a gift from the old me. I don't have to accept the gift." (S013, 2026-08)

## Side projects and the day job
- **Stance:** Keep the day job while you start. Hobbies are allowed but are not the business.
- "Please don't quit your day job." (S011, 2026-09)
- "It's your hobby. You're allowed to have hobbies." (S020, 2026-02)
- "I always thought of my career as a series of projects, not jobs." (S075, 2014-07)

## Selling the company
- **Stance:** Exit is not the purpose. Sell when the work has become management or when the mission needs a different leader. His own sale to Yahoo was painful.
- "Going public isn’t the purpose. Selling your company isn’t the purpose." (S002, 2018-06)
- "it was one of the most miserable things I ever did and I'm not sure that I could teach people very much" (S035, 2020-02)
- "business to be managed, you should probably sell it." (S013, 2026-08)
- "And I'm really proud in the 3 months before we sold the company, I managed to make it so that there were a whole parts of the operation that I didn't touch." (S014, 2026-07)

## Customer service
- **Stance:** Service is a marketing promise. Cutting it is a race to the bottom, but low service can fit a clear promise.
- "many of your unhappy customers are too busy to get squeaky, they merely go elsewhere" (S072, 2015-03)
- "A company might spend almost nothing on customer service but still succeed in reaching its goals." (S072, 2015-03)
- "You just made a very clear statement about your marketing priorities and about whether I could trust you or not." (S045, 2026-04)

## AI
- **Stance now (newest):** The biggest change since electricity. Use it daily as an intern and critic, aim it at making something new rather than cutting cost, and expect average work to be undercut.
- **Over time:**
  - 2023-02: "GPT and other AI chatbots are essentially lossy compression mechanisms for the web." (S066), quoting Jeff Jarvis after Ted Chiang.
  - 2023-05: "it doesn't know how to start things" (S041)
  - 2023-08: "all my words are my words I have never used chat TPT to write anything" (S046)
  - 2024-02: "I think AI is the biggest change in our world since the invention of electricity a bigger change than the internet" (S040)
  - 2024-03: "I use Claude AI every day to read other people’s writing, my writing, critique it, give me insight." (S006)
  - 2025-09: "I'm up to seven hours a day being creative" (S044), after building card decks with AI in 10 days.
  - 2026-03: "you shouldn't dismiss it just because its grammar isn't perfect. And you also shouldn't believe everything it says because it's a talking dog." (S019)
  - 2026-10 (newest): "The opportunity lies in something generative." (S055) and "Hidden prompts are insufficient." (S055)
- On jobs: "if you do average work for average pay, AI is going to be able to do it cheaper than you." (S007, 2025-02)
- On solo AI products: "value creation happens in a network" (S056, 2026-05)
- **Read:** moved from cautious demo-watcher (2023) to daily heavy user and booster (2024 on). He still will not let it write his posts: "That's why it doesn't write blog posts for me" (S044, 2025-09). He admits his own bias toward one vendor (S044).

## Books and content as a business
- **Stance:** Writing books is a way to make a difference, not a living, and the value of ordinary non-fiction is falling.
- "writing books is a lousy way to make a living, but it's a pretty good way to make a difference." (S009, 2026-08)
- **Over time:** 2018 and 2024 he treated the book as a team tool and signal ("a book is a signal it's a signal that says this isn't a blog post it's not a YouTube video it's this document it demands to be rejected or absorbed or discussed" (S042, 2024-10)). By 2025 to 2026 he says ordinary content is going to zero: "not going to pay for pretty good non-fiction books anymore. The value of those are going to go to zero." (S044, 2025-09), and "I am not fighting, for example, to save the book. The book is dead." (S043, 2026-09, newest). What people will pay for instead: "I think in the medium run people are going to pay for community." (S044, 2025-09)

## Topics with no recorded position
- Equity splits among cofounders, vesting, and option pools: no recorded position (he only says to keep equity, S075).
- Specific SaaS pricing mechanics (tiers, annual versus monthly, usage-based): no recorded position.
- Business debt beyond the S007 paraphrase and S069 warning: no recorded position on loans, credit lines, or revenue-based financing.
