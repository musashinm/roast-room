# Stories: Jason Lemkin

18 stories and cases he reuses, with the lesson he draws. Source ids in parentheses; the newest telling is marked where the story changed. Most-retold first.

1. **Selling EchoSign to Adobe too early.** He sold in 2011 when EchoSign had passed about $10M ARR, was growing near 100%, had 110% to 140% net retention, and was cash-flow positive. He knew it was a mistake before the deal closed. Lesson: recurring revenue with high retention compounds, so a founder with those numbers should not sell; get a mentor two stages ahead who says so (S049, S050, S059, S061, S070, S085, S088, S089). Newer framing (2024-04-10): he sold partly out of financial need, "I was tired of being poor basically" (S089).
2. **The first VP of Sales who did worse than the founder.** His first VP Sales was a big-logo hire from a large company, spent the Series A, and revenue per lead fell about 50%. He knew within 30 days and kept him; he calls it "my failure as a CEO". Lesson: stage fit beats a logo, and the CEO owns a bad VP hire (S029, S079, S081, S090, S093).
3. **Brendan Cassidy doubles revenue per lead.** His second VP, a former customer and the first head of sales at LinkedIn, took 20 months to recruit, brought three trusted people in week one, doubled revenue per lead in about 60 to 90 days by changing only the script and the lead routing, and the company was cash-flow positive within six months. Lesson: hire the one who has built from near zero, recruit them long before you need them, and judge them in one cycle (S025, S067, S079, S080, S081, S093).
4. **EchoSign Year 1: the $2M promise.** He planned $0 to $2M ARR in 12 months and hit about $200K. The team was dejected, a co-founder walked, and he closed BT, Dell, GE, and Qualcomm himself. Lesson: he got the freemium conversion metrics wrong coming from B2C, and he invented micromilestones, a nearer scoreboard the team can win, out of desperation (S034, S070, S092).
5. **SaaStr's sales team quits on site, then 20 agents.** Two high-paid reps quit around the 2025 SaaStr Annual, and he told his chief AI officer they were done hiring humans in sales. SaaStr went from zero agents at the end of Q1 2025 to about 20, with a human team of three. An AI SDR produced 25% of new pipeline in 90 days, and 70,000 agent emails drove 15% of revenue for one event. Lesson: the cause was exhaustion with turnover and overpaying, and the tool works only if you train it yourself (S037, S040, S047, S055, S072, S076, S084, S095). Newer counts: 21 agents in 2026-07 (S062).
6. **The rep who quit the day the RevOps tool tracked him.** An AE blamed the new AI RevOps tool, then on a live Zoom the data showed he had done nothing for 30 days and he quit that day. Lesson: tools expose people who were hiding, and SaaStr never put in the 30 days of training (S047, S055, S072, S076).
7. **The $10B company with the untrained AI SDR.** A public company's CMO planned to buy a $400,000 AI SDR tool and hand it to untrained SDRs, and a 20-person call had nobody who had trained an agent themselves. Lesson: you cannot delegate learning to juniors or an agency (S047, S055, S076, S064).
8. **The Marketo unsubscribe bug and the $14 migration.** Marketo left a bug in place for 10 days with no answer; a Replit-built page fixed it in 10 to 20 minutes. Migration quoted at about a year and $100,000 was done in an hour for about $14 with an LLM. Lesson: agents erase lock-in and make weak vendors look slow (S056, S057, S062). Newer (2026-07-17): "We are gone if you let us down." (S062)
9. **Vendor CEOs who just do the setup.** Vector's CEO set up the ads in 15 minutes on a call, Artisan sent an engineer while another vendor asked for $100K up front, and WorkOS offered to migrate him that day while Clerk argued the setup was his fault. Lesson: do the work for the customer; the vendor that deploys wins (S047, S057, S076, S084).
10. **Replacing 20 BDRs with two or three humans.** A friend's sales exec went from a $100K job to about $800K, down to $200K, and could not get a job. He pairs this with the $250,000 SDR who manages agents. Lesson: the market now pays the elite and drops the middle, so be a product expert (S047, S055, S068, S076).
11. **A $4M deal closed by a sales engineer.** The head of sales engineering at an AI-native dev-tools company closed a $4M+ annual deal himself and sales only priced it. Lesson: where the buyer values technical proof, the person who proves the value closes; he admits "I didn’t have a great answer for him." (S040)
12. **A portfolio company fires its whole sales team.** At about $5M revenue and growing over 100%, the founders thought sales was icky, fired the team, and fell below $1M. Lesson: self-serve versus sales-led is "when, not if"; do not run from customers who need sales (S046).
13. **The CEO who stopped taking sales calls.** A portfolio company with a seasoned VP of Sales hit $20M, the CEO left the calls, and it lost every complicated deal including an $800,000 one. A COO hired to replace the founder in sales failed too. Lesson: on complex products the founder cannot leave the deals; "you don't get one hour back." (S063, S077, S090)
14. **Pipedrive, Algolia, and Salesloft as proof.** Pipedrive's first rep out-earned the founders by calling multi-seat customers and still paid off; Algolia founders faked a Bay Area presence a week and a half a month from $12K MRR; Salesloft dumped an $8M product, fell to near zero, and sold for $2.3B to $2.5B. Lesson: let reps make money when they bring in more than they take, show up in person, and commitment beats early numbers (S046, S020, S052, S060, S091).
15. **The Rainforest QA price raise.** ARR went from about $100K to about $6M in 17 months after price per customer moved from about $300 a month toward $100K a year, found through diligence calls where two early customers said they would pay half the cost of an engineer. Lesson: price discovery happens in customer calls; ask your best customers if they would pay double (S079).
16. **EchoSign's free edition and the fax room.** EchoSign launched 100% free, built 100,000+ free ambassadors, and Adobe ripped the free edition out almost the day he left because nobody but the CEO owned it. A Comcast buyer repurposed an old fax room of 100 people and became a hero. Lesson: a product needs an owner, and give your champion an instant win (S021, S055).
17. **Boomerang founders and the stealth-churn Canva case.** Daniel Dines returned to UiPath four months after handing over; Dustin Moskovitz left Asana and the stock fell 25% overnight. He was a Canva power user from 2020 to 2025, then went 100 days without logging in while still paying. Lesson: a founder leaving at the wrong moment is a signal, and stealth churn hides in loved products (S041, S057).
18. **SaaStr's own events and the COVID cancellation.** Events looked cheap early ($800 per attendee in 2017 against about $10 at meetups) and broke each year without a strong owner; the 2020 Annual was cancelled and cost about $10M, four months with no revenue, and about half the team. He rebuilt a $4M to $5M digital business in a year and later said he would not start an event. Lesson: events have diseconomies of scale and are a trap as a revenue stream (S014, S049, S050, S053, S058, S078).
