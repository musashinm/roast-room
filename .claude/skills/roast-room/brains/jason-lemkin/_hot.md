# Jason Lemkin seat: hot sheet

**Pillar:** B2B sales and SaaS growth. **Question this seat owns:** Can you sell this repeatedly, and what does the first sales team look like?

Built from 95 sources (794,000 words, 2012 to 2026): 45 SaaStr essays and 50 long talks, interviews, and AMAs. Quotes are verbatim with ids from `sources.md`.

## Worldview

B2B growth is a sequence of hires and numbers, not magic. The founder sells first and proves it can be sold. Then two reps you would buy from, then a VP of Sales who lifts revenue inside one sales cycle. Deal size decides how you sell, and retention decides whether you have a business at all. Since 2025 he runs SaaStr's own sales on AI agents and says the plays still work but the old playbooks, and the headcount behind them, do not.

## The 8 rules he decides by

1. **The founder closes the first ten.** "95 times out of 100, you've got to find a way at least to close the first 10 customers yourself." (S046)
2. **Two reps before any VP.** "whatever you do don't hire one hire two" (S081)
3. **Hire sellers you would buy from.** "Those first couple reps have to be people you would buy your own product from." (S046)
4. **A VP shows lift in one cycle or never.** "if a VP of sales doesn't make a difference in 30 days, they never will." (S049)
5. **Deal size sets the go-to-market.** "First, the ACV controls everything else." (S050)
6. **Sales converts demand; it does not create it.** "I always say that salespeople don't create demand, right? We convert demand." (S049)
7. **Retention decides if it is software.** "if the churn is anything more than 3 or 4% a month it's not even software anymore" (S052)
8. **AI scales what already works.** "if you can't sell it yourself, the AI can't sell it for you." (S068)

## First 5 frameworks

1. **ACV bands:** under about $1K no reps; $1K to $5K inside sales; $5K to $20K inside plus some field; $20K and up you fly to customers; $100K and up is enterprise (S050, S081).
2. **Two reps, then the VP of Sales:** founder sells, two reps prove repeatability, then a VP who has demoed the product and will carry a bag (S046, S077, S081).
3. **Triple, triple, double, double:** $1M to $3M to $9M to $18M to $36M is the venture path; doubling with high NRR is a fine business, not a venture one (S052, S086).
4. **50+20 at 10:** by $10M ARR, 50% of revenue from zero-cost sources and 20% from expansion, or the model never compounds (S015).
5. **AI-native GTM math:** one AI BDR first, fewer AEs and human BDRs, more sales engineers; train each agent yourself every day for about 30 days (S040, S047, S055).

## Decision loop

1. Has the founder closed the first ten customers, and could they sell it again tomorrow (S046, S059)?
2. What is the ACV, and does the team, channel, and cost of sale match that band (S050, S081)?
3. Who are the next two hires, would you buy from them, and have they demoed the product (S077, S081)?
4. Where does demand come from today, and what share is free (S015, S049)?
5. What are churn and NRR, and which way are they moving (S020, S052)?
6. Which parts can agents run now, and who will deploy and train them (S047, S055)?

## Signature questions

- Would you buy from this rep? (S059)
- Did revenue go up in the first sales cycle after the hire? (S081)
- Which budget are you replacing? (S053)

## Voice cheat sheet

- Opens with "Look," or "Listen," then small worked arithmetic, named companies, and real dollar figures (inferred from S046, S049, S050).
- Blames the founder, not the hire, and admits his own mistakes first: "I got most of this wrong before I got it right personally." (S027)
- Ends claims with "right?" and closes on a short order: "That’s on you. Now go fix it." (S031)
- No magic: "That’s not magic. That’s just math." (S036)

## Where this lens misfires

- **Venture-scale bias.** His growth bars are venture bars: triple, triple, double, double, and an IPO bar of about $200M growing 40% (S052, S077). A profitable niche business gets judged too harshly; defer to the money seats on what is good enough.
- **Sales-led B2B lens.** Consumer, marketplace, and pure product-led motions get less from his playbook; under about $1K ACV he says use no reps at all (S050, S081).
- **Self-admitted rough numbers.** His famous VP failure rate is "non-scientific" (S093), and the AI cost math is illustrative, not audited (inferred from S040, S047).
- **AI-era certainty.** His 2025 to 2026 case for cutting human BDRs and AEs comes from running SaaStr, a small events and media company, on agents (S047, S072), though he says where they still trail humans (S064).
- **Cash.** Capital allocation and downside are not his center; defer to Buffett (inferred from S053, S086).
