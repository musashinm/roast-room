# Positions: Jason Lemkin

36 topics. Each stance has a verbatim quote, source id, and date (post date, or YouTube upload date for talks; see `sources.md`). Where he changed his mind the versions sit side by side with dates and the newer one is marked **newer**. Topics are ordered by how much of the corpus touches them, most-used first. Topics with no evidence say so.

## Hiring the first VP of Sales: when
- **Stance:** Founder sells first, then hire two reps (never one), get them to quota, then hire the VP. A VP monetizes demand and a working process; a VP cannot create either. Started at 10 customers, begin recruiting; hire when two reps hit quota, about $1M to $1.5M ARR. Cited by at least 14 sources (S004, S024, S031, S046, S049, S050, S059, S066, S071, S080, S081, S086, S092, S093).
- **Quotes:**
  - 2013-02-12: "you shouldn’t hire a VP Sales until you are ready to scale and build and fund a small, growing sales team." (S004)
  - 2014-03-31: "when you have 10 customers, you should start recruiting a VP sales, right?" (S093)
  - 2024-02-18: "If you hire a VP of sales before then, it's approaching 100% chance of failure." (S046)
  - 2020-11-05: "whatever you do don't hire one hire two" (S081)
- **Over time:** the failure rate he cites is "about 70% of the SaaS VP of sales don't make it 12 months" (S093, 2014-03-31), and he later admits the number is "non-scientific" (S093) and invented on a small sample (S090, 2024-09-19). In 2025 he adds a harder line: do not hire a VP at about $500K ARR, "I’ve seen this movie 30 times. It always ends the same way." (S036, 2025-11-14) and says he is contradicting his earlier "magical first rep" post (S036). **Newer (2025-11-14):** at that stage fix the funnel and have the founder stay in the deals; hire the first rep for follow-up and qualification, not closing.
- **Exception:** break the rule only if you truly cannot sell and have the best product. "unless you have no other choice unless you’re truly just have the world’s best product truly and you’re just incompetent at sales then break the rule" (S071, 2015-05-28).

## Hiring the first VP of Sales: who
- **Stance:** A closer who wants to sell, knows your product, has recruited at least a couple of good people who would follow, and sold at your ACV or next year's ACV. One stage ahead, not two. A stretch hire beats a veteran who is waiting for a later stage. 14 sources (S024, S025, S031, S032, S046, S063, S067, S077, S081, S083, S090, S093, S066, S027).
- **Quotes:**
  - 2023-07-22: "But a VP that can’t hire isn’t a real VP." (S031)
  - 2021-02-11: "Don't hire a VP of Sales that has only sold products that are easier to sell than yours, or less complicated, or less technical, or simpler" (S024)
  - 2023-11-23: "A VP of sales has to love closing. They have to be closers." (S067)
  - 2024-07-05: "do not hire a VP a sales that won't carry a bag that won't sell sell themselves" (S077)
  - 2020-11-05: "two stages is is too much" (S081)
- **Over time:** on carrying a bag he moved from unsure to firm. "I used to be a little a little nuanced on this." (S067, 2023-11-23); "I used to not be sure now I think it's 100% required" (S077, 2024-07-05). **Newer (2024-07-05):** mandatory. On blame: until about 2023 he blamed the VP, then "it’s my fault I hired him" (S090, 2024-09-19, paraphrase of his 11 to 12 year realization).
- **Read:** the through-line is that the last job was easier. "their last job was easier. That is almost always the main cause." (S050, 2021-02-28)

## Judging a VP of Sales: tilt the curve
- **Stance:** Give a new VP one sales cycle or one quarter to get more dollars out of the same leads (he sets the bar at about 20% more). If the curve has not tilted, fire, demote, or top them. 8 sources (S020, S030, S031, S049, S050, S081, S082, S083).
- **Quotes:**
  - 2023-03-31: "fire that VP, or demote them, or top them if you don’t see the curve tilted in one quarter ." (S030)
  - 2023-03-31: "every VP will be weak somewhere ." (S030)
  - 2022-08-25: "if a VP of sales doesn't make a difference in 30 days, they never will." (S049)
- **Over time:** 30 days (S049, S050; first stated years earlier and "hated" by readers, S049) became "one sales cycle" in the 2019 to 2023 talks (S020, S030, S081). In S083 (2019-12-11) he calls 20% lift enough and says not to hold "crazy expectations".

## Sales compensation
- **Stance:** Pay a lot, pay on performance, no cap, no long guaranteed draw. The first reps must be able to eat; reps should close about 3x to 5x what they earn. Pay the top 10 to 15% extremely well. A VP's pay ties to the company ARR goal until about $20M to $30M ARR. 11 sources (S024, S027, S029, S046, S049, S066, S067, S069, S079, S093, S094).
- **Quotes:**
  - 2022-05-02: "Try to avoid, or at least minimize, a guaranteed draw ." (S027)
  - 2022-05-02: "No cap ." (S027)
  - 2024-02-18: "What matters is, can a sales rep close more than they take home?" (S046)
  - 2023-11-23: "we obsess way too much about OTEs and not enough about attainment and what it takes." (S067)
  - 2022-09-05: "You don’t pay sales reps commissions upon renewals, ever." (S029)
- **Over time:** 2021: he calls the no-draw advice controversial: "Yes, I know some will disagree and this is controversial. Just my view." (S024, 2021-02-11). 2022-12-22: "hey sales reps if you want to make a hundred thousand dollars you have to close five hundred thousand if you want to make 200 000 you have to close a million" (S069). **Newer (2025-12-16):** elite sellers "should be paid compensation that is two to three times higher than it used to be." (S055); see the AI era entry for SDRs.

## Big-company hires and logo hiring
- **Stance:** Do not hire pure BigCo people into a pre-brand startup, and do not fall in love with the logo. The exceptions are late, with systems and data, roughly $8M to $12M ARR and beyond. 12 sources (S005, S020, S025, S046, S049, S050, S059, S066, S077, S083, S047, S068).
- **Quotes:**
  - 2013-07-01: "guys without any start-up experience are almost always a terrible fit for a true start-up." (S005)
  - 2019-12-12: "Well it never works and we know it never works" (S020)
  - 2024-07-05: "would you have still hired them if they didn't work there" (S077)
  - 2022-08-25: "don't fall in love with logos, right?" (S049)
- **Over time:** 2013 he admits his own BigCo hire at about $2M ARR was a costly mistake, the fault was his own (S005, paraphrase), though a senior BigCo exec at $12M just killed it (S005, paraphrase). **Newer (2026-01-01):** the old-playbook VP who "worked at Slack" does not automatically have the skills, and he guesses only about 20% make the jump (S047).

## SDRs and AI SDRs
- **Stance:** The human email-cadence SDR role is a high-churn entry role he now wants replaced by agents, but agents scale only what already works. Cited by 20+ sources (S020, S037, S040, S046, S047, S055, S063, S064, S068, S072, S073, S074, S076, S084, S095, S092, S045, S057, S019, S049).
- **Era 1, before 2025 (skeptic):**
  - 2020: outsourced SDRs are a "B-minus job" for ordinary products and "it doesn’t go from low productivity, it just goes to zero" for quirky ones (S020, 2019-12-12).
  - 2023-12-21: the SDR "is a hyper entry-level, high-churn role" (S063) and "everyone thinks that AI will radically change what the SDR does." (S063).
  - April 2024: "AI can't beat a good sales rep. No way. It's dumb. It hallucinates." (S074 reports his own old view, 2025-05-28).
- **Era 2, from mid 2025 (convert):**
  - 2025-05-28: "I missed the point that faster day SDRs do work." (S074)
  - 2025-11-20: "If your outbound didn’t work with humans, AI won’t save it." (S037)
  - 2025-12-04: "Is it as good as our best human BDR? Not quite. But it’s better than our average BDR." (S040)
  - 2026-01-01: "The emailbased cadence SDR will be 90% displaced by AI next year." (S047)
  - 2026-07-08: the BDR/SDR role should "go extinct as soon as humanly possible" (S084).
- **Why he converted:** two high-paid reps quit on site at the 2025 SaaStr Annual (S047, S055, S084). "We didn’t do this to increase revenue or to save money. We did it because we were burnt out with people" (S095, 2025-10-05). Cost reference: "I just can't pay a junior SDR $150,000 a year to quit." (S047)
- **Read (inferred from S047, S055, S076):** his case is for a small team that cannot hire the best; he says a large team with a big budget "maybe I would come to a different conclusion." (S055, 2025-12-16)

## Account executives and the size of the sales team
- **Stance:** Sales has no efficiency of scale. In the classic era he held that you need headcount in line with revenue; in the AI era he says agents replace the middle of the pack, not the best. 14 sources (S016, S028, S046, S047, S055, S068, S069, S072, S074, S076, S090, S040, S084, S073).
- **Quotes:**
  - 2018-02-28: "Sales gets more expensive as you add customers. Engineering generally goes the other way, if you are doing it right." (S016)
  - 2022-05-27: "If you want to double, you have to at least double your sales team. At least." (S028)
  - 2024-02-18: "I'd much rather have two reps, each closing a million than 20 reps struggling to close 100k each." (S046)
- **Over time (two eras):**
  - Classic, 2022 to 2024: "you will need to hire headcount infinitely linearly with revenue and you can't do it without help" (S090, 2024-09-19); "half your company is going to be in sales at 10 millionaire, half your company is going to be in sales at 50, half your company is going to be in sales at 100." (S046, 2024-02-18)
  - **Newer, 2025 to 2026:** "AI is replacing the jobs people don't want to do today, and it is displacing the midpack and the mediocre." (S047, 2026-01-01); "Our AI agents are better than a midpack AE or SDR or BDR." (S055, 2025-12-16); "not better than the best. And so we just don't need them." (S055). He floats a 30 to 40% smaller team if productivity is truly 3x (S076, 2026-01-21).
  - The new top-end role: the sales engineer who closes. "The SE should get paid like an AE on that one. Full stop." (S040, 2025-12-04). Limit: "The further removed your customers are from tech, the less true this will be." (S040)

## Deploying AI agents in go to market
- **Stance:** Buy, do not build; deploy and train it yourself, daily, for about a month; pick one or two vendors; know who at the vendor will deploy; put one internal "nerd" in charge; start with inbound. 12 sources (S037, S047, S055, S056, S057, S064, S065, S068, S072, S073, S076, S095).
- **Quotes:**
  - 2025-11-20: "Never deploy more agents than you can actively manage" (S037)
  - 2025-12-16: "don't sign the contract until you talk to your forward deployed engineer or sales agent." (S055)
  - 2026-04-22: "your agents will will drift, they'll take shortcuts, and they'll and they'll in their own QA they'll lie." (S057)
  - 2026-04-08: "the 90/10 rule applies. Buy 90%, vibe 10% at most." (S065)
  - 2025-10-09: "This is one of the biggest lies in a lot of AI B2B applications that they magically work out of the box without training." (S072)
- **Over time:** 2025-08-18: "You have to fire all of them. Fire them all." about staff who will not go on the AI journey (S073). 2026-04-29 on cost: SaaStr pays more for agents than for its CRM (S056, S072, S076). He dates the claim: "This is a snapshot of what works in May 2026, not a permanent law of the universe." (S043, 2026-05-20)
- **Read (inferred from S037, S055, S064):** "if you can't sell it yourself, the AI can't sell it for you." (S068, 2025-12-02) is the filter he applies before any agent purchase.

## Outbound versus inbound
- **Stance:** Outbound works when it is great and tied to a top need; the mass-email playbook is dead. Inbound is the first place to automate. Leads are precious. 12 sources (S011, S028, S066, S068, S069, S092, S095, S063, S079, S093, S045, S076).
- **Quotes:**
  - 2022-05-27: "Outbound always works. You just have to do it right." (S028)
  - 2024-12-13: "that old Playbook of just load up a database with the same freaking email is dead" (S092)
  - 2025-10-05: "you have to read your sales team's emails because they're probably terrible." (S095)
  - 2025-12-02: "So no inbound isn't dead." (S068)
- **Over time:** 2016: "A top-tier SDR would NEVER do this." about blasting key accounts (S011, 2016-03-03). 2023-12-21: "actually talk to 50 great prospects a week. That always works." (S063). **Newer (2025 to 2026):** AI outbound beat most human SDRs: "Even a year ago, the AI emails were better than almost any human SDR we’d worked with" (S045, 2026-10-24).

## Marketing leader and demand generation
- **Stance:** Hire a VP Marketing or demand gen who owns a lead number early, about $20K MRR, before or with the VP of Sales. Do not put marketing under the CRO or sales. 11 sources (S007, S049, S050, S059, S069, S077, S078, S080, S092, S054, S063).
- **Quotes:**
  - 2022-08-25: "I hired my VP of Marketing with $20K in MRR." (S049)
  - 2022-12-22: "this is not ahead of demand gen that has a number that has a commitment to the sales team" (S069)
  - 2026-10-07: "Well first of all I think it's a terrible idea." (S078, on marketing under the CRO)
  - 2024-07-05: "when you cut salespeople you cut your present when you cut marketing you cut your future" (S077)
- **Over time:** in 2013 and 2014 the notes only record that he opposed agencies instead of a VP (S007). By 2024 to 2026 he says before 10 customers do not hire a brand or product marketer, hire someone who generates leads (S077, 2024-07-05).

## Customer success and retention
- **Stance:** Invest in CS earlier than feels right; most lifetime revenue comes after the first deal; keep CS out of sales. Second-most important metric after growth is revenue retention. 18 sources (S006, S015, S020, S033, S042, S046, S049, S057, S059, S063, S065, S069, S077, S078, S081, S083, S086, S093).
- **Quotes:**
  - 2013-12-04: "the great VPs of Customer Success put customers first, revenue second." (S006)
  - 2019-12-12: "ultimately your second most important metric is going to be revenue retention." (S020)
  - 2023-12-21: "But when CS reports to sales, all CS does is try to get more money out of you." (S063)
  - 2026-10-07: "There's no one that cares about the customer anymore when CS is part of sales and there's no controls." (S078)
- **Over time:** 2017: hire "50% more than plan" CSMs from $1M to $10M ARR (S015, 2017-07-18). 2023-12-21: "I think if you have humans in a renewal you're you have failed your customers." (S063). **Newer (2026):** agents handle the installed base (a 70% cut in human hours, S043) but fear of repeating the old mistake: "this is why customer success was cut to zero and repurposed into a sales function because it was viewed it as a cost center" (S057, 2026-04-22).

## Churn, NRR, and SMB versus enterprise
- **Stance:** Net revenue retention is the long-run engine. Mid-market and enterprise carry growth on 110% to 120%. Monthly churn above 3% to 4% is not software. SMB must be self-serve and product-led. 16 sources (S009, S015, S020, S033, S049, S050, S051, S052, S053, S059, S069, S083, S019, S008, S064, S046).
- **Quotes:**
  - 2024-05-27: "if the churn is anything more than 3 or 4% a month it's not even software anymore" (S052)
  - 2024-05-27: "it has to be self- Ser it has to be plg it's not a choice it's a requirement" (S052)
  - 2024-06-03: "we learned that generally High nrr lasts forever it lasts to a billion two billion 10 billion but we also learned the CAC never comes down" (S053)
  - 2014-07-07: "SMBs are not early adopters, at least not of solutions (vs. tools)." (S008)
- **Over time:** 2019 he cited SMB-heavy firms at 140% retention (S020, S083). 2024 he says triple-digit NRR in SMB with small deals is "very hard" (S051, 2024-01-04). 2022-12-22: at 8% monthly churn "you’re not sass you’re not business software you’re at the edge of consumer software" (S069). **Newer (2026-04-29):** stealth churn is "the number one most important in B2B" (S056), i.e. customers still paying but not logging in.

## ACV and deal size
- **Stance:** Deal size sets the whole go to market. Below about $20K to $30K you cannot afford field reps or Bay Area sales teams. Raise ACV before anything else. 14 sources (S002, S008, S009, S016, S048, S052, S063, S079, S086, S093, S019, S065, S067, S066).
- **Quotes:**
  - 2012-10-31: "Deal Size is the single most important factor in your SaaS business model." (S002)
  - 2018-02-28: "an SMB rep may struggle to close $350k a year on $3k deals." (S016)
  - 2015-06-24: "Selling 20 seats at $20 / month in revenue is worth far, far more than 1 seat at $5 / month." (S009)
  - 2023-12-21: "What's 10K * 10,000 customers? It's a pretty good business." (S063)
- **Over time:** 2014 the floor was $300 a month (S008); 2015 he walks away below $7, $5, or $3 a month (S048); 2016 most small businesses will pay $20K a year for the one core system (S079, 2016-08-02); 2023 target $10K in vertical SaaS (S063).

## Pricing and price increases
- **Stance:** Founders underprice. Test price early, raise it, and copy the bigger competitor instead of getting clever. But do not raise prices on a base without adding value. 14 sources (S009, S028, S036, S044, S051, S052, S062, S063, S069, S077, S081, S089, S042, S075).
- **Quotes:**
  - 2015-06-24: "Even if your conversion rates plummet until you roll back, you’ll learn" (S009)
  - 2023-12-21: "my first principle is copy whoever's bigger and like you." (S063)
  - 2023-12-21: "nine times out of 10 innovation innovating on pricing is a terrible idea because it confuses your market." (S063)
  - 2025-11-14: "Your rep can’t fix your pricing strategy." (S036)
  - 2024-04-10: "SaaS became software as a rip-off. Everyone got massive price increases for no benefit" (S089)
- **Over time:** 2022-12-22 do not raise prices on early customers (S069). 2022-05-27: "In the scaling phase, I vote for leaving existing customers be." (S028). 2024 he blames serial hikes for burned trust: "everyone raised prices without earning it" (S077, 2024-07-05). 2026-07-17: raising a vendor's price needs value: "A tenth the value, three times the price." (S062)

## New pricing models in the AI era
- **Stance:** **Newer (2026-08-25):** seats are dying but not going to zero; add a second line priced on consumption, outcome, or resolution into one segment, and do not convert the installed base in one move. 4 sources (S044, S063, S068, S075).
- **Quotes:**
  - 2026-08-25: "Do not convert the base, in general." (S044)
  - 2026-08-25: "Any model that bills the org chart drifts further from the value every quarter from here." (S044)
  - 2026-08-25: "Seats aren’t going to zero." (S044)
- **Earlier (2023-12-21):** "AI is extremely expensive" may force new models, flagged as a guess (S063, paraphrase). Admits uncertainty: "You don’t have to know where pricing ends up." (S044)

## Discounting, free pilots, and freemium
- **Stance:** Paid pilots beat free ones; never give free pilots in the enterprise; free is a brand army and not a marketing strategy. 9 sources (S021, S028, S059, S080, S087, S069, S020, S048, S095).
- **Quotes:**
  - 2022-05-27: "paid pilots are so, so much better for most of us." (S028)
  - 2020-03-21: "It’s an army of brand ambassadors. Often 10x or more larger than your paid army." (S021)
  - 2018-06-21: "just remember free is not a marketing strategy" (S059)
  - 2025-10-05: "Pilots still work folks." (S095)
- **Over time:** 2015: "the idea that what drove SAS was try before you buy is a bit apocryphal" (S048, 2015-11-20). On discounting, a nervous CEO gives 50% off and a VP gives 10% (S020, paraphrase, 2019-12-12).

His market read: growth is the only thing that matters to investors in the AI era. 18 sources (S010, S020, S048, S051, S052, S053, S055, S059, S060, S063, S069, S071, S072, S073, S075, S083, S084, S089).

  - 2022-12-22: "you kind of have to assume today that if you do raise capital that that might be it for you" (S069)

(S072, 2025-10-09).

- **Quotes:**

  - 2012-09-28: "if you sell, then whoever buys it, owns it." (S001)
  - 2022-08-25: "even before the acquisition was finalized, I knew it was a mistake." (S049)
  - 2024-01-04: "some of the worst advice I've given to Founders is when to seller their company" (S051)

## Metrics: growth, 50+20, burn, and L4M
- **Stance:** Growth is king, revenue retention second; keep quarterly burn below that quarter's net new ARR; judge growth against competitors. 14 sources (S003, S015, S017, S020, S033, S034, S051, S052, S053, S083, S091, S069, S077, S088).
- **Quotes:**
  - 2017-07-18: "have that max quarterly burn rate always be less than your new ARR add that quarter" (S015)
  - 2024-05-27: "even for startups an l4m model is great take the last four months and average almost any metric" (S052)
  - 2024-07-05: "growth is still worth twice as much as profitability for startups it's probably four to 10 times" (S077)
  - 2012-11-28: "Measure everything. Publish the results. And watch them magically grow" (S003)

## Remote work and distributed sales
- **Stance:** Distributed sales worked for small deals; sales is the last function to distribute; visit customers. 12 sources (S020, S046, S049, S050, S054, S059, S067, S073, S076, S083, S087, S028).
