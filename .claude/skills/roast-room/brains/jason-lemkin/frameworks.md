# Frameworks: Jason Lemkin

25 frameworks, roughly most-used first. "Sources" counts distinct sources where the idea is used (ids listed). Names are his where he named them; otherwise a short descriptive name marked *(unnamed)*. The How-to-score lines are scoring guidance for the council built from his stated thresholds, not quotes. Built from 95 sources (SaaStr posts 2012 to 2026 and talks 2014 to 2026). His sales-team advice has two eras: the SaaS playbook (2012 to 2024) and the AI-agent era (2025 to 2026). Entries 20 to 25 are the newer era, and where an older rule was revised the entry says so.

---

## 1. Two reps first, then the VP of Sales
- **What it is:** Do not hire a VP of Sales until two reps you hired are hitting quota. Before that there is no repeatable process for the VP to scale. The VP's job is to take what works with two reps and make it work for reps 3 to 300.
- **Parts:** The founder acts as the first VP of Sales. Hire two reps, not one, so you can A/B test people. Both must be hitting quota. Start courting VP candidates months early, then hire.
- **When to use:** A founder asks "when do I hire a VP of Sales?" or wants to hand sales to someone before there is an engine.
- **How to score:** 5 if two ramped reps hit quota and the lead-to-revenue ratio is known (he gives about $1M to $1.5M ARR, 50 to 100 customers). 3 if one rep is working. 1 if the plan is to hire a VP to create the process.
- **Older and newer:** In 2014 he said to start recruiting a VP at 10 customers (S093). In 2021 he said a good VP will not join before roughly $10M ARR (S066). In 2025 (newer) he said not to hire a VP Sales at about $500K ARR at all, because the founder sells first (S036).
- **Quote:** "two reps that are hitting quota and then you hire VP of sales. If you hire it before then, you're doing a Hail Mary." (S046)
- **Sources:** 10 (S004, S024, S046, S059, S066, S071, S080, S081, S082, S092)

## 2. The founder closes the first ten customers and never leaves sales
- **What it is:** A hired seller cannot conjure the first sales. The founder closes the first 10 to 20 customers, learns the script and the mistakes, then hands over a motion that works. After the hire, the founder's time in sales stays the same; the role changes from opener to middler.
- **Parts:** Close the first 10 yourself. Hire the first help at customer 11. Stay in every deal on a complex product. Ask each rep for a two-meetings-a-week quota that drags you in.
- **When to use:** A founder wants to hire a salesperson, a CRO or a COO to "get sales going".
- **How to score:** 5 if the founder has personally closed 10 or more customers and can write down the demo, objections and price points. 2 if fewer than 5. 1 if the hire is meant to find product-market fit.
- **Newer (2025):** The founder owns sales and carries the quota until $2 to 3M ARR, and builds inbound to 30 to 40 qualified demos a month before hiring several AEs (S036). The first AE is for replication, not invention.
- **Quote:** "95 times out of 100, you've got to find a way at least to close the first 10 customers yourself." (S046)
- **Sources:** 13 (S020, S036, S046, S059, S063, S072, S077, S081, S082, S083, S086, S090, S093)

## 3. Deal size (ACV) sets the whole go-to-market
- **What it is:** Average annual deal size decides who you can hire, how you sell, what a lead is worth and where you can afford to hire. Price is only one variable in deal size.
- **Parts:** Under about $1,000: no salespeople, freemium or word of mouth. About $1,000 to $5,000: efficient inside sales. $5,000 to $20,000: inside sales plus some field. $20,000 and up: inside plus field, and you can fly to customers. Above $100,000: enterprise. In 2020 he gave $3 to 5K transactional, $20 to 40K consultative, six figures on planes (S081). In 2018 he put the cut-off for scaling sales in the Bay Area at $30K ACV (S016).
- **When to use:** Any question on hiring, channel, price or whether outbound can pay back.
- **How to score:** 5 if the team, channel and cost of sale all match one ACV band. 2 if the product is priced in the gap between bands (his example: $30 a month sits between a tool and a solution, S008). 1 if the plan needs field sales at a sub-$20K ACV.
- **Quote:** "you pretty much sell products at a given ACV the same way" (S081)
- **Sources:** 10 (S002, S016, S025, S033, S049, S050, S059, S065, S081, S086)

## 4. A marketing leader who owns a number, hired before or with the VP of Sales
- **What it is:** More leads is the fastest route to more revenue early. His order: VP Marketing first, then VP Sales, then VP Product. The one test for the marketing hire is whether they held and hit a number.
- **Parts:** Ask what lead, MQL or pipeline commit they held and when they missed it. Look at the first line of their LinkedIn: a strategist or brand title is a pass, a percent-of-commit line is a hire. Hire the demand-gen person before the brand person until you have 1,000 to 2,000 customers. In 2026 he asked every CEO to force-rank the top three things they want from marketing (S078).
- **When to use:** Growth is flat and the founder wants a CRO or more AEs, or the first marketing hire is on the table.
- **How to score:** 5 if the candidate names a number and a miss. 3 if they describe channels but no commit. 1 if they cannot say what their commit was ("I'll have to get back to you" is a no).
- **Quote:** "They have to have hit and held a number." (S020)
- **Sources:** 10 (S020, S049, S050, S059, S069, S077, S078, S080, S083, S092)

## 5. Would I buy from them? (the buy-from test)
- **What it is:** For the first reps, the single cheat code is whether you would buy your own product from them. Test it by having them sell your product for real before you hire.
- **Parts:** Interview about 20 to 30 people. First meeting is a pitch-style conversation. In the second, they demo your product. They must have sold at your ACV. Mid-pack is fine for rep one and two; the hire must have a couple of years of real SaaS experience.
- **When to use:** Hiring the first two to six reps, or any sales hire who has not learned the product.
- **How to score:** 5 if you would buy from them and they demoed well. 1 if you would not, whatever else is good. He says a no here "never works".
- **Quote:** "Those first couple reps have to be people you would buy your own product from." (S046)
- **Sources:** 8 (S036, S046, S059, S066, S067, S077, S088, S090)

## 6. What a VP of Sales actually does (top five) and tilting the curve
- **What it is:** The VP is a recruiter and process manager, not mainly a seller. Ranked: recruiting (20% or more of their time), backfilling and helping the team close, tactics, strategy, and closing deals themselves last. They are hired to inflect the curve in one cycle.
- **Parts:** Raise revenue per lead. Great VP lifts output by 20% or more, good VP holds velocity, mediocre one lets it fall, bad one sees it fall off a cliff in a quarter. Marketing's version is leads in one sales cycle; customer success's is NPS in one quarter.
- **When to use:** Scoping the job, writing the scorecard, or deciding whether to keep a VP.
- **How to score:** 5 if revenue per lead is up 20% or more on the same leads after one cycle. 3 if flat. 1 if the pipeline "looks great" but nothing has closed.
- **Quote:** "number one job is getting 8 to 10 great people on your team not 8 to 10 mediocre people" (S081)
- **Sources:** 7 (S004, S024, S030, S081, S082, S086, S093)

## 7. The two-hires test (and the reference check)
- **What it is:** Before hiring a VP of Sales, ask for the two people they personally recruited who then hit quota. Call both and ask how they were found. If the story falls apart, walk away.
- **Parts:** Ask for the first three people they would bring with them. Inherited teams, being promoted from SDR or being on a team do not count. Also check a customer or two. In 2024 he said 95% of people no longer do reference checks (S046).
- **When to use:** Any VP or head-of-function hire.
- **How to score:** 5 if two named hires hit quota and would follow them. 2 if one. 0 if none; he says a VP who cannot recruit cannot do the job.
- **Quote:** "they have to have found hired two people and made them successful" (S090)
- **Sources:** 9 (S020, S024, S032, S059, S067, S081, S082, S083, S090)

## 8. The 30-day / one-sales-cycle test
- **What it is:** A new VP of Sales who makes no visible difference in 30 days never will. Give them the same leads and one sales cycle; if revenue does not rise (he says at least 20%), it will not change. Gains come from better closing, discounting and urgency, not new leads.
- **Parts:** In the first 30 days they should work the existing pipeline 40 to 50 hours a week, rescue forgotten deals, coach the weak AE, hire or recruit one or two better reps. A first 30 days spent on process is a sign it will fail (S031).
- **When to use:** Deciding whether to keep, back or replace a VP.
- **How to score:** 5 if closed deals and recruiting improve inside 30 days. 3 if pipeline talk only. 1 if excuses at 90 days.
- **Quote:** "if the VP of sales doesn't make a difference in 30 days, he never will; it'll never work" (S050)
- **Sources:** 10 (S020, S030, S031, S049, S050, S069, S081, S082, S083, S089)

## 9. Stretch, not wash-up (stage and ACV fit)
- **What it is:** The perfect "Goldilocks" VP does not exist for your stage. A famous-logo hire is usually a wash-up who fails in one cycle to nine months. The best compromise is a one-level stretch (director to VP) who has sold at your ACV.
- **Parts:** Never a double stretch (best AE to VP with no hiring record). Hire at most one stage above you. Four stages: Evangelist, Mr. Make-it-Repeatable, Ms. Go Big, Mr. Dashboards. He counted 48 types by stage, competitive history, deal size and lead type. Do not bring a pure big-company person into a pre-brand startup.
- **When to use:** Comparing VP candidates, or tempted by a big logo.
- **How to score:** 5 if one-level stretch, sold at next year's ACV, recruited two reps. 3 if proven at a bigger stage. 1 if hired for the logo or never managed anyone.
- **Quote:** "One level stretch is the risk we should be taking as founders." (S020)
- **Sources:** 12 (S020, S025, S027, S032, S046, S049, S050, S059, S067, S081, S083, S090)

## 10. Quota-to-pay multiples and early comp plans
- **What it is:** A rep must close several times what they take home: 3x for SMB, 4x for mid-market (deals of $20K to $40K), 5x for enterprise. The whole sales team, managers and SDRs included, should bring in about 3x its cost.
- **Parts:** Early reps can keep what they close for one quarter or so. Pay about 20 to 25% of ACV with no commission until base plus benefits is earned back. Never commission renewals. VP Sales comp (50/50/25+): 50% of OTE as base with little or no draw, 50% bonus tied to a company revenue number he is 50 to 60% confident of hitting, 25% or more upside with no cap, paid monthly. One manager per eight AEs and ten SDRs.
- **When to use:** Designing the first comp plan or checking if a team is affordable.
- **How to score:** 5 if most reps can hit quota and the team returns 3x or more. 2 if only a few hit. 1 if quota is a number nobody reaches. Treat as guidance for SaaS sales; he cited these as pre-2019 norms, then said in 2023 "we're back to three to five x OTE" (S091).
- **Quote:** "it's got to be 3x to 5x at a minimum, 3x for small businesses, 4x for mid-market" (S046)
- **Sources:** 11 (S020, S024, S027, S029, S046, S066, S067, S069, S083, S091, S093)

## 11. Customer success ahead of the load ($2M per CSM)
- **What it is:** Hire customer success before you think you need it, segment by deal size, and anchor at one CSM per $2M ARR at scale (nearer $1M earlier). Big accounts get an in-person visit; mid accounts get a CSM who knows their process.
- **Parts:** First CSM as soon as you have two big customers. Spend up to 10% of revenue on customer success in growth mode, about 5% at scale. Segment NRR by customer size. Customer marketing is the most important part of marketing.
- **When to use:** Churn or expansion is the question, or a founder wants to cut customer success to hit a margin target.
- **How to score:** 5 if a named owner holds a retention number and top customers are visited each quarter. 2 if customer success is a ticket desk. 1 if the seat is vacant.
- **Quote:** "when in doubt over invest in customer success" (S083)
- **Sources:** 8 (S015, S032, S033, S059, S080, S083, S086, S091)

## 12. Impossible to Inevitable (zero to one, one to ten, ten to a hundred)
- **What it is:** Zero to one is impossible, one to ten is unlikely, ten to a hundred is inevitable if you have happy customers and positive net revenue retention. Once you reach about $10M of recurring revenue, a company takes years to die.
- **Parts:** Before about 50 customers only product-market fit matters; after, execution from 50 to 5,000. Do nothing that does not add revenue until $10M. He says he regrets selling EchoSign at $10M ARR with 120 to 140% retention.
- **When to use:** Judging a company's stage and what to ignore.
- **How to score:** 5 if growing fast with negative churn and NPS above 40. 2 if flat NRR. His revision is below.
- **Revised (2025, newer):** With AI competition the move from $10M to $100M "used to be almost inevitable if you had high NR and a good team" and no longer is (S073).
- **Quote:** "Going from 0 to 1 is impossible. From 1 to 10 it's unlikely, but from 10 to 100" (S050)
- **Sources:** 6 (S049, S050, S059, S071, S073, S085)

## 13. Triple, triple, double, double (and sequencing the next act)
- **What it is:** The growth path a venture-backed company needs: $1M to $3M to $9M, then $18M and $36M. Below it you are not fundable by venture. He credits the phrase to Niraj of Battery in one source (S091).
- **Parts:** The best seed investments go from $1M to $10M in five quarters or less (about 15% month over month, S086). At about 10% of your core market, add a second product, a broader ICP, a move upmarket or a new country. Add the second product before growth stalls, and make it bigger than the first by about 10,000 customers.
- **When to use:** Raising money, planning a second product, or deciding if "good growth" is good enough.
- **How to score:** 5 if on the path or ahead. 3 if doubling with high NRR (fine for a business, not for venture). 1 if growth is falling while the market share is rising.
- **Newer (2025 to 2026):** He says VCs show no interest in classic SaaS growing at pretty good rates (S072, S075), and to IPO today you need about $200M growing 40% or more (S077).
- **Quote:** "you can't IPO unless you triple triple double double" (S052)
- **Sources:** 10 (S052, S053, S055, S061, S075, S077, S079, S086, S089, S091)

## 14. 50+20 at 10, and the mini brand
- **What it is:** By $10M ARR, 50% or more of revenue should come from zero-cost sources (word of mouth, brand, referrals) and 20% or more from expansion. Miss it and you have a capital-inefficient business.
- **Parts:** At a couple million in revenue a niche company gets a mini brand and leads arrive free. Then measure the all-in cost to close; healthy is 30 to 40% of first-year revenue. Only then raise to buy the more expensive customers. Target 120% cohort growth.
- **When to use:** Deciding how much capital to raise or whether CAC will fall by itself.
- **How to score:** 5 if half of deals are inbound and expansion is 20% or more. 2 if all growth is paid. He noted in 2024 that companies layer paid on top, so blended CAC never falls (S053).
- **Quote:** "Because you have to hit 50+20 by $10m in ARR for SaaS to really compound." (S015)
- **Sources:** 5 (S015, S048, S053, S080, S086)

## 15. Churn and net revenue retention thresholds
- **What it is:** Retention decides the model. Enterprise should be above 110% NRR by $1M ARR or he passes. SMB can live with a few percent monthly churn only with self-serve and low CAC.
- **Parts:** Above 3 to 4% monthly churn it is not software. At 3% a month and $10 to 20M you need double-digit monthly growth to refill the bucket. 120% NRR at scale means about 20% growth before you sell anything. Gross margin below 70% is not really software. Judge churn by its direction as well as its level. Collect more than 100% of your MRR.
- **When to use:** Evaluating a SaaS business, an SMB motion, or a sales-led plan with leaky retention.
- **How to score:** 5 if NRR is above 110% for enterprise, or SMB churn is falling toward 100% NRR. 1 if SMB churn is above 4% a month with sales reps in the loop ("unsustainable").
- **Quote:** "if the churn is anything more than 3 or 4% a month it's not even software anymore" (S052)
- **Sources:** 7 (S015, S017, S042, S052, S058, S059, S089)

## 16. Tool versus solution, and raise the price
- **What it is:** SMBs buy cheap tools but few solutions. A solution is the system of record the customer is in several times a day, priced at $300 a month or more. Price sitting in the middle gets stuck.
- **Parts:** Seven exits from the SMB price trap: raise prices (try 2x), sell team deals, go upmarket with an enterprise package, localize, add a second product, upgrade the team, test price early. Anchor to comparable products and charge the same or 10 to 20% less. Raise price only when value goes up. A seller of the same effort at a higher ACV is the fastest accelerator.
- **When to use:** Pricing a new product or when growth depends on price per customer.
- **How to score:** 5 if buyers see it as indispensable and price sits near comps. 2 if cheap with heavy support. 1 if $5 to $30 a month with an inside team. Math: $300 a month to SMBs needs about 25,000 customers for $100M (S008).
- **Quote:** "You just gotta raise prices . Even if the competition is cheaper. Even if your customers say they can’t afford it." (S009)
- **Sources:** 7 (S008, S009, S010, S063, S079, S086, S089)

## 17. Paid pilots and friction removal
- **What it is:** Take friction out of buying from first look to renewal. Use paid, pre-packaged pilots; never free pilots in the enterprise.
- **Parts:** Publish a standard pilot (his example: 90 days at about $5,000 a month with a named team). Let customers downgrade. Do not gate everything behind a contact-sales form until tens of millions of revenue. Close fast. Do not allow price or budget as the reason for a lost deal.
- **When to use:** Long cycles, stalled enterprise deals, or a team that blames price.
- **How to score:** 5 if a buyer can start in a week with a clear price. 2 if every deal is custom. He says pilots still work in the AI era (S095).
- **Quote:** "free pilots are a horrific idea in the enterprise they're terrible for both sides" (S087)
- **Sources:** 6 (S063, S074, S080, S087, S089, S095)

## 18. Opener, middler, closer (specialize the team)
- **What it is:** Selling has three jobs: find the customer, run the demo, ask for the money. Customer success or happiness staff can middle but cannot open or close. A great VP Sales takes over opening and closing so the founder can be a dedicated middler.
- **Parts:** Specialize after about two reps by size, vertical and time frame. A sales engineer is needed when the CEO or CTO still joins calls for technical answers: first SE by AE number three, ratio about 1 to 8. In 2025 he added that the SE who closes should be paid like an AE (S040).
- **When to use:** Mixing roles, or deciding who handles a lead above about $5K ACV.
- **How to score:** 5 if roles are split and leads are routed by size. 1 if one person does all three at an ACV above $5K.
- **Quote:** "Happiness Officers are Great Middlers. But Not Openers or Closers." (S023)
- **Sources:** 7 (S023, S040, S049, S050, S077, S080, S093)

## 19. Runway and planning (zero cash date, L4M, C60/C90/C10)
- **What it is:** Know the date you run out of money and plan on measured confidence levels.
- **Parts:** Zero cash date is cash divided by trailing three-month burn. L4M averages the last four months of growth, burn and churn to predict the next six to nine. C60 plan is the base case, C90 the cash case, C10 the stretch. Keep about $1 in the bank per $2 of ARR to fund hires that pay back in 3 to 6 months. With 12 to 30 months of cash, do not do layoffs; add six months by cutting weak marketing and side projects.
- **When to use:** Any budget, cut or hiring decision.
- **How to score:** 5 if the zero cash date and a no-new-cash plan exist. 1 if the plan assumes the next round.
- **Quote:** "know you’re zero cash date, when you run out of money and track it every month." (S020)
- **Sources:** 7 (S020, S052, S060, S069, S080, S083, S088)

## 20. AI inning one: clone the best human first
- **What it is:** AI sales agents scale what already works and do not fix what is broken. Prove the messaging, ICP and process with humans, then copy your best person at scale.
- **Parts:** Take the best rep or marketer's emails as the template. Agents A/B test well. Agents only need to beat a mediocre human, and look for areas where they beat any human. Lead-rich teams stop following up; agents recover those leads (one source cites a 70% response rate from Agentforce on leads reps had dropped, S047).
- **When to use:** A founder asks whether to buy an AI SDR.
- **How to score:** 5 if the founder has sold it by hand and outbound works with humans. 1 if no motion exists yet; stop.
- **Newer (2025 to 2026):** Early 2024 AI could not beat a good rep; by April 2026 he claimed "our AI is better than 80% of reps" (S074).
- **Quote:** "AI SDRs scale what works. They don’t fix what’s broken." (S037)
- **Sources:** 8 (S037, S047, S055, S064, S068, S072, S074, S084)

## 21. Deploy it yourself: one agent, a forward-deployed engineer, two vendors
- **What it is:** Pick one painful problem, pick one tool, and train it yourself for about 30 days. Never hand the training to an agency.
- **Parts:** Budget 20 to 60 hours of the leader's time and $50K to $100K. QA daily for an hour or two. Choose a vendor by who will deploy it: talk to the forward-deployed engineer before signing. Two-vendor bake-off, not eight. Start with inbound or support. Seven-step rollout: what works, one use case, one or two vendors, best SDR learns with it, 90 days of daily management, empower from draft to autonomous, scale. Never deploy more agents than you can manage.
- **When to use:** First AI GTM deployment.
- **How to score:** 5 if a named leader reads output daily. 1 if the plan is buy and delegate. He says no set and forget (S065).
- **Quote:** "Do it yourself. Do it, watch it, iterate it, train it, iterate every day." (S068)
- **Sources:** 7 (S037, S047, S055, S068, S072, S073, S076)

## 22. Steal or be stolen (where the budget comes from)
- **What it is:** In the AI era most new software spend is substitution. A buyer cancels something to pay for you, so know which budget line you replace.
- **Parts:** Three budget sources: replace humans, augment humans dramatically, or be a vendor allowed to raise prices. A co-pilot or a feature alone is none of them. Ask in discovery if it is budgeted. Pipeline coverage moved from 2x to 3x because deals are harder (S053). Ask each year whether you gained or lost market share.
- **When to use:** Positioning an AI feature or forecasting a pipeline.
- **How to score:** 5 if you replace a named budget or headcount. 1 if you add a feature and hope. Test: could the AI product charge five times today's price?
- **Quote:** "You got to steal this budget from some of these people." (S075)
- **Sources:** 6 (S053, S056, S068, S075, S091, S095)

## 23. AI-native versus traditional GTM cost math, and the 2026 hiring plan
- **What it is:** A traditional team of 10 BDRs and 5 AEs costs about $1.8M for $5M of new ARR (36%). An AI-native mix of 3 AI BDR agents, 1 human BDR, 2 AEs and 3 sales engineers costs about $1.17M (23%). These are his illustrative figures, not audited ones.
- **Parts:** Fewer AEs, especially at $50K to $250K ACV. Far fewer human BDRs. More technical customer-facing people. Start with one AI BDR. Reps must be product experts. Be honest with the team that AI changes jobs.
- **When to use:** Planning 2026 sales headcount.
- **How to score:** Guidance only: below $1K tickets agents can close on their own; at $100K human plus agent; automate inbound completely.
- **Older view:** The 2012 to 2024 posts build sales with more reps and a VP (entries 1 and 6). The 2025 to 2026 posts reverse that for AI-exposed teams (newer; inferred from S040, S047, S084).
- **Quote:** "Start with one AI BDR. See what happens." (S040)
- **Sources:** 6 (S040, S047, S065, S074, S084, S095)

## 24. Seats are dying: the three new B2B pricing models
- **What it is:** Per-seat pricing erodes when agents do the work. Pick consumption, outcome or resolution pricing; incumbents run a hybrid of platform fee plus one of the three.
- **Parts:** Consumption risks bill shock; give a hard cap. Outcome is hard to attribute. Resolution is for one countable thing. Do not convert the installed base; add a second line sold into one segment. Credit reps at full size during the test. Write the billable unit's definition first. He noted that SaaStr cut Salesforce seats 60 to 70% yet its bill rose 80% (S056), so the story is not simple.
- **When to use:** An incumbent on seats facing AI agents.
- **How to score:** 5 if the unit tracks value and gross margin per unit is measured. 1 if seats are the only meter and the base is being squeezed.
- **Quote:** "Do not convert the base, in general." (S044)
- **Sources:** 3 (S041, S044, S056)

## 25. Agent-era retention: stealth churn, 60% solutions, agents steal customers
- **What it is:** Moats are shallower. A customer can pay and not use you, and a customer's own agent can propose replacing you.
- **Parts:** Stealth churn is still paying but not using for 30 days or more; watch DAU, WAU, MAU. A 60% solution gets tried but not paid for. Expose everything your product can do through an agent-friendly API. Surprise and delight once a quarter. At scale, two full-time jobs: keep the installed base happy (NRR above 110%, churn below 5%) and win the AI agent war in your category.
- **When to use:** Reviewing churn, a roadmap, or a bundle of AI features.
- **How to score:** 5 if usage is tracked and the product wins agent recommendations. 1 if retention is judged by billing alone.
- **Quote:** "If you build a 60% solution, no one's going to pay for it." (S057)
- **Sources:** 5 (S042, S056, S057, S062, S084)
