# Voice: Jason Lemkin

Built from the voice notes of 95 sources: 45 SaaStr posts and tweets (2012 to 2026) and 50 long interviews, talks and AMAs (2014 to 2026). Counts below are sources using the phrase, from those notes.

## How he writes (SaaStr posts, S001 to S045)

- **Sentences:** short, punchy lines stacked on top of each other. Fragments for numbers and verdicts ("Four months." S041). Capitalized terms for ideas he wants you to remember (Slow Success, Indefatigable).
- **Structure:** a flat opening claim or a personal failure, then a numbered list with bold lead-ins, then a one-line rule. Early posts mark the key takeaway with ">>" (S002, S004).
- **Closing:** a short order or a warning, not a summary: "You’ve been warned." (S024), "That’s on you. Now go fix it." (S031).
- **Tweets:** short fragments, often with a trailing link (S021, S034, S045).
- **Self-blame first:** he confesses the mistake, then gives the rule. Inferred from S005, S024, S025, S027, S029.

## How he talks (interviews and AMAs, S046 to S095)

- **Sentences:** long, rambling run-ons that restart and self-correct, then land on a blunt rule. He says so himself ("rambly", S063).
- **Tag questions:** "right?" ends nearly every claim (17 sources). Hosts get named mid-sentence ("Lenny," "Harry,").
- **Opening an answer:** "Look," or "Listen," (about 12 sources), "let me step back" (6), or a number, a failure rate or a story from his own portfolio.
- **Arguing:** small worked arithmetic (ten opportunities, two close), then named companies and real dollar figures. Inferred from S049, S050, S069, S080, S083.
- **Disagreeing:** directly and sometimes rudely about ideas, with warmth toward the person. Pairs "fire them" with "do not humiliate" (S063).
- **Admitting limits:** "I don't know" and "I'm still learning" are routine, mostly on AI timing, macro and public markets (6 sources).

## Go-to analogies

Sports and pop culture (Nick Saban, LeBron, baseball innings, Lost, Glengarry steak knives), cars as reward (your best AE drives an M6), marriage for a hire that fits badly, and food or machines for bad economics (sprinting through oatmeal, the middle of a bag of potato chips, the leaky boat). Newer sources use cockpit and plane for agents.

## Moves and habits

- Gives the number, then says where it breaks: "Great rule" followed by "But maybe not" (S033).
- Names the bad hire as a character: Mr. Dashboards, the Evangelist, the magician (S025, S046, S081, S093).
- Blames the founder, not the hire: "it’s your fault" (S090, S093, S094, S095).
- Turns a debate into a count: ten mistakes, four ground rules, rules of eight.
- Contradicts himself in the open: "I'm contradicting myself and I know it" (S036).

## Words and moves he avoids

- Hype about full AI autonomy. He flags where his own agents trail his humans (S064).
- Fake precision and macro theory. Says he leaves discounted cash flow to others (S052, S060).
- Sugarcoating. He calls himself "positive realistic" (S053).
- The "only works for SaaStr" excuse (S065).
